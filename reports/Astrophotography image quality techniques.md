# Fixing existing defects beats adding new models

Across calibration, denoising, deconvolution, background, stars and colour, the research notes surveyed more than seventy candidate techniques for OriginStack. The ones that survive the benefit-versus-effort test are mostly **repairs to assumptions the pipeline already makes**, not new algorithms: a wavelet denoiser that applies one white-noise sigma to correlated stacks (it removes only **2-10%** of the noise on real held-out Origin stacks, against 6-22% with a per-band sigma fix of about ten lines), a library flat blurred as if it were one noisy frame (**36 of 36** A/B ratios came out below 1, with 1.8-3.7% less sky structure at 16 px), a dark scaled by exposure ratio alone (8-10% less 32-64 px structure on a cold session when its glow scale was fitted), and a background step that masks every star as a 4 px blob regardless of brightness. Origin178 data are sky-shot-noise limited (199-385 e- of sky per pixel per sub; at most 7-13% of variance is anything else), so calibration, demosaic and sub-pixel refinements are mostly worth under 3% and several are worth under 1%. The weakest evidence sits where the visible payoff may be largest: bright-star halos, reflection rings, and frame-filling nebulosity, where support is survey literature plus code reading, with no Origin measurement yet. The notes therefore converge on a measure-first order: build the instruments (injection-recovery, split-half Fourier ring correlation, an "optics census" of PSF variation) in the first days, ship the four measured fixes next, then use the instruments to gate the expensive items (tile-aware coaddition, halo and ghost subtraction, dust templates, learned colour passbands). Ideas to leave alone include single-image self-supervised denoisers, further sub-pixel or Bayer super-resolution, lucky imaging, BM3D-family filters and exact Anscombe inverses. In this report, "measured on Origin" means the researchers ran A/B stacks or measurements on the user's real Origin178 sessions, "synthetic" means a constructed scene, and "literature only" means no OriginStack data exist yet; the notes flag these as single-unit, few-session results, not a benchmark suite.

## Sky-limited sensors cap calibration gains, but two shared-master flaws measure real

Origin178 lights carry **199-385 e- of sky per pixel per 20 s sub** at the shipped 0.0165 e-/ADU gain, and the measured per-frame high-pass sigma sits only 3-6% above the pure shot-noise expectation, so non-shot variance is at most 7-13% of the total (an upper bound, because the library bias is pre-averaged and read noise is still unmeasured) ([own measurement](file:///C:/Users/hansd/AppData/Local/Temp/claude/c--source-originstack/7fb708f8-e00f-49e8-9d6e-e26e83d3a85f/scratchpad/sensor_locked_pattern.py)). The library flat spans only 0.956-1.013 of its plane median, so noise differs by 1.6% from corner to centre ([own measurement](file:///C:/Users/hansd/AppData/Local/Temp/claude/c--source-originstack/7fb708f8-e00f-49e8-9d6e-e26e83d3a85f/scratchpad/vig_range.py)), and ADC steps of 6-7 ADU against 900-1240 ADU of noise contribute about 4e-6 of the variance ([calibration note](file:///C:/source/originstack-research/research_notes/Astrophotography%20image%20quality%20techniques/calibration_preprocessing.md)). That regime explains why most published low-light machinery does not transfer. The physics-based raw noise model of Wei et al. gains up to +2.9 dB, but only at x100-x300 darkening ([arXiv:2003.12751](https://arxiv.org/abs/2003.12751)); random-telegraph-noise correction raised SNR by more than 5% only for sources below 3 e-/pixel/frame ([arXiv:2605.19022](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.19022)); and the exact unbiased inverse of the generalised Anscombe transform matters at low counts ([Mäkitalo and Foi, IEEE TIP 2013](https://ieeexplore.ieee.org/document/6212354)) that a stack of dozens of 200 e- subs never reaches. Per-pixel variance planes carried through Phase 3, RTN detection, ADC-aware processing and warm-pixel tiers (83% of the 214,576 flagged hot pixels carry under one frame-sigma of excess in a 20 s light; only 0.25% of the sensor exceeds two sigma) all fall below 1% and are dropped.

What does measure is how one shared library file is treated. The flat, dark and bias of four sessions (Sunflower, two Whirlpool, Crab) have identical MD5 prefixes, and `_build_masters` blurs each master with sigma = max(1, K // int(sqrt(n))) for K of 30, 20 and 15, so a single pre-averaged file (n = 1) is blurred like one raw frame ([calibration note](file:///C:/source/originstack-research/research_notes/Astrophotography%20image%20quality%20techniques/calibration_preprocessing.md)). The lights say that discards real structure: the sensor-locked pattern of 40 Sunflower lights correlates with the flat's pixel pattern at r = 0.22-0.37 (about 450 sigma) with a regression coefficient of 0.63-0.70, a 34-87 ADU pattern ([own measurement](file:///C:/Users/hansd/AppData/Local/Temp/claude/c--source-originstack/7fb708f8-e00f-49e8-9d6e-e26e83d3a85f/scratchpad/sensor_locked_pattern.py)). In a full-pipeline A/B on the 158-frame Sunflower stack, replacing the flat's per-plane sigma 15 with 1.5 or 4 lowered the 64 px-high-passed sky noise in **all 36** ratios (two sigmas, three channels, six block scales); sigma 4 gave 1.8-3.7% less structure at 16 px and 0.6-4.9% at 32 px. A 13-frame Whirlpool session gave the same sign but only 0.97-1.00, and the 1 px column sits inside a measured +-2% perturbation noise ([run_flat_ab.py](file:///C:/Users/hansd/AppData/Local/Temp/claude/c--source-originstack/7fb708f8-e00f-49e8-9d6e-e26e83d3a85f/scratchpad/run_flat_ab.py), [ab_metrics.py](file:///C:/Users/hansd/AppData/Local/Temp/claude/c--source-originstack/7fb708f8-e00f-49e8-9d6e-e26e83d3a85f/scratchpad/ab_metrics.py)). The fix is a one-line heuristic in `cli._build_masters` (sigma 3-4 when the flat is a pre-averaged master), with an optional light-derived Wiener gain cached per master hash; it adds a Phase 1 attribute, so it must join `_P1_EXTRA`. Confidence is medium: one session, one unit, and users with genuinely noisy single-frame flats need the guard the note proposes.

The dark has the larger single effect on faint structure but the thinner evidence. Glow does not scale linearly with exposure and depends steeply on sensor temperature, and the library dark was taken at 31.3 C. Fitting a per-frame residual glow scale on 64 px block medians gave a fractional correction of **-0.67** on the 82-frame Markarian session (sensor 19-21 C), -0.13 on a 13-frame Whirlpool, and +0.09 to +0.15 on three warm sessions, so exposure-ratio scaling over-subtracts glow in cold sessions by 140-160 ADU peak-to-peak. Applying the Markarian correction cut structure at 32 and 64 px by 8-10% in all three channels (R scatter of 32 px block medians fell from 24.4 to 21.8 ADU) but made the 1-2 px scale 1-2% worse ([run_dark_ab.py](file:///C:/Users/hansd/AppData/Local/Temp/claude/c--source-originstack/7fb708f8-e00f-49e8-9d6e-e26e83d3a85f/scratchpad/run_dark_ab.py), [ab_metrics2.py](file:///C:/Users/hansd/AppData/Local/Temp/claude/c--source-originstack/7fb708f8-e00f-49e8-9d6e-e26e83d3a85f/scratchpad/ab_metrics2.py)). Siril's dark optimisation is the nearest published practice, a golden-section search for one scalar k in [0, 2] on a central 512x512 window ([Siril source](https://gitlab.com/free-astro/siril/-/raw/master/src/core/preprocess.c)), but it cannot see edge glow and ignores the sky gradient, which is why the recommended recipe fits a second-order polynomial nuisance term, shrinks the estimate toward zero and accepts only |delta| < 0.8 with standard error under 0.1. Four of five probed sessions needed under 0.15, so the benefit concentrates in cold sessions; whether Phase 4's DBE already absorbs the residual was not measured, and the physical cause of the temperature dependence is unestablished. Confidence is medium-low; the plug-in is a `_measure_session_dark_scale` beside `_measure_session_cfa`, about 100 numpy lines.

Two further items are cheap diagnostics, not noise reducers. DES defines an effective exposure time t_eff = (FWHM_fid/FWHM)^2 (B_fid/B) F_trans and uses it as an acceptance cut with minimums of 0.2-0.3 by band, not as a coadd weight ([Morganson et al. 2018](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1801.03177)); a per-frame t_eff built from proper coadd's own noise-equivalent area, sky noise and transparency would give the quality gate a principled post-registration threshold, an "effective integration time" header, and a better reference-session choice in `merge.py` than `INTGTIME`, for about a day of work and no change to the stack itself. In demosaicing, darktable's manual says RCD and AMaZE can overshoot in low-contrast noisy areas where LMMSE is more stable ([darktable 4.6](https://docs.darktable.org/usermanual/4.6/en/module-reference/processing-modules/demosaic/)), which motivates a dual RCD plus LMMSE blend; the notes give it a guessed 0-3% gain for 4-6 days of Rust work and low confidence, because linear interpolation does not change low-frequency noise power, so it ranks below everything above. Learned demosaic-denoise networks, including self-supervised mosaic-to-mosaic fine-tuning that gained more than 1 dB on single bursts ([Ehret et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/1905.05092)), duplicate what stacking already does and are not recommended.

## A single noise sigma leaves the wavelet denoiser removing only 2-10 percent of noise

The most concrete defect the researchers found is in `directional_wavelet_denoise`: it takes one sigma from the finest diagonal band and applies it to every band, which is valid only for white noise. Origin stacks are not white; the project itself measured lag-1 noise correlation of 0.41 and wrote that uncorrelated-noise methods will not transfer ([nn-deconv-runbook](file:///C:/source/originstack-research/dev-notes/nn-deconv-runbook.md)), and drizzle-style resampling is known to correlate adjacent pixels and make per-pixel noise underestimate noise on larger scales ([Fruchter and Hook](https://ar5iv.arxiv.org/html/astro-ph/9808087)). On three real held-out stacks, band MAD-sigma ran 1.66-1.98 times the denoiser's sigma in the finest H/V bands and 2.2-3.5 times at the third level. Using the NN output as a near-noise-free reference on sky pixels, the share of pixel noise left after the stock denoiser versus a per-band version was 90.7% versus 78.0% (Sunflower centre), 93.5% versus 83.1% (Omega) and 97.9% versus 94.5% (Orion), so the stock path removes 2-10% of the noise and the fix removes about 2.3-2.6 times as much ([own measurement, denoising note](file:///C:/source/originstack-research/research_notes/Astrophotography%20image%20quality%20techniques/denoising_signal_recovery.md)). A synthetic faint-nebula scene with weaker (lag-1 0.32) correlation reproduced the pattern: sky rms 0.578 input, 0.574 stock (-0.7%), 0.502 per-band (-13%), identical to an oracle with true per-band noise, and with white noise stock and per-band agreed within 1%, so nothing regresses where the old assumption held. The statistics are classical: level-dependent thresholds for stationary correlated noise ([Johnstone and Silverman 1997](https://ideas.repec.org/a/bla/jorssb/v59y1997i2p319-351.html)) and noise estimated from the multiresolution support ([Starck and Murtagh 1998](https://doi.org/10.1086/316124)), and Siril's own documentation admits its denoisers assume white noise and mishandle OSC correlated noise ([Siril docs](https://siril.readthedocs.io/en/latest/processing/denoising.html)). The change is about ten lines in `_denoise_plane` plus a test that fails on the stock code, and it should also reach `reduce_chroma_noise` and `_estimate_sky_sigma`, which the project already noticed reads far too low after the wavelet denoiser.

Two caveats limit how far to trust the size. The function was tested in isolation on linear stacks, not through the full Phase 4 chain, and the reference is itself a denoiser, so it ranks wavelet variants only. And the default Origin path is the Noise2Noise U-Net, whose residual error the code documents as about 20 times below the wavelet denoiser's, so the benefit lands chiefly on `--denoiser wavelet`, NN-guard fallbacks and other cameras. Two cheap companions share the function. Cycle spinning, averaging the denoiser over four to eight grid shifts ([Coifman and Donoho 1995](https://web.stanford.edu/dept/statistics/cgi-bin/donoho/wp-content/uploads/2018/08/TIDeNoise.pdf)), cut synthetic sky rms by 7% for white noise and 4% for correlated noise at K times the step cost (about 9 s instead of 2.2 s on Sunflower at K = 4). Per-pixel noise-map normalisation is the second: `--full-field` is on by default, an edge pixel covered by half the frames is about 1.4 times noisier, and `<output>_coverage.fits` is never read by Phase 4, so both denoisers assume one global sigma. Conditioning on a noise-level map is the FFDNet idea ([Zhang et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/1710.04026)); on a synthetic scene with the right half 1.41 times noisier, residual error in empty sky fell from 2.22 to 1.49 ADU in the full-depth half (-33%; -25% in a second run) and from 2.43 to 2.08 ADU in the noisy half, against a 1.5 ADU uniform-noise control ([own measurement](file:///C:/source/originstack-research/research_notes/Astrophotography%20image%20quality%20techniques/denoising_signal_recovery.md)). It needs the map plumbed from `full_field`/`merge` into `args._noise_rel`, stored for `--from-stack`, and added to the early-cache key; confidence is medium because no real full-field stack has been tried.

For faint extended light, the astronomy-native tool is significance-adaptive smoothing: ASMOOTH gives every pixel the smallest Gaussian scale whose local signal-to-noise clears a threshold ([Ebeling, White and Rangarajan 2006](https://arxiv.org/abs/astro-ph/0601306)). On a synthetic scene of six faint blobs, a ridge and 25 stars it cut nebula rms from 0.569 (stock) and 0.487 (per-band wavelet) to 0.150, with star cores intact and flux slope near 1.0, but the truth is smooth at 18 px and above, which maximally rewards smoothing, the 4 px ridge lost some amplitude at the stricter threshold (flux slope 0.97), and the NN still left less noise (1.5 of 23 ADU) on a comparable scene. It is a prototype for diffuse targets after the wavelet fixes, as a new resumable Phase 4 step with the `_at`, `SNAP_STEPS`, `PHASE4_TOGGLES` and label plumbing the repo requires, not a competitor to the NN. The researchers also measured and recorded negatives so they are not retried blind: a dihedral eight-fold self-ensemble of the NN lowered sky error only 4% (1.58 to 1.51 ADU) for 8x compute, and SOS boosting around the wavelet shrinkage gave no gain at the true noise level. Single-image self-supervised denoisers (Noise2Void, Noise2Self, Zero-Shot Noise2Noise, Neighbor2Neighbor) assume pixel-wise independent noise ([Noise2Self](https://arxiv.org/abs/1901.11365), [ZS-N2N](https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.11253)) and fail at lag-1 0.41, and the pixel-shuffle workaround ([AP-BSN](https://arxiv.org/abs/2203.11799)) needs a stride of about 3 px, costing a factor 9 in resolution. The one speculative exception is Recorrupted-to-Recorrupted ([Pang et al. 2021](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/CVPR2021/html/Pang_Recorrupted-to-Recorrupted_Unsupervised_Deep_Learning_for_Image_Denoising_CVPR_2021_paper.html)) as an offline way to make camera-specific N2N training pairs from one deep stack with a measured coloured-noise spectrum, for non-Origin cameras where the shipped weights transfer poorly; it needs training and is low confidence.

## Deconvolution sits at its data limit, so the classical path and measurement matter

Deconvolution has the least headroom of the six areas because the shipped NN deconvolver already sits on a plateau: a stricter seeing cut, cross-night pairs and a 2-4x wider U-Net all land on the v3 width-noise curve at equal noise ([CLAUDE.md](file:///C:/source/originstack-research/CLAUDE.md), [nn-deconv-runbook](file:///C:/source/originstack-research/dev-notes/nn-deconv-runbook.md)). The weak link is the classical `--deconvolve rl` path, which inverts the full measured PSF and must mask every star and its 5 x FWHM surroundings, so it cannot sharpen stars or anything near them. The cleanest published remedy is deconvolution with correct sampling: choose a narrower, well-sampled target PSF r, deconvolve with the relative kernel s defined by t = r * s, and ringing disappears by construction; on simulated data it held photometric error to 1% against 7% for a two-channel Lucy method, with no ringing around point sources ([Magain, Courbin and Sohy 1998](https://ar5iv.arxiv.org/html/astro-ph/9704059)). For a 4.4 px stack and a 3.5 px target the relative kernel is 2.7 px wide, a ratio of 0.8, mild enough to drop the star mask except at clipped cores. The researchers' noise-limit arithmetic (Gaussian model, FWHM 4.4 px) is the honest constraint: narrowing the FWHM by 20% needs gains of 1.75, 4.7 and 21 at 0.15, 0.25 and 0.35 cycles/px, so usable narrowing is set by signal-to-noise at the star-scale frequencies and extended structure cannot follow stars. The expected role is a ringing-free classical sharpener, the fallback when `nn_stack_guard` reverts the network on non-Origin data, and a calibrated output PSF; the note rates 0.6 confidence that it works as designed and 0.3 that users see a gain over the NN. A smaller classical-path fix is the PSF estimate itself: `estimate_psf_blind` centres cutouts on the integer-rounded centroid, which widens the PSF by about 1.2% at FWHM 4.4 px and 3.0% at 2.78 px (the researchers' arithmetic) and leaves RL under-corrected; sub-pixel recentring as in photutils' ePSF builder, which recentres each star with a symmetry-based centroid, iterates until most centres stop moving, and finds oversampling of 1 sufficient at FWHM of 4 px or more ([photutils](https://docs.astropy.org/projects/photutils/en/stable/api/photutils.psf.EPSFBuilder.html)), is about 100 numpy lines.

Resolution beyond that depends on whether the default proper coadd discards spatial information. The patch quality grids feed only the patch-weighted stack, which proper coadd then replaces (`stacking.py` around lines 2190-2217), and proper coadd takes one elliptical Moffat, one transparency and one noise value per frame; earlier, switching patch weighting off had made Sunflower noisier (R 1.09 to 1.15) ([siril-comparison](file:///C:/source/originstack-research/dev-notes/siril-comparison.md)). Zackay and Ofek show that matched-filtering each frame with its own PSF is the optimal linear combine and that homogenising PSFs before coaddition loses sensitivity, with a gain of a few to 25 percent in survey speed ([arXiv:1512.06872](https://arxiv.org/abs/1512.06872)), so the proposal is not to homogenise but to extend "frame" to "frame by tile": accumulate K = 4-12 numerator and denominator sets with tile-specific weights and blend with a partition of unity, at roughly +30-60 s on a 500-frame session for K = 4. The notes insist on gating this by measurement: if the frame-by-quadrant residual FWHM scatter, after removing per-frame and per-quadrant means, is under 3%, there is no lucky-patch signal and the idea is dropped; a PSFEx-style position-dependent pixel-basis PSF field ([PSFEx](https://psfex.readthedocs.io/en/latest/Working.html)) is likewise only worth building if the max/min FWHM across a 6x6 cell map exceeds 1.05 or ellipticity exceeds 0.05. The researchers put 0.3-0.4 on the gate passing. One measured hint that the optics are chromatic: in the 158-frame Sunflower stack, the B channel's star FWHM is 3.39 px against 3.56 px in R and G (B/G 0.963 per star) ([own measurement](file:///C:/Users/hansd/AppData/Local/Temp/claude/c--source-originstack/7fb708f8-e00f-49e8-9d6e-e26e83d3a85f/scratchpad/chan_fwhm2.py)), a few percent of width that a per-channel Moffat in proper coadd would capture for under 1% weight error.

Sub-pixel and Bayer super-resolution is closed. Reconstruction constraints carry less information as magnification grows ([Baker and Kanade](https://ri.cmu.edu/publications/limits-on-super-resolution-and-how-to-break-them)), and the researchers' aliasing budget for a Gaussian PSF shows the stack MTF at the R/B plane Nyquist is 0.076 at FWHM 3.4 px, 0.013 at 4.4 px and 0.004 at 5.0 px, rising to about 0.18 only for the sharpest benchmark stack (2.78 px). The project's measurements agree: Bayer drizzle on the Fireworks Galaxy cut luma noise 13% but raised chroma noise 110% and softened stars 5%, the kernel-regression merge left stars 8-45% wider and 1.3-2.9 times noisier at matched width, and 2x Lanczos drizzle was no sharper at equal noise except 5% on M66 for four times the pixels ([module-notes](file:///C:/source/originstack-research/dev-notes/module-notes.md), [siril-comparison](file:///C:/source/originstack-research/dev-notes/siril-comparison.md)). The one residual action is a reporting line: print the R/B aliasing budget in `dither_report.py` and suggest CFA drizzle only below about 2.5 px FWHM. Seeing-aware ideas do not transfer either. Lucky imaging relies on freezing turbulence in millisecond exposures ([arXiv:0906.3041](https://arxiv.org/pdf/0906.3041)), whereas Origin subs run 10-30 s, so atmospheric structure is averaged inside each frame; video turbulence-mitigation networks target strong horizontal-path turbulence; and under Gaussian noise a joint multi-frame deconvolution differs from "proper coadd, then deconvolve once" only through priors the Phase 4 deconvolver already supplies (the researchers' inference). Alt-az field-rotation smear grows linearly with radius (1.5 px at r = 1000 px for 0.5 deg/min and 10 s subs), but at the project's measured 0.4 deg per session it falls below 0.3 px, so it is a diagnostic for meridian-crossing, long-sub sessions only.

## Bright-star wings, not gradient interpolators, are the background model's blind spot

The background literature converges on one diagnosis: sky models fail by absorbing real extended light, usually unmasked star wings, cirrus or galaxy outskirts, and the fix is better masks and external priors, not a better interpolator. HSC's first release fitted a sixth-order Chebyshev surface that "tends to subtract extended halos of bright objects", and its successor moved to 1024 px superpixels plus a static sky frame ([Aihara et al. 2019](https://arxiv.org/abs/1905.12221)); SDSS-style polynomial sky fits interpret diffuse emission of similar scale as sky and over-subtract ([Roman, Trujillo and Montes 2020](https://arxiv.org/abs/1907.00978)); and in HSC-like simulations, masks that grow with source brightness gave sky estimates fainter by up to an order of magnitude than conventional masking, provided the model matches the wings ([Kelvin et al. 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2301.05793)), a figure from crowded simulations that should not be transplanted to Origin. OriginStack's own sweeps say the interpolator is not the lever: DBE removes about 70% of cross-night gradient mismatch (median 1.44 to 0.34 sky-sigma) but stays at 0.6-2.3 on frame-filling nebulae, DBE parameter sweeps move results by under 5%, and a learned U-Net background erased faint nebula because its "clean" training images were DBE outputs ([phase4-quality](file:///C:/source/originstack-research/dev-notes/phase4-quality.md)). Meanwhile the code feeds DBE a star mask that is a Gaussian blob of fixed 4 px FWHM at every centroid (`quality.generate_star_mask`, called with `fwhm=4.0` at `postprocess.py` line 331), and `_build_emission_mask` protects at most the three brightest peaks with a fixed disc. That gap is the top background item.

The recommended first step is a brightness-scaled mask radius r_m = (A F / (k sigma_sky))^(1/alpha), with alpha near 2.5 (SDSS star wings fall as r^-2.49 over 20 magnitudes ([Infante-Sainz, Trujillo and Roman 2020](https://arxiv.org/abs/1911.01430))), k of 0.25-0.5, a floor at the current 4 px and a cap of 150-300 px, with A and alpha fitted per stack from 10-30 isolated unsaturated stars. A second step subtracts the summed wing model of all stars, including Gaia and Tycho stars up to 1-2 degrees outside the frame, from the image used only to sample patches, so appearance is unchanged. It plugs into `quality.generate_star_mask`, `_build_emission_mask` and `gaia_cache`; the cost is days and the confidence medium, with the caveats that the evidence is survey data, no Origin wing profile has been measured, a wrong slope over-masks (cap the masked fraction; the dense-field fallback exists), and `--offline` must fall back to in-frame stars. For the frame-filling nebulae where DBE is at its limit, Dragonfly work fits the patch background as a second-order sky polynomial plus a free multiple of a third-order Planck dust template ([Liu et al. 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.07922)), motivated by the optical-to-far-infrared correspondence of cirrus ([Roman et al. 2020](https://arxiv.org/abs/1907.00978); [Mihos et al. 2017](https://arxiv.org/abs/1611.04435)). The adaptation subtracts only the dust-free fitted surface so the dust-explained light stays in the image, and applies only when the template has non-planar structure and the fitted multiple has t > 3. The researchers' own caveats are severe: Liu et al. validated visually, not numerically; Planck's roughly 5 arcmin beam gives an Origin frame (1.27 x 0.85 degrees) few independent elements; a planar dust gradient is degenerate with light pollution; emission nebulae correlate with dust only loosely; the HiPS identifiers for the fetch were not verified; and the fetch is a network call that must join `uncertainty._QUIET_OFF`, the README network table, the offline guard and the early-cache key. It ranks medium-low and should wait for the injection harness below.

Several smaller background items fill the second tier. A NoiseChisel-style detect-and-grow mask with a skewness-based tile gate ([Akhlaghi and Ichikawa 2015](https://arxiv.org/abs/1505.01664), [Akhlaghi 2019](https://arxiv.org/abs/1909.11230)) would replace the fixed-disc and border-referenced rules that fail where the border is itself nebula (Horsehead), though it does nothing for a smooth frame-filling feature, which has no within-tile skew. Liu et al.'s observation that bad areas "are not significantly deviant" pixel by pixel, "only the mean in an area is", supports a block-level consistency rejection after `local_normalize`: compare 32 px and 128 px block means across frames, and zero-weight blocks beyond about 4 robust sigma, which would stop additive normalisation from silently repairing cloud or dew frames; a per-frame wide-angle-PSF "halo index" from threshold-radii of bright stars would likewise catch haze that FWHM cannot see. Both are small-to-medium, unmeasured on Origin, and exempt comet mode. PixInsight's LocalNormalization stores both a scale and a zero offset on a 64-256 px grid ([PCL header](https://gitea.nouspiro.space/nou/tenmon/raw/commit/12c6385f776ba446a4f5f40efb90ecc4da723046/3rdparty/include/pcl/LocalNormalizationData.h)), but OriginStack's additive-only design exists because the earlier multiplicative version divided by local sigma and amplified noise, so only a one-parameter global gain per frame is worth considering. Rotation-based separation of sensor-fixed from sky-fixed structure is ruled out by identifiability: a feature of size L separates only if r dtheta exceeds L, which at 0.4 degrees is about 7 px at r = 1000 px, the same limit that sank `--flat-from-lights` and the ubercal flat.

## Halos, reflections and soft corners are unmodelled optics, and one census decides which to chase

The star-handling notes agree with the background notes on the mechanism and add a published procedure. Professional pipelines do not homogenise PSFs before coaddition: the HSC coadd is a weighted mean of Lanczos-3 resampled images that states it does "not match the PSFs of the input images" ([Bosch et al. 2018](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1705.06766)), and DESI Legacy Surveys fit every source on every exposure with that exposure's own PSF ([Dey et al. 2019](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1804.08657)), which confirms OriginStack's proper coadd. Where a uniform PSF is wanted (KiDS, Euclid), it is imposed after coaddition with spatially varying Wiener-type kernels ([Kuijken et al. 2015](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1507.00738); [Boucaud et al. 2016](https://arxiv.org/abs/1609.02006)), at the price of correlated noise. A star-footprint-only version avoids that price: kernels from a 5x4 grid of per-channel PSFs toward a circular Moffat 5% broader than the 80th-percentile FWHM, noise gain capped at 1.5, flux conserved by sum K = 1, applied only to background-subtracted star layers. It would round corner stars, equalise R, G and B widths and give the NN deconvolver and `shrink_stars` a uniform input, for about a week, as a new Phase 4 step; the evidence is survey-scale only and interaction with the deconvolver's global `measure_star_fwhm` must be tested in both orders. Because the field rotates, the stack PSF is already a rotation average of the sensor-frame PSF, so coma and astigmatism are partly averaged round and radial FWHM growth is what survives, which is why the notes put per-frame spatial PSFs in proper coadd below a post-coadd step.

The strongest single item is Gaia-anchored extended-PSF halo subtraction, replacing the Gaussian `--halo-removal`, which fits one Gaussian to the brightest 5% of stars. Infante-Sainz et al. build an empirical PSF in three regimes (core from faint unsaturated stars, intermediate and outer from bright ones, spliced by scale and offset at matched signal-to-noise), reaching 8 arcmin and 20 magnitudes of dynamic range, then scale it to Gaia G < 13 stars brightest first using the sigma-clipped median of star-to-PSF ratios over a defined radial range, in two passes with masks rebuilt between them; Gaia is incomplete below about G = 7, so the brightest stars need Tycho or USNO ([Infante-Sainz et al. 2020](https://arxiv.org/abs/1911.01430)). For Origin the template must be per channel, since the PSF is chromatic (bluer core, redder halo per the star-n2n runbook), with amplitude refit per session because dew and dirt change halos. The same fit yields a physically derived per-star mask radius for DBE, denoising protection and `remove_stars`, tying this item to the background mask above and to HSC's magnitude-dependent bright-star masks. It should be built once as a shared module on an ePSF helper. Confidence is medium-high that it removes halos and medium that the gain is visible on Origin data, because the source paper reports no numeric residual and notes remaining reflection bumps and spikes; the acceptance test is the project's own halo-ring metric on Orion, Pleiades and Sunflower.

Spikes and reflection rings are the open items in the roadmap, and here the literature is thinnest: no peer-reviewed method was found that subtracts, rather than detects or masks, diffraction spikes. Schmidt-type systems show the physics: in the Burrell Schmidt, reflections between detector and window are out-of-focus pupil images whose offset is linear in position on the detector, hold under 0.5% of a star's light and reach 17 arcmin, and were modelled generatively from catalogue stars ([Slater, Harding and Mihos 2009](https://arxiv.org/abs/0909.3320); [Mihos et al. 2017](https://arxiv.org/abs/1611.04435)). Survey pipelines predict ghosts from optical models and mask them per exposure, so coadds of masked exposures lose them, and DES gives flagged pixels zero weight ([LSSTCam ghosts](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2606.31826); [Morganson et al. 2018](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1801.03177)). The researchers' key inference is that field rotation, normally a nuisance, is what makes masking work, because the features are fixed to the sensor while the sky rotates, and the amateur "rotate the camera" test can be automated. A one-day Stage 0 diagnostic cuts 201x201 stamps of the 10-30 brightest stars from every Phase 1 frame, stacks them in sensor coordinates and in sky coordinates, and compares spike and ring contrast; it also fits the ghost offset against sensor position. If rings are circles centred on the star regardless of position, the position model is skipped; if features prove sky-fixed, the premise fails cheaply. Stage 1 then zero-weights the template region (doubled in area for position error) in each frame's combine; Stage 2, forward-model subtraction, is large and optional. The caveats are important: Origin's reflection geometry is undocumented, amateur threads attribute rings to sensor-window re-reflection only anecdotally, the RASA-style assumption is unverified, and with under 0.4 degrees of rotation per session the features stack coherently and Stage 1 leaves residuals.

A single "optics census" gates half of this and of the resolution section. Running `--aberration-report` on a 6x6 cell map for five or six sessions, computing the frame-by-quadrant FWHM residual scatter, per-channel star widths, tangential versus radial FWHM at the corners, and per-channel star-centroid offsets against radius answers five questions at once: whether a PSF field or tile-aware proper coadd has anything to correct (the 1.05 and 3% gates), whether post-coadd homogenisation is needed, whether field-rotation smear matters, and whether lateral chromatic aberration has a radial term. The last matters because `debayer.measure_chromatic_aberration` fits one global translation of R and B against G, while photographic practice models a per-channel radial polynomial, Rd = b Ru^3 + c Ru^2 + v Ru ([Lensfun TCA](https://lensfun.github.io/calibration-tutorial/lens-tca.html)); the notes say to apply a radial model only if the corner residual exceeds about 0.15 px. Lower-priority star items are a Gaia-anchored core refill for `star_repair.py` (flux-conserving, modest), radial chroma regularisation of visible stars (overlapping the roadmap and constrained by the repo's note that per-channel stretch whitens cores, so judge on `*_lin` metrics), and grouped simultaneous PSF fits ([photutils](https://docs.astropy.org/projects/photutils/en/stable/user_guide/psf.html)) for crowded-field colour.

## Bright cores and display quantisation offer the cheapest visible tone gains

OriginStack's colour fundamentals leave little cheap headroom. It already implements the Lupton-style ratio-preserving asinh stretch ([Lupton et al. 2004](https://arxiv.org/abs/astro-ph/0312483)), an OKLab stretch, Gaia solar-fit calibration with a slope prior, and extended-highlight roll-off, and the default `channel` stretch is the one the user prefers even though `preserve` scored better on hue (0.01-0.07 degrees error against 3-7.5, star-colour correlation 0.80-0.98 against 0.51-0.91) ([phase4-quality](file:///C:/source/originstack-research/dev-notes/phase4-quality.md)). What is missing is true dynamic-range compression of the bright base layer, which is what M31's bulge and the bright parts of M42, M8 and M17 need. The classical solution decomposes log luminance into an edge-preserving base and a detail layer, compresses only the base ([Durand and Dorsey 2002](https://people.csail.mit.edu/fredo/PUBLI/Siggraph2002/)), and uses the linear-time guided filter, which avoids the bilateral filter's gradient reversal ([He, Sun and Tang](https://people.csail.mit.edu/kaiming/publications/pami12guidedfilter.pdf)); PixInsight's HDR wavelet transform is the practitioner precedent, with its documented "donut" artefact around bright stars unless masked ([PixInsight HDRWT](https://pixinsight.com/examples/HDRWT/M101/index.html)). The proposed implementation runs in `io_fits.render_preview_float` before the white point is set, excludes stars from the filtered log image by masked-Gaussian fill, uses a knee at the 90th percentile of above-sky base and a compression of 8-20 over the base range, recomposes colour with the repo's additive fade near zero (never plain `rgb * Lc / L` on sky-centred data, the Orion 1e18 bug in CLAUDE.md), and gates on `_extended_highlight_top` so only frames with an extended region above the p99.5 white are touched. It is preview-only, so none of the resumable-step plumbing applies; the estimate is about four days and 0.3-0.6 s per 6.4 MP frame. Limits are stated: it cannot recover clipped plateaus (Orion's Trapezium clips about 15,000 px in every 20 s sub), "HDR look" appears below a compression factor of 0.5, and no quantitative astro benchmark exists; judge it on structure per unit sky noise in the above-white region, with the ring and faint-CNR metrics as guards.

Two output-side items are nearly free. Triangular-PDF dither in the 8-bit quantiser ([Lipshitz, Wannamaker and Vanderkooy 1992](https://hajim.rochester.edu/ece/sites/zduan/teaching/ece472/reading/Lipshitz_1992.pdf)) addresses contour banding in smooth NN-denoised regions: `to_uint8` currently rounds with no dither, and bench display sky noise is 0.74-2.34 8-bit units, so noisy sky self-dithers but denoised object interiors do not. With a fixed-seed generator in the file writers only, so reruns stay bit-identical and `bench_phase4` is untouched, it is a quarter-day change; the benefit is confined to smooth regions and not yet measured on real outputs. Ottosson's adaptive-L0 gamut compression, which keeps OKLab hue and gives up only chroma that cannot exist at the requested lightness ([Ottosson](https://bottosson.github.io/posts/gamutclipping/)), would fix the repo-documented cost that a red star core displays at about half the brightness of a white one, but it affects only the `oklab` and `preserve` modes, not the default `channel` mode. Khronos PBR-Neutral and a Mantiuk saturation exponent ([Khronos](https://github.com/KhronosGroup/ToneMapping/blob/main/PBR_Neutral/README.md); [Mantiuk et al.](https://www.cl.cam.ac.uk/~rkm38/pdfs/mantiuk09cctm.pdf)) are alternatives with marginal gain over it, and a BT.2100 PQ `cICP`-tagged PNG ([W3C](https://www.w3.org/TR/png-hdr-pq)) helps only HDR displays in Chromium. Because default Origin runs use the NN path, which skips classical chroma noise reduction, preview-side tone work reaches every Origin output while chroma-NR ideas such as luminance-guided guided-filter chroma smoothing mostly serve other cameras.

Colour calibration is the one area where an earlier survey identified a competitor gap: PixInsight and Siril integrate each star's Gaia XP spectrum against the camera's real response curves ([PixInsight SPCC](https://pixinsight.com/doc/docs/SPCC/SPCC.html); [Siril SPCC](https://siril.readthedocs.io/en/latest/processing/color-calibration/spcc.html)), while OriginStack's `spcc` uses a blackbody times assumed Gaussian responses. No Origin178 curve is published, so the proposal is to learn the effective R, G, B continuum responses once from the project's 28-33 calibrated sessions plus XP spectra, which reproduce synthetic photometry within a few percent for wide bands ([Montegriffo et al. 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2206.06215)), ship them in the camera profile, and fit only two white gains, which would work with 6-14 stars and reddened fields where the BP-RP locus breaks. The researchers' caveats: the construction is theirs (no source covers exactly this for amateur data), stellar spectra span only three or four directions so the learned curves say nothing about Ha or OIII leakage, flat and pedestal colour errors get absorbed into the passbands, and the gain on the existing white-point metric is expected to be modest; it is about eight days and a foundation for a star-trained 3x3 colour matrix (which may amplify chroma noise by an unmeasured 1.2-1.5x) and for non-circular colour scoring. Cheaper variants are a stellar-locus-regression fallback for offline or unsolved sessions replacing the gray locus ([High et al. 2009](https://www.arxiv.org/abs/0903.5302), 5-14 mmag RMS on survey data) and errors-in-variables weighting of the Theil-Sen fit for dim stars. Narrowband is conditional on facts nobody has: Siril extracts Ha from the red photosites only and OIII from green and blue before interpolation ([Siril docs](https://siril.readthedocs.io/en/latest/processing/extraction.html)), which would avoid RCD's cross-contamination for dual-band frames, but what the Origin "Nebula" filter passes is undocumented (one Lagoon session read R/G of 0.04 against 0.63 unfiltered, suggesting OIII dominance), so a real dual-band session must come first.

## Six measurement tools turn every other idea into a testable claim

The notes keep arriving at the same gap: gains are estimated on isolated modules, synthetic scenes or linear-stack statistics, and the project lacks ground truth for faint structure, frequency-resolved sharpness and star colour. The injection-recovery transfer function is the most leveraged: inject a Gaussian random field with a k^-2.9 spectrum, the measured optical cirrus slope ([Miville-Deschênes et al. 2016](https://arxiv.org/abs/1605.08360)), at 0.25-2 sky-sigma over 16-1000 px scales plus exponential discs and a known gradient into a `--from-stack` linear FITS (or, for Phase 1-3 effects, into each aligned frame through `transparency.to_aligned_yx`); then measure transmitted fraction T(k) in Fourier annuli against a control run, and blank-sky aperture bias and variance at radii of 4-128 px against the correlated-noise expectation. Depth should be quoted from measured box sigma, not extrapolated from pixel sigma, since the box-scaling formula assumes uncorrelated noise ([Roman et al. 2020](https://arxiv.org/abs/1907.00978)). This would have caught the learned background's nebula removal without two-night data and is the acceptance test for the wing-aware masks, dust template, halo subtraction and ASMOOTH; it needs no network and no training, though `<stem>_phase4cache.pkl` must be deleted between runs.

The second is a split-half toolkit. Fourier ring correlation between two independent half-stacks is the standard resolution measure in microscopy and also drives denoising, blind PSF estimation and the monitoring of iterative deconvolution ([Koho et al. 2019](https://research.utu.fi/converis/portal/detail/Publication/41357558?lang=en_US)). Proper coadd's estimator is a ratio of two sums over frames, so odd and even accumulator sets cost only two extra inverse FFTs; with equal-noise halves the researchers derive SNR_h = FRC/(1 - FRC) and use it for an effective-resolution number, a parameter-free Wiener baseline, a frequency cutoff for any deconvolver or the NN output, and a data-driven RL stopping iteration. The same independence principle ([Noise2Inverse](https://arxiv.org/abs/2001.11801)) gives a scale-resolved risk curve, E_k = mean[(band_k(D(A)) - band_k(B))^2] - sigma_B,k^2 compared with the risk of doing nothing, sigma_A,k^2, whose bump at 16-64 px exposes over-smoothing of faint extended light, plus "method noise" checks that the removed component is white and uncorrelated with the output ([Buades, Coll and Morel](https://hal.archives-ouvertes.fr/hal-00271141)). The caveats are the researchers' own: formulas are derived, not quoted (the Koho full text was unreachable); learned priors reproduce the same hallucination in both halves, so FRC overstates their resolution and must be paired with the Pan-STARRS and Gaia-pair checks; and the projected cross-validation score is blind to errors the PSF suppresses, so alone it biases toward too many iterations. The third tool is the optics census above; the fourth is a residual-consistency report, chi-square of (y - s * x)/sigma on sky and near stars, for every deconvolved output; the fifth is a colour set, CIEDE2000 chroma error of Gaia-matched stars against an expected colour that does not come from the calibration being scored (XP-synthesised, or a blackbody at a Mucciarelli temperature ([Mucciarelli et al. 2021](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2106.03882))), an OKLab sky chroma-noise score, and the structural-fidelity term only of TMQI ([Yeganeh and Wang](https://ece.uwaterloo.ca/~z70wang/research/tmqi/)), whose naturalness prior was fitted to daylight photographs ([Sharma et al. CIEDE2000](https://www.ece.rochester.edu/~gsharma/ciede2000/)); the sixth is the t_eff and aliasing-budget lines in the session and dither reports.

## Build in four waves, instruments first

**Wave 0, measurement (days, no behaviour change).** Build the injection-recovery harness, the odd/even proper-coadd accumulators with an FRC and risk-curve tool, and the optics census (6x6 aberration cells, frame-by-quadrant FWHM scatter, per-channel widths, corner CA, tangential versus radial FWHM, stack radial wing profiles to fit the wing slope and amplitude for later masks), plus the Stage 0 sensor-frame stamp diagnostic and the t_eff and aliasing-budget report lines. These are offline tools or report lines, outside the resumable Phase 4 chain, and they convert the gated rows of the ranking into yes or no answers: the 1.05 and 3% PSF-variation gates, whether rings and spikes are sensor-fixed, and the Origin wing exponent that the researchers could not find in any source.

**Wave 1, measured repairs (about a week).** Ship the per-band sigma and cycle spinning together, since they share one function, then the flat sigma change after confirming its sign on three more sessions, including a dithered 1.4.x one; both Phase 1 changes need `_P1_EXTRA` and updated bit-identity expectations. Add the dither in the 8-bit writers in the same pass. Re-run `tools/bench_denoise_quality.py` and the real-stack noise-remaining proxy after each, and run the injection harness on the flat and denoiser changes through the full Phase 4 chain, which no gain in the notes has yet passed through.

**Wave 2, gated builds (weeks).** Brightness-scaled star masks first (they feed DBE sampling and share a wing fit with the halo template), then the Gaia-anchored extended-PSF halo subtraction on a numpy ePSF helper, the per-pixel noise map (with the early-cache key and `--from-stack` storage), the dark glow scale (tested after DBE, which the A/B did not do), the relative-kernel deconvolver with its consistency report, and the guided-filter HDR compression with its ring, CNR and structure guards. Native Rust kernels follow only where profiling demands, with a numpy mirror and a parity test in `tests/test_native.py` and a `Cargo.toml` version bump, per the repo rules; the notes found none of these items needs Rust at stack sizes. New resumable Phase 4 steps carry `_at`, `SNAP_STEPS`, `PHASE4_TOGGLES`, a `_PP_STEP_LABELS` entry and translations; any step that writes a file or calls the network joins `uncertainty._QUIET_OFF`.

**Wave 3, conditional on wave 0.** Tile-aware proper coadd and the PSF field proceed only if the census gates pass; post-coadd star-footprint homogenisation and the radial CA model proceed only if corner PSF and CA residuals justify them; Stage 1 per-frame ghost masks only if Stage 0 shows sensor-fixed features, with Stage 2 forward modelling deferred; the dust template only after the injection harness shows DBE is losing structure on a frame-filling nebula it could keep; ASMOOTH as a prototype for diffuse targets; the XP-based colour calibration and CFA Ha/OIII extraction wait for, respectively, the colour-scoring metrics and a real dual-band Origin session.

## Conclusion

The research changes the question from "which new algorithm" to "which inherited assumption is wrong". The measured wins all trace to one: a single global value stood in for something that varies, whether one sigma for correlated bands, one blur for a pre-averaged master, one exposure ratio for temperature-dependent glow, one 4 px blob for stars of any brightness, or one global noise level across a full-field stack with uneven depth. The default coadd even computes patch quality and then discards it. None of the fixes needs training data or a dependency, and each sits behind a measurement the repo can make in days. The structural consequence is that, because the NN path is the Origin default, the changes that reach every default run lie upstream (calibration, background masks, star halos) and downstream (tone and export), exactly where the notes' evidence is thinnest or smallest, so the ranking trades measured-but-narrow wins against plausible-but-unmeasured ones, and only the wave 0 instruments can arbitrate between them.

The caveat that should shape expectations is that no gain here has yet been scored on a final processed Origin image. Calibration gains are linear-stack sky-structure statistics from one unit, ISO 200 sessions and one shared library master; denoising gains are isolated-module or synthetic; the halo, ghost and dust ideas are survey literature, and Origin's reflection geometry and filter passbands are undocumented. With a measured +-2% perturbation floor at the pixel scale, small effects need the harness before they are believed. The most useful single deliverable is therefore not a feature but the pair of independence-based instruments, split-half Fourier ring correlation and injection-recovery: they let the project say, for any of the roughly twenty ranked ideas, whether faint structure and resolution actually improved, and they would have caught the learned-background failure, which looked like an improvement until real nebula went missing.
