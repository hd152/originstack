## How should the PSF be modelled and estimated (spatial variation, sub-pixel sampling, star brightness/colour, per-frame vs coadd, joint multi-frame)?

### Takeaway
The professional PSF toolchain (PSFEx, PIFF, photutils ePSF) models the PSF as a small pixel-basis image whose pixels vary as a low-order polynomial (or interpolant) of position and optional "context" parameters, fitted with sub-pixel star centres and an iterative flux/centre/PSF loop; OriginStack's PSFs are a single integer-centred median or per-tile Moffat medians. The only structural gap that could change the delivered image (not just the classical RL fallback) is that the default proper coadd uses one global PSF per frame, so spatial (patch/tile) seeing and optics variation never reaches the final weights; whether that is worth fixing must be measured first (cheap experiment given below).

### Cited Findings
- PSFEx models the PSF either as a tabulated pixel basis (`BASIS_TYPE=PIXEL`, each pixel a free parameter, Lanczos4 interpolant to place it on each star, Tikhonov regularisation toward the median PSF with weight about 1e-2, chi-square outlier clipping at kappa=4) or as a Gauss-Laguerre shapelet basis; each PSF component is weighted by a polynomial in chosen parameters, and `PSFVAR_KEYS` can be X/Y image position (default) or any SExtractor measurement or FITS header keyword (e.g. `:AIRMASS`), with `PSFVAR_DEGREES` default 2 (a degree-3 polynomial in X,Y uses 20 PSF vectors) — [PSFEx documentation, "Working"](https://psfex.readthedocs.io/en/latest/Working.html)
- PSFEx star selection uses a half-light-radius-vs-magnitude box (`SAMPLE_VARIABILITY` 0.2), `SAMPLE_MINSN` 20, `SAMPLE_MAXELLIP` 0.3; for undersampled data it relies on interpolation, and its documentation says Fourier-domain super-resolution of the PSF "gives unsatisfactory results with real data" — [PSFEx documentation](https://psfex.readthedocs.io/en/latest/Working.html)
- photutils `EPSFBuilder` (Anderson & King 2000 ePSF, with modifications) re-centres each star with a symmetry-based centroid (`centroid_symmetry`, 5x5 box, `center_accuracy` 0.001 px, converged when 95% of stars move less than that), iterates up to `maxiters`=10, combines residuals with a sigma-clipped median in a 0.375-px half-width box, low-pass filters each iteration to suppress alias checkerboards, and states that for well-sampled data (FWHM of 4 px or more) an oversampling of 1 is often sufficient; stars must be background-subtracted beforehand and dithered exposures can be linked to constrain fluxes of undersampled data — [photutils EPSFBuilder API](https://docs.astropy.org/projects/photutils/en/stable/api/photutils.psf.EPSFBuilder.html)
- PIFF ("PSFs In the Full FOV") is the DES Year-3 PSF modeller; its paper (Jarvis et al., MNRAS 501, 1282, 2021) reports PSF-model systematic errors that are very small over the angular scales relevant to DES Y3 weak lensing and significantly smaller than in Y1 — [DES Y3 PSF modelling record](https://iac.es/es/ciencia-y-tecnologia/publicaciones/dark-energy-survey-year-3-results-point-spread-function-modelling). The retrieved page did not describe the interpolant; the Gaussian-process detail was not confirmed (see Gaps).
- Zackay & Ofek: the S/N-optimal coaddition applies a matched filter to each image using its own PSF and then sums with weights; methods that match-filter after coaddition or homogenise PSFs before coaddition lose sensitivity; gain over weighted coaddition "between a few and 25 percent" in survey speed (simulations and PTF DR2) — [arXiv:1512.06872](https://arxiv.org/abs/1512.06872). The retrieved abstract does not discuss within-image PSF variation or sub-image handling.
- RC Astro's BlurXTerminator page states that aberrations "are not assumed to be stationary" and vary across the field, that corner stars with coma are made round and then sharpened while round central stars are only sharpened, that the PSF is measured automatically from the image's own stars, and that aberration correction can run as a separate step or combined with sharpening — [RC Astro BlurXTerminator](https://www.rc-astro.com/software/bxt/)
- A low-rank-plus-sparse regulariser for space-variant deconvolution of galaxy survey images (Farrens, Ngole Mboula, Starck, A&A 601, A66, 2017) was tested on 10,000 space-based galaxy images with a known spatially varying Euclid-like PSF; it beats a sparsity prior once more than roughly 1000 galaxies are processed jointly. It operates on ensembles of galaxy stamps, each with its own local PSF, not on one full frame — [arXiv:1703.02305](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1703.02305)
- Fast models for space-variant blur used in astronomical deconvolution expand the blur operator over a small set of PSF samples with interpolation weights (Denis, Thiebaut, Soulez, ICIP 2011; IJCV 115, 253, 2015, which unifies several fast approximations as matrix decompositions); the ICIP test case was simulated HST data — [IJCV 2015 record](https://hal-ujm.archives-ouvertes.fr/ujm-00979825), [ICIP 2011 record](https://typeset.io/pdf/fast-model-of-space-variant-blurring-and-its-application-to-4sfi2c5sva.pdf)
- Celestron Origin (original): 152 mm aperture, 335 mm focal length (f/2.2), Sony IMX178 colour sensor, 2.4 um pixels, 3096 x 2080, about 1.48 arcsec/px and a 1.27 x 0.85 degree field — [Origin spec sheet (astroshop datasheet)](https://www.astroshop.ro/Produktdownloads/81182_2_Datenblatt-EN.pdf), [Scope Trader launch article](https://scopetrader.com/smart-telescope-origin-intelligent-launches-from-celestron/) (figures as summarised by the search tool; pages not opened, plate scale re-derived by me: 2.4 um / 335 mm = 1.477 arcsec/px)
- Repo: stack FWHMs on real sessions are 2.78-4.9 px (Gaussian), and the proper coadd output MTF is "the weighted RMS of the frame MTFs"; the project's blind-PSF code, SV-RL and NN rescale are as listed in the scope note — [dev-notes/siril-comparison.md](C:/source/originstack-research/dev-notes/siril-comparison.md), [dev-notes/module-notes.md](C:/source/originstack-research/dev-notes/module-notes.md), [src/psf_deconvolution.py](C:/source/originstack-research/src/psf_deconvolution.py)

### Inferences
Idea P1 - Sub-pixel-centred empirical PSF (ePSF) for `estimate_psf_blind` and the tile PSFs (small fix).
- What/why: the empirical PSF is built from cutouts centred on `int(round(centroid))`, so every cutout carries an extra uniform offset of variance 1/12 px^2 per axis. For a Gaussian stack PSF a mean stack of such cutouts is the PSF convolved with a 1-px box, which widens it by about sqrt(1 + (1/12)/sigma^2): +1.2% at FWHM 4.4 px (sigma 1.87), +3.0% at FWHM 2.78 px (sigma 1.18) (my arithmetic; the code takes a median, which behaves similarly but not identically), flattening the peak, so RL/FISTA is run with a PSF slightly wider and lower-peaked than the real one (under-correction).
- Algorithm: for each selected star (isolated >= 3 stamp-widths from a neighbour, unsaturated, SNR > 50, background subtracted by annulus median), (1) fit a symmetric centre (photutils `centroid_symmetry` or the existing least-squares in `nn_denoise.measure_star_fwhm`, which already returns dx,dy); (2) shift the stamp by (-dx,-dy) with a Fourier shift or Lanczos-3; (3) normalise by fitted flux; (4) sigma-clipped median over stars; (5) iterate 3-5 times: fit each star's flux/centre against the current PSF, rebuild, stop when 95% of centres move < 0.01 px (photutils defaults); (6) keep the existing Tukey taper. Oversampling 1 is enough at FWHM >= 4 px (photutils docs); use 2 below FWHM 3 px.
- Plug-in: `psf_deconvolution.estimate_psf_blind` (and `estimate_psf` tile calls). Cost: S (about 100 lines, numpy only). Gain: 1-3% PSF width bias removed in the classical path only; no effect on the NN path. Confidence: high that the bias exists, low that it is visible in the delivered image.

Idea P2 - Session PSF field model (PSFEx-style pixel basis x polynomial in position, with a colour term) replacing per-tile Moffat medians.
- What/why: continuous, noise-averaged PSF at any pixel from hundreds of stars (a 3096x2080 stack has many hundreds of usable stars) instead of 9 tiles of about 30 stars; supports a smooth tile grid for `richardson_lucy_svpsf` (6x6 or per-pixel interpolation instead of 3x3 piecewise-constant with feather blending), per-channel PSFs (the notes document B wider than R by 7-15% second-moment radius, a halo in the wings), and a colour context.
- Algorithm: stamps S_i (25x25) from stars i=1..N with Gaia BP-RP colour c_i and position (x_i,y_i) normalised to [-1,1]. Model: S_i(u,v) = F_i * sum_m a_m(x_i,y_i,c_i) B_m(u - dx_i, v - dy_i) + b_i, with a_m a polynomial (degree 2 in x,y = 6 terms; degree 1 in c) and B_m a free pixel basis (first basis = median PSF). Alternating least squares: (a) given B,a solve F_i,b_i,dx_i,dy_i per star (linearised); (b) given those, solve all B_m pixels in one linear system (normal equations of size (terms x 625)^2 is too big; solve per-pixel: for each (u,v) the unknowns are the polynomial coefficients, a 6-12 term least squares across N stars, with Tikhonov weight 1e-2 toward the median PSF and kappa=4 chi-square clipping, as PSFEx does). (c) evaluate PSF(x,y,c) on demand. Fit per Bayer colour channel of the linear stack, or on luminance plus a width scale per channel.
- Gate before building anything: the existing `--aberration-report` (`src/aberration.py`, per-cell FWHM/ellipticity) on 5-6 sessions. If max/min Gaussian FWHM across 6x6 cells is < 1.05 and ellipticity < 0.05 after the sessions' common rotation, the field model has nothing to correct; stop.
- Plug-in: new module (e.g. `psf_field.py`), consumed by `richardson_lucy_svpsf`, by per-tile proper coadd (Idea S1) and optionally by the NN path (a smooth local FWHM map instead of one global number). Cost: M for the model + RL-SV consumer; L if the NN path is made local (the network is trained for a roughly uniform width, a local resample would need per-tile warping or retraining). Risk: PSF-field noise in star-poor sparse fields (fall back to the global Moffat as today). Confidence in benefit: low-medium (unknown amplitude of variation on Origin), high in the technique.

Idea S1 - Tile-aware proper coaddition (restore "lucky patch" information that the default path currently discards).
- Finding in the code: the patch quality grids (Phase 1/2 Brenner patch scores) are used only by the patch-weighted sigma-clip stack, which proper coadd then replaces (`stacking.py` ~2190-2217); `proper_coadd` has no patch input. `dev-notes/siril-comparison.md` shows turning patch weighting off made Sunflower noisier (R 1.09 -> 1.15) before proper coadd became the default, i.e. patch weighting had a real effect that is now bypassed.
- Algorithm (keeps the FFT shortcut): the Zackay-Ofek estimator is a ratio of two sums over frames, num = sum_j (F_j/s_j^2) conj(P_j) M_j and den = sum_j (F_j^2/s_j^2) |P_j|^2. Per frame j and per tile-centre tau in a coarse grid (e.g. 3x4 over 3096x2080), measure (a) noise s_{j,tau} (lag-4 MAD in the tile), (b) PSF width scale g_{j,tau} (stars in the tile, or the session field of Idea P2 rescaled by the frame's global width), (c) transparency F_{j,tau}. Compute each frame's FFT once; accumulate K=12 numerator/denominator pairs (one per tile centre) with the tile-specific weights; run K inverse FFTs; blend R_tau with a smooth partition of unity (bilinear hat functions) and normalise per tile (each R_tau/sqrt(sum_j F_{j,tau}^2/s_{j,tau}^2) is an unbiased flux estimate, so the blend is flux-preserving). Splitting the same accumulators over odd/even frames also yields the half-stacks needed for Idea E1 for the price of two extra inverse FFTs.
- Cost: accumulate step is K-fold (the notes say the coadd accumulate is memory-bound: 25 ms/frame at 6 threads), so K=4 (quadrants) is roughly +30-60 s on a 500-frame session; K=12 would roughly triple the coadd's accumulate cost. Native work: extend `proper_coadd_accum32_tile` to K weight sets. Risk: M (output PSF becomes spatially varying, which the global-FWHM NN normalisation does not model; test on `bench_vs_siril` shared-star width and noise).
- Gate before building: for 10 sessions compute, per frame, the Gaussian FWHM of stars in 4 quadrants; remove the per-frame mean and the per-quadrant session mean (instrument pattern); if the residual (frame x quadrant) scatter is < 3% of FWHM there is no lucky-patch signal to exploit and S1 should be dropped. Expected gain if the scatter is 5-10%: a few percent narrower stars at equal noise in the affected tiles (my estimate, no external measurement found). Confidence: low-medium.

Idea P3 - Carry the analytic coadd PSF forward and use it as a self-test.
- From the repo's own formula, the proper-coadd transfer function is |P_eff(f)|^2 = sum_j w_j |P_j(f)|^2 / sum_j w_j (w_j = F_j^2/s_j^2), real and non-negative, i.e. a zero-phase (point-symmetric) PSF: asymmetries such as trailing or coma of individual frames do not propagate into the coadd's PSF (my reading of the note's formula; derivation: numerator uses conj(P_j) P_j). Phase 4 currently re-measures the PSF from 30-60 stack stars; the analytic P_eff uses every frame and every star.
- Use: (1) feed P_eff to RL/FISTA/relative-kernel deconvolution (Idea D1), (2) compare to the measured star profile in the stack; a disagreement > 3% in FWHM flags that the elliptical-Moffat model is inadequate (the notes already needed an FWHM-parametrised Moffat with beta bounds to fit the Omega stack), a free health check. Cost: S. Confidence: medium. Caveat: the Moffat fit and the stack star measurement both include resampling blur, so compare like with like.

Idea P4 - Camera-constant instrument PSF prior (extension of P2).
- If the stack Gaussian FWHM of the sharpest sessions is 2.78 px (4.1 arcsec) while seeing in the notes' seeing-pair study is the dominant variable (sharp 30% frames are 0.78-0.89 of the rest), a large part of the width is camera-constant (optics, pixel, debayer, resampling). A per-model PSF-field prior (like `camera_profile.py`'s shipped per-model constants) could stabilise tile fits in star-poor tiles. Needs data across many sessions (the project has 80+) and a measurement of the camera-constant term; unverified. Cost: M. Confidence: low.

Joint multi-frame deconvolution (not recommended): given Zackay-Ofek's result that the matched-filter/proper coadd is the S/N-optimal linear statistic and that PSF-homogenising frames before coaddition loses sensitivity ([arXiv:1512.06872](https://arxiv.org/abs/1512.06872)), a joint multi-frame deconvolution under Gaussian noise differs from "proper coadd, then deconvolve once" only through priors and positivity, which the single Phase 4 deconvolver already supplies (my inference; no paper was found comparing the two for ground-based OSC data).

## Which deconvolution solvers, priors and star/extended-object strategies would add something to what OriginStack already has?

### Takeaway
The classical path is the weakest link (full-PSF RL with a 5 x FWHM star mask cannot sharpen stars or anything near them), and Magain, Courbin and Sohy's 1998 "deconvolution with correct sampling" is the cleanest published fix: deconvolve not to a delta but to a narrower, well-sampled target PSF using the relative kernel, which removes ringing by construction and calibrates the output PSF. For the shipped NN path the evidence found says its width/noise curve is at a plateau for its training data, so the useful additions are consistency checks and data-driven strength selection rather than a different network; plug-and-play and unrolled approaches (AIRI, DPIR, Tikhonet, Deep Wiener) are real but need tuning or retraining.

### Cited Findings
- Magain, Courbin, Sohy (ApJ 494, 472, 1998): deconvolving with the full observed PSF t pushes the solution past the sampling limit; instead choose a narrower final PSF r (usually Gaussian, a few pixels FWHM, compatible with the sampling) and deconvolve with the kernel s defined by t = r * s (neither constrained positive). The sky model is f(x) = h(x) + sum_k a_k r(x - c_k): point sources with free amplitude and position, each exactly the narrow PSF r, plus a smooth background h restricted to frequencies the sampling supports. Functional S = sum_i (1/sigma_i^2)[sum_j s_ij (h_j + sum_k a_k r(x_j - c_k)) - d_i]^2 + lambda sum_i [h_i - sum_j r_ij h_j]^2, with a pixel-dependent lambda_i adjusted so smoothed normalised residuals are about 1 and the number of point sources set to the smallest value giving locally correct residuals. Reported: no ringing around point sources (contrasted with maximum entropy and Richardson-Lucy); a 200-star simulated field (197 stars with a neighbour inside 2 FWHM) with no systematic photometric error; astrometry better than 0.1 px even in severe blends (0.02 px for 1-2 FWHM blends at high S/N); on simulated data a two-channel Lucy deconvolution had largest residual 14 sigma vs 4.8 sigma and mean photometric deviation 7% vs 1% for the new method; ground-based Sk 157 cluster resolved >40 stars vs 12 from earlier maximum entropy work; optimisation is conjugate-gradient and can miss the global minimum with many sources — [Magain et al. via ar5iv astro-ph/9704059](https://ar5iv.arxiv.org/html/astro-ph/9704059), [abstract](https://arxiv.org/abs/astro-ph/9704059)
- Sureau, Lechat, Starck (A&A 641, A67, 2020), "Deep learning for space-variant deconvolution in galaxy surveys": a U-Net post-processes the closed-form Tikhonov deconvolution (and an ADMM variant with the learned network); both reportedly outperform convex-optimisation methods on GREAT3 simulations; the Tikhonov+U-Net variant is the most accurate except for ellipticity at high S/N where ADMM is slightly better, ADMM is cheaper for millions of galaxies — [arXiv:1911.00443](https://arxiv.org/abs/1911.00443). The retrieved page does not state the exact network input or numbers.
- Dong, Roth, Schiele (NeurIPS 2020), "Deep Wiener Deconvolution": Wiener deconvolution applied to learned deep features, followed by a multi-scale feature refinement network, trained end to end for non-blind deblurring; reported to beat state of the art "by a wide margin" on simulated and real blur — [NeurIPS 2020 abstract](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper/2020/hash/0b8aff0438617c055eb55f0ba5d226fa-Abstract.html) (abstract only; numbers not verified)
- Zhang et al., "Plug-and-Play Image Restoration with Deep Denoiser Prior" (DPIR): a CNN denoiser is inserted into half-quadratic splitting and applied to deblurring, super-resolution and demosaicing; reported to beat model-based methods and be competitive with or better than learning-based ones — [arXiv:2008.13751](https://arxiv.org/abs/2008.13751) (abstract only)
- Terris, Dabbech, Tang, Wiaux (MNRAS 518, 604, 2023), AIRI: a DNN trained as a denoiser at a noise level estimated from the data's S/N replaces the handcrafted proximal regulariser in a forward-backward loop (gradient step on the data term, then denoise); competitive with SARA/uSARA in quality and much faster, with CLEAN faster but of lower quality and an end-to-end DNN faster still but much worse; a 2023 follow-up reports the denoisers plug into other algorithmic backbones and are robust to variations — [arXiv:2202.12959](https://arxiv.org/abs/2202.12959v2), [arXiv:2312.07137](https://www.arxiv.org/pdf/2312.07137). This is radio interferometry, not optical imaging.
- The Starck-Pantin-Murtagh review (PASP 114, 1051, 2002) concludes that most recent astronomical deconvolution work incorporates some form of multiresolution, and that different algorithms reflect different noise models and point-like vs extended assumptions — [Royal Holloway record](https://repository.royalholloway.ac.uk/items/d8089a5b-db1d-2848-e6c5-629d44411776/3) (abstract-level only; the multiresolution-support RL description was not retrieved).
- Repo evidence that the NN is at a plateau for its data: a stricter seeing cut, cross-night pairs and a 2-4x wider U-Net all land on v3's width-noise curve at equal noise; the faint-source flux lost without a low-frequency "restore" is "up to 2/3" — [CLAUDE.md "Tried and reverted"](C:/source/originstack-research/CLAUDE.md), [nn-deconv-runbook](C:/source/originstack-research/dev-notes/nn-deconv-runbook.md)
- The earlier notes already record that no 2022+ paper proposes a fundamentally new RL regulariser and that diffusion/DIP/transformer approaches have no validation on OSC amateur data — [earlier note](C:/source/originstack-research/research_notes/Astrophotography%20stacking%20techniques%20since%202022/denoising_psf_deconvolution.md)

### Inferences
Idea D1 - Relative-kernel (target-PSF) deconvolution for the classical path, plus a residual-consistency check usable on the NN output.
- What/why: today `--deconvolve rl` asks RL to invert the whole PSF, so it rings and has to be masked off every star and its 5 x FWHM surroundings (and off every pixel above p99.7). Following Magain et al., ask for a narrower, well-sampled output PSF r instead (for a 4.4 px stack, r = 3.5 px Gaussian); the kernel to invert is then s with t = r * s, a much milder deconvolution (for Gaussians FWHM_s = sqrt(FWHM_t^2 - FWHM_r^2): 2.7 px for FWHM_t 4.4 -> FWHM_r 3.5, i.e. ratio 0.8), and the output image obeys the sampling theorem, so stars, galaxy cores and their neighbourhoods can all be processed without a protection mask. Useful as (i) a ringing-free classical sharpener, (ii) the fallback when `nn_stack_guard` reverts the NN on non-Origin data (`postprocess._apply_auto_nn_guard`), (iii) a calibrated output PSF for photometry and honest evaluation.
- Algorithm: (1) t = stack PSF (existing `estimate_psf`, or Idea P1/P3), N = 31-41 px. (2) Choose rho in [0.7, 0.85], FWHM_r = rho * FWHM_t, refuse if FWHM_r < 2.3 px (sampling). (3) S(f) = T(f) conj(R(f)) / (|R(f)|^2 + eps), eps ~ 1e-3 max|R|^2; inverse FFT; truncate to radius 2.5 FWHM_s; clip negatives and renormalise to unit sum; verify ||t - r*s|| / ||t|| < 2% (else fall back to the analytic Gaussian-width kernel). (4) Run the existing `_rl_deconvolve_xp(y, s, k)` on luminance for k about 10-30 (or until the measured output star FWHM reaches FWHM_r within 3%, a self-calibrating stop that needs no ground truth); drop `star_mask` except for clipped cores (flat tops), which RL still mishandles. (5) Check: residual e = (y - s * x)/sigma should be noise-like on sky; report reduced chi-square on star-free pixels and the rms of e within 3 FWHM of the 40 brightest stars (> 1.5 flags inconsistency). (6) For per-channel colour, optionally use each channel's own t_c and one common r (a linear, flux-conserving way to equalise channel PSFs; supported only by the notes' B-vs-R wing observation, unproven).
- NN extension: measure s_nn from the same stars in the NN input y and output x (Wiener-regularised division of their stacked profiles); e = (y - s_nn * x)/sigma should be white; coherent structure in smoothed e marks lost flux (faint extended light) or invented structure. The existing `nn_restore_sigma` (4 px Gaussian lowpass of y - x) is the s_nn ~ 1 special case of this data-consistency step, so the incremental value on the shipped path is mostly the diagnostic, not a replacement for the restore.
- Source: Magain et al. 1998 (above). Cost: S-M (about 200 lines numpy; reuses existing RL; tests: ringing absent on synthetic star+galaxy, output FWHM equals target). Expected gain: star FWHM -> rho x original on the classical path at the noise of an RL deconvolution to that width; I found no published number for ground-based amateur data, so the gain is a design property, not an externally measured result. Confidence: medium-high that it removes the star-mask limitation; medium that it beats the NN (it will not, on Origin data; the NN stays default).
- Noise limit worth stating (my calculation, Gaussian model, FWHM_t 4.4 px): narrowing the FWHM by 10% needs gains of 1.34 / 2.3 / 5.0 at 0.15 / 0.25 / 0.35 cycles/px; by 20% needs 1.75 / 4.7 / 21; by 30% needs 2.2 / 9.0 / 74. So a 20% narrowing already amplifies the 4-px-period structure of a star 4.7x; usable narrowing is set by S/N at those frequencies, which is why stars (a known point-source prior, as in Magain's model and in the NN) can be narrowed far more than extended structure.

Idea D2 - Gaia-anchored point-source channel (MCS-style two-channel model) - NOT recommended now.
- Magain's model with positions fixed from Gaia (the project has `gaia_cache`, `local_solve` and a refined stack WCS) would reduce the non-linear fit to a linear solve for amplitudes and give exact star subtraction for crowded fields and star-adjacent galaxy light. Risk: structurally close to the reverted "starless-layer deconvolution and separate-layer stretch" and to the existing NN star remover; positional errors of 0.1-0.3 px leave dipoles; Magain notes the optimisation can miss the global minimum with many sources. Listed only so it is not re-proposed without that history. Confidence in benefit: low.

Idea D3 - Data-driven strength and cutoff selection from independent half-stacks (see Idea E1/E2 in the evaluation section); this is the main new use of "priors and solvers" knowledge that fits the pipeline (it removes hand-tuned iteration counts, `nn_deconv_blend`, `restore_sigma` guesses).

Idea D4 - Noise-gated multiresolution RL (MR-RL), optional.
- Variant of RL that keeps positivity and flux conservation but only lets significant residual structure drive the update: y_k = H x_k + W[ y - H x_k ] where W keeps only wavelet coefficients of the residual above k_sigma * sigma_j (a multiresolution support), then the usual RL multiplicative update with y_k in place of y. Uses `src/wavelet.py` and the stack's noise map (`--uncertainty-map` sigma or `noise_model`). Source: the Starck-Pantin-Murtagh review (details recalled, not retrieved; verify against the paper before implementing). Overlaps with `sparse_wavelet_deconvolve` (L1 prior) but, unlike FISTA-L1, never shrinks flux. Cost: M. Gain: bounded noise amplification of the classical path; no external measurement found. Confidence: low-medium. Lower priority than D1.

Idea D5 - Plug-and-play / data-consistency refinement around the shipped network (experimental).
- Reuse `src/data/nn_denoise.npz` (a Noise2Noise denoiser already working in "noise units") as the prior in an AIRI/DPIR-style loop, initialised with the NN-deconvolved image, so no training is needed: z = x - gamma * S^T((S x - y)/sigma^2) restricted to the SNR > 1 band; x <- (1-a) z + a * D(z); K = 2-4 iterations; step gamma <= sigma^2/||S||^2; positivity; S = relative kernel s_nn from D1. Intended effect: enforce flux consistency and cap invented structure at frequencies the data cannot support. Risks: the denoiser is trained at one noise level, and iterates are not at that noise level (DPIR feeds a noise-level map; AIRI trains the denoiser at a noise level chosen from the data S/N), so strength tuning is empirical; cost K extra forward passes (the project already runs two tiles concurrently); AIRI's evidence is radio interferometry. Gain unknown; not recommended before D1/E1 exist. Confidence: low.

Idea D6 - Tikhonet / Deep-Wiener hybrid with PSF conditioning (training required, long term).
- Make the network PSF-aware instead of resampling the image into its trained width range: first stage a Wiener/Tikhonov deconvolution with the measured local PSF (closed form, FFT), second stage the U-Net on that output plus a noise-level map. Sureau et al. and Dong et al. both pair a physics-based Wiener/Tikhonov step with a CNN. Training would reuse the seeing-pair banks (`tools/gen_n2n_pairs.py`, `tools/train_nn_denoise.py`). Given the capacity sweep and "more data" experiments, expect no gain at equal noise on Origin data; the value is for other cameras (ROADMAP section 1) and spatially varying widths. Cost: L (train). Confidence: low-medium; skip unless non-Origin support becomes the priority.

Considered and not recommended: diffusion posterior sampling and DIP/DIPLI (already covered in the earlier note; per-image optimisation or large models are not laptop-CPU friendly and have no OSC validation); kernel-regression/CFA-drizzle variants (rejected in the repo).

### Gaps
- The Starck-Pantin-Murtagh multiresolution-support RL algorithm and its published performance numbers were not retrieved (only the review's abstract and bibliographic data).
- No optical (non-radio) validation of plug-and-play deconvolution with a Noise2Noise denoiser as the prior was found; AIRI/DPIR evidence is radio-interferometric and natural-image.
- The "DeepWiener" named in the assignment was not found under that name; the closest matches are Deep Wiener Deconvolution (Dong et al., NeurIPS 2020) and, for turbulence, the BM-WF / BM-CNN of Hoffmire/Hardie (see next section).
- Magain et al.'s tests are on simulated data and ground-based images at 0.35-1.1 arcsec scales with CCDs; no source reports the method on one-shot-colour CMOS stacks.
- Euclid/LSST-specific deconvolution pipelines beyond Farrens et al. and Sureau et al. were not covered.

## What can seeing-aware methods (lucky imaging, anisoplanatic registration, turbulence mitigation, short exposures, patch weighting) still add?

### Takeaway
Nothing in the lucky-imaging or video turbulence-mitigation literature applies directly: both rely on millisecond-scale frames that freeze the atmosphere, whereas Origin sub-exposures are 10-30 s, so the speckle/tilt structure is already averaged inside each frame and what remains is slow seeing variation between frames, which the project's quality gating, patch weights and proper-coadd per-frame weights already address. The one seeing-adjacent item that is both new and deterministic is intra-exposure field-rotation smear on an alt-az mount, worth a quantitative check on each session before any modelling.

### Cited Findings
- Lucky imaging relies on the probability of a near-diffraction-limited short exposure, Fried's P ~ 5.6 exp(-0.1557 (D/r0)^2): about 1 in 9 at D/r0 = 5, 1 in 50 at 6, 1 in 3800 at 8; frame selection works best around D/r0 of 6-7, and a 2009 Baldwin-group study found Strehl gains of 4-6 over D/r0 = 3-12 with a slight peak near 7 — [An investigation of lucky imaging techniques, arXiv:0906.3041](https://arxiv.org/pdf/0906.3041), [Law, Mackay, Baldwin 2006, ar5iv](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/astro-ph/0507299) (figures as summarised by the search tool)
- A contrasting view in that literature: lucky imaging is effective only for D/r0 up to about 8, because beyond it the speckled halo exceeds the coherent peak — [arXiv:0906.3041](https://arxiv.org/pdf/0906.3041)
- Zhu & Milanfar (IEEE TPAMI 35(1), 157, 2013): non-rigid (B-spline) registration to undo geometric distortion, temporal regression to a fused image that behaves as if convolved with a space-invariant near-diffraction-limited blur, then blind deconvolution; this reduces the space- and time-varying deblurring to a shift-invariant one — [TPAMI abstract record](https://katalog.dhi-paris.fr/vufind/Record/NLM222643366), [manuscript PDF](https://users.soe.ucsc.edu/%7emilanfar/publications/journal/manuscript_turbulence.pdf)
- Deep-learning turbulence mitigation (BM-CNN of Hoffmire/Hardie et al., Optical Engineering 60(3), 2021, replaces the Wiener step of block-matching + Wiener filtering with a CNN; DATUM, arXiv:2401.04244, is a recurrent network mirroring registration, temporal fusion and post-processing; a 2024 review is arXiv:2409.14587) is designed for video-rate imaging through strong (long-range horizontal-path) turbulence with simulators to generate training data — [SPIE Opt. Eng. paper](https://journals.spiedigitallibrary.org/journals/optical-engineering/volume-60/issue-03/033103/Deep-learning-for-anisoplanatic-optical-turbulence-mitigation-in-long-range/10.1117/1.OE.60.3.033103.full), [DATUM](https://alphaxiv.org/abs/2401.04244), [review](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2409.14587)
- Origin sub-exposures in the repo's benchmarks are 10 s (Sculptor), 20 s (Sunflower), 30 s (Omega, Whirlpool), and the repo already has per-patch Brenner weights, an `--elastic-registration` displacement field (`fit_displacement_field`), per-frame elliptical-Moffat proper coadd weights and the "sharp 30% vs rest" seeing-pair study (0.78-0.89 relative FWHM) — [dev-notes/siril-comparison.md](C:/source/originstack-research/dev-notes/siril-comparison.md), [nn-deconv-runbook](C:/source/originstack-research/dev-notes/nn-deconv-runbook.md), [CLAUDE.md](C:/source/originstack-research/CLAUDE.md)
- The repo's session report already measures per-frame rotation from the registration transforms (the frame centre's displacement, with a quadratic baseline), and the first real session report read a rotation of 0.5 deg/min; on one low-SNR session with translation-only registration (affine matching had declined for lack of stars) 2.3 deg of between-frame field rotation arced stars by about 60 px at the edge, which is why registration now rescues thin catalogs (this is between-frame misregistration, not within-exposure smear) — [dev-notes/module-notes.md](C:/source/originstack-research/dev-notes/module-notes.md) (session_report and registration_stars rows)

### Inferences
- D/r0 for the Origin aperture (152 mm) is 3.0 / 3.8 / 4.5 / 5.3 / 6.0 at seeing of 2.0 / 2.5 / 3.0 / 3.5 / 4.0 arcsec (my arithmetic with r0 = 0.98 lambda / seeing at 500 nm, a standard relation that was not retrieved); the Fried probability of a diffraction-limited short exposure is then 1.0 / 0.62 / 0.24 / 0.075 / 0.02. So a lucky-imaging gain is physically available on this aperture in poor seeing, but only for exposures of order a few milliseconds (atmospheric coherence time, standard value not retrieved); the Origin's 10-30 s subs average the turbulence completely. Unless the camera can deliver high-rate short exposures, "freeze the seeing" is not available, and the SER reader already in the repo (`io_ser.py`) is the relevant route for planetary/lucky data, not for DSO subs.
- The Zhu-Milanfar pipeline (non-rigid registration, fusion, one space-invariant deconvolution) is structurally the pipeline OriginStack already runs (elastic registration option, proper coadd, one Phase 4 deconvolver), so it supplies no new algorithm; its premise (after registration the residual blur is space-invariant) is exactly what Idea S1/P2 test on Origin data.
- Patch/region-level selection: the one gap found is that lucky-region information (per-patch Brenner scores) no longer reaches the default output (Idea S1, in the PSF section).
- Idea F1 - Deterministic in-exposure field-rotation smear in the PSF (alt-az specific, conditional).
  - What: an alt-az mount without a derotator rotates the field during each exposure about the optical axis, so every frame has a tangential smear of length L(r) = r * omega_j * t_j (r in px from the rotation centre, omega_j the rotation rate in rad/s, t_j the exposure). Registration removes the between-frame rotation but not the within-frame smear; in the aligned stack all frames' smears are tangential about the same centre and add coherently. The project's global elliptical Moffat per frame cannot represent a radius-dependent direction.
  - Size (my arithmetic): rate 0.5 deg/min and 10 s -> 0.083 deg -> 1.45e-3 rad -> 1.5 px at r = 1000 px and 2.7 px at the corner (r ~ 1860 px); 20 s doubles that. For motion blur of length L the variance is L^2/12: L = 3 px adds 0.75 px^2 to sigma^2 = 3.5 px^2 (FWHM 4.4 px), about +10% tangential width; L = 1.75 px adds about +3.6%. At 0.4 deg per whole session (the CLAUDE.md bound for the 13 local sessions) the smear is below about 0.3 px for sessions of 20 minutes or more at 30 s subs, so it is irrelevant on those sessions; it matters only for fast-rotating (near-meridian) pointings.
  - Algorithm: from `session_report.collect_session_table` take per-frame rotation angle and time; omega_j = d(theta)/dt from neighbouring frames, L_j(r) = r * omega_j * t_j. Trigger only if max_j L_j(r_max) >= 0.5 px. Proper coadd: multiply frame j's spectrum, tile by tile, by sinc(pi L_j(r_tau) k . t_hat_tau) (the Fourier transform of a line segment) inside P_j (requires the tile-aware coadd of Idea S1); deconvolution: the stack-level kernel at a tile is the frame-weighted mixture of segments of lengths L_j, convolved into the tile's PSF (Idea P2).
  - Source: no external publication was found for correcting this; derived from geometry. The rotation-rate formula for an alt-az mount (15.04 arcsec/s x cos(latitude) x cos(azimuth) / cos(altitude)) was NOT confirmed by any retrieved source, which is why the algorithm takes the rate from the measured registration transforms instead. Cost: M, only worthwhile with S1. Confidence: low-medium; first run a one-session check of tangential-vs-radial FWHM at the corners with `--aberration-report` on a meridian-crossing, long-sub session.
- Short-exposure strategy for the Origin itself: if sub length can be shortened without losing S/N (the notes' "sharp 30%" study shows seeing quality varies strongly between frames), shorter subs with proper-coadd weighting (already implemented) are the practical way to exploit seeing variation; nothing further to implement.

### Gaps
- No source quantified anisoplanatic (differential) image motion for a 152 mm aperture at 1.3 x 0.85 degrees; the isoplanatic angle and differential-tilt variance are therefore not estimated here. The existing elastic-registration option is the test.
- The Fried-probability and lucky-imaging figures were taken from search-tool summaries of arXiv:0906.3041 and astro-ph/0507299, not from reading the full texts.
- The "DeepWiener" turbulence method named in the assignment was not located; BM-CNN, DATUM and Deep Wiener Deconvolution are the nearest items found.
- The field-rotation rate formula and the Origin firmware's behaviour on rotation (any exposure cap) were not verified from a retrieved source.

## How much real gain is left from sub-pixel / multi-frame super-resolution on this Bayer sensor?

### Takeaway
Very little: at the project's measured stack widths (2.8-4.9 px FWHM, at 1.48 arcsec/px) the PSF is essentially fully sampled in the luminance/green grid and only marginally aliased in the half-resolution R/B planes, so extra sub-pixel information to recover is small; the real limit is S/N after deconvolution. The project's own CFA-drizzle and kernel-regression results agree, and general super-resolution theory says reconstruction constraints carry less and less information as magnification grows.

### Cited Findings
- Baker & Kanade (CVPR 2000; IEEE TPAMI 24(9), 1167, 2002): analytical results showing that reconstruction constraints provide less and less useful information as the magnification factor increases, and that for large enough magnification any smoothness prior leads to overly smooth results; they propose learned recognition-based priors for specific scene classes — [Limits on Super-Resolution and How to Break Them (CMU RI)](https://ri.cmu.edu/publications/limits-on-super-resolution-and-how-to-break-them) (a numeric magnification threshold in one extracted copy was garbled and is not used)
- Repo measurements: Bayer drizzle on the Fireworks Galaxy (FWHM 4.9 px): luma noise -13%, chroma noise +110%, star sharpness -5%; on dithered Whirlpool: stars 2.5% wider and pixel noise +25 to +75%; kernel-regression merge: stars 8-45% wider and 1.3-2.9x noisier than RCD + proper coadd at matched width; 2x Lanczos-3 drizzle on Fireworks/M66: no sharper at equal noise except 5% on M66 for 4x the pixels; the notes expect a CFA-drizzle benefit only for FWHM under about 2.5 px — [dev-notes/module-notes.md](C:/source/originstack-research/dev-notes/module-notes.md), [dev-notes/siril-comparison.md](C:/source/originstack-research/dev-notes/siril-comparison.md)
- photutils documentation: for well-sampled data (FWHM >= 4 px) an ePSF oversampling of 1 is often sufficient — [photutils EPSFBuilder](https://docs.astropy.org/projects/photutils/en/stable/api/photutils.psf.EPSFBuilder.html); PSFEx documentation: Fourier-domain super-resolution of undersampled PSFs "gives unsatisfactory results with real data" — [PSFEx](https://psfex.readthedocs.io/en/latest/Working.html)
- The earlier note records the amateur practice of pairing 2x drizzle with BlurXTerminator for contrast restoration and the absence of any shipped classical Irani-Peleg IBP in the major tools — [earlier note, combine_rejection_drizzle_superres.md](C:/source/originstack-research/research_notes/Astrophotography%20stacking%20techniques%20since%202022/combine_rejection_drizzle_superres.md)

### Inferences
- Aliasing budget (my calculation, Gaussian PSF, MTF = exp(-2 pi^2 sigma^2 f^2)): at the full-resolution Nyquist (0.5 cycles/px) the stack MTF is 3e-5 (FWHM 3.4 px), 3e-8 (4.4 px), 2e-10 (5.0 px), so luminance sampling loses nothing. At the R/B plane Nyquist (0.25 cycles/px of the full grid) the MTF is 0.076 (3.4 px), 0.013 (4.4 px), 0.004 (5.0 px) and about 0.18 for the sharpest benchmark stack (2.78 px, Sculptor, 10 s subs). Appreciable aliased energy in R/B therefore appears only for the sharpest stacks (about 0.18 at 2.78 px, borderline at 0.076 for 3.4 px); on the Fireworks (4.9 px) and Whirlpool (3.4-4.9 px) sessions the repo's CFA drizzle already lost, and the kernel-regression merge lost on Sunflower (3.6 px), to RCD + proper coadd on noise and width.
- Beyond drizzle, the remaining multi-frame super-resolution families (sparse/Bayesian MAP, Gaussian-process/Fourier-domain interpolation, bilateral-TV L1 reconstruction) are all constrained by the same information limit: with aliased energy this small there is nothing to unmix, and the regulariser decides the result. No source was found showing a measured width-at-equal-noise gain from them on ground-based OSC data at these sampling ratios. Recommendation: do not spend effort on further sub-pixel reconstruction; the resolvable-detail lever is S/N-limited deconvolution and weighting.
- A cheap way to keep this conclusion honest per session: report the R/B-plane aliasing budget (the MTF at 0.25 cycles/px from the measured Gaussian FWHM) in the dither report (`src/dither_report.py`) and enable CFA-drizzle suggestions only below about 2.5 px FWHM, which the project's own notes already imply.

### Gaps
- No external primary source quantifying theoretically achievable resolution gain from dithered Bayer data at 3-5 px FWHM was found; the aliasing numbers above are my own Gaussian-model calculation.
- Gaussian-process and Fourier-domain multi-frame super-resolution papers were not searched for specifically (out of tool budget); Farsiu-type bilateral-TV L1 reconstruction and joint multi-frame demosaic-SR were not retrieved.
- The Baker-Kanade numeric limit was not usable (garbled extraction).

## How can sharpness gains be evaluated honestly?

### Takeaway
The project already scores star width at equal noise on shared stars, faint-source recovery, Gaia pair/single-star splits and survey truth (Pan-STARRS); the missing piece is a frequency-resolved, ground-truth-free measure built from independent half-stacks, which can also drive parameter selection. Fourier ring correlation (FRC) between two independent halves is the established tool in microscopy for exactly this, including monitoring iterative deconvolution; independent-subset splitting is the same principle behind self-supervised inverse-problem training.

### Cited Findings
- Koho et al. (Nature Communications 10, 3103, 2019): FRC, commonly used to measure effective image resolution, can also drive restoration: FRC-based frequency-domain denoising filtering, blind linear and non-linear deconvolution that estimates the effective PSF directly from the images using FRC, and FRC as a metric to observe the progress of iterative deconvolution — [Koho et al. 2019 (record)](https://research.utu.fi/converis/portal/detail/Publication/41357558?lang=en_US), [DOAJ entry](https://doaj.org/article/0a6642b240384880a542af3c8e2edb97). The full text was not retrievable (PMC, Nature and core.ac.uk blocked), so formulas below are derived by me, not quoted.
- Noise2Inverse (Hendriksen, Pelt, Batenburg, IEEE Trans. Computational Imaging 2020): uses the noise model to compute multiple statistically independent reconstructions from the measurements and shows that training on them yields a denoising CNN without clean data; methods that assume pixel-independent noise fail in inverse problems because that assumption does not hold — [arXiv:2001.11801](https://arxiv.org/abs/2001.11801) (abstract only)
- Magain et al. evaluate with injected-source photometry/astrometry and normalised residuals (reduced residual about 1 locally), including blends at 1-2 FWHM and a two-channel Lucy comparison — [ar5iv astro-ph/9704059](https://ar5iv.arxiv.org/html/astro-ph/9704059)
- Sureau et al. evaluate with shape (ellipticity) recovery on simulated galaxies with known truth — [arXiv:1911.00443](https://arxiv.org/abs/1911.00443)
- The project's existing metrics: same-star FWHM at matched noise (`tools/bench_vs_siril.py`, `common_star_fwhm`), `tools/eval_nn_denoise.py` (error vs an independent half-stack), `tools/eval_nn_faint.py` (faint-source recovery, confirmed peaks), `tools/eval_gaia_pairs.py` (resolved Gaia pairs, single-star split test), Pan-STARRS truth (`tools/eval_ps1_truth.py`), second-night starlet-scale regression — [CLAUDE.md](C:/source/originstack-research/CLAUDE.md), [nn-deconv-runbook](C:/source/originstack-research/dev-notes/nn-deconv-runbook.md), [phase4-quality](C:/source/originstack-research/dev-notes/phase4-quality.md)

### Inferences
Idea E1 - FRC / spectral-SNR curve from odd-even half-stacks.
- Halves: free inside proper coadd. The estimator is a ratio of sums over frames (numerator sum_j (F_j/s_j^2) conj(P_j) M_j, denominator sum_j (F_j^2/s_j^2)|P_j|^2); accumulate odd and even frames in separate accumulator sets and form A, B, and A+B together for two extra inverse FFTs. (Alternatively reuse `noise_validation`'s odd/even means, which exist only under `--noise-validate`.) Interleave frames by sorted quality so both halves have the same PSF.
- Computation: rings in cycles/px; FRC(q) = Re sum_ring F_A conj(F_B) / sqrt(sum_ring |F_A|^2 sum_ring |F_B|^2) on Hann-windowed 1024^2 crops, star-masked or not as the question requires. With equal-noise halves A = S + N_A, B = S + N_B, E[FRC] = SNR_h / (1 + SNR_h), so SNR_h = FRC/(1 - FRC) per half and the combined stack has SSNR_full = 2 FRC/(1 - FRC) (my derivation).
- Uses: (a) an effective-resolution number (the q where FRC falls to a chosen level; conventional levels in cryo-EM/microscopy such as 1/7 and 0.5 were not retrieved) for stack-to-stack and method-to-method comparison without ground truth; (b) parameter-free Wiener deconvolution: with SSNR(q) defined on the observed signal, G(q) = (1/T(q)) * SSNR/(1 + SSNR), i.e. the inverse filter shrunk by the data's own FRC (the "FRC filter" idea of Koho et al.), using t from Idea P1/P3; (c) a frequency cutoff for any deconvolver or the NN output: low-pass the output where SSNR < 1 to bound invented high-frequency detail; (d) RL stopping: run the classical deconvolver on A and B separately and pick the iteration k that maximises the area under FRC(q; D_k(A), D_k(B)) over the resolved band (Koho et al.: FRC as a monitor of iterative deconvolution), evaluated on star-free sky/extended regions because identical ringing in both halves would inflate FRC.
- Caveats: learned priors reproduce the same hallucination in both halves, so FRC overstates their resolution; use it for classical/linear methods and combine with the Pan-STARRS and Gaia-pair checks for the NN. Cost: S-M (numpy FFT, ring binning, about 150 lines; tool `tools/eval_frc.py` first, pipeline hook later). Gain: indirect (honest metric, removes manual strength tuning); confidence medium-high for the metric, medium for the stopping rule.

Idea E2 - Projected cross-validation score for strength selection (secondary).
- Score J(theta) = mean over analysis pixels of ((B - T_B * x_hat_A(theta))/sigma_B)^2: x_hat_A is a deconvolution of half A, T_B the blur of half B. Because B's noise is independent of x_hat_A, E J = ||W T_B (x - x_hat_A)||^2 + N sigma^2 (cross terms vanish), so J estimates the error visible after blurring without ground truth or a noise model (same independence principle as Noise2Inverse). Important limitation (my reasoning): the criterion is blind to errors the PSF suppresses, which is where RL amplifies noise, so minimising J alone biases toward too many iterations; pair it with a noise-amplification cap (sky rms of the output vs input) or prefer E1(d). For the NN path T is the relative kernel s_nn of Idea D1.
- Cost: S (one extra convolution per candidate; deconvolution of one half). Confidence: medium.

Idea E3 - Residual-whiteness / consistency report for every deconvolved output.
- With the relative kernel of Idea D1 and the measured noise map, report the chi-square of (y - s * x)/sigma on sky and near stars, plus a smoothed-residual map. Flux loss (structure in y that x lacks) and invented structure (in x but not in y) both show as coherent residual. This operationalises the project's existing faint-peak confirmation tests as a per-run self-check. Cost: S given D1.

Other honest-evaluation points: report width at equal noise by blurring the noisier image until the noise matches (already done in `bench_vs_siril.matched_noise`); report Gaia-pair resolved fraction as a function of separation in FWHM units (done); add FRC resolution vs noise as a two-axis front; always compare against the best available independent half rather than the full stack that shares the data. Avoid single-frame vs stack FWHM comparisons (the repo's own warning).

### Gaps
- Koho et al.'s exact formulas, thresholds and caveats could not be retrieved (full text blocked); the FRC-to-SSNR relation and the Wiener form here are my derivations and should be checked against van Heel & Schatz-type references before being cited in a report.
- No published astronomy-specific use of FRC to select a deconvolution strength was found in this pass.
- No quantification of how much a Noise2Noise-type network's hallucination inflates FRC on astronomical data was found; the caveat is reasoning, not evidence.

## Which ideas should be built first (ranked by benefit / effort)?

### Takeaway
Rank 1: the relative-kernel (target-PSF) deconvolver with a residual-consistency report (Ideas D1 + E3). Rank 2: the split-half FRC/SSNR toolkit (Idea E1, E2 as a secondary score), because it makes every other change measurable and automates strength selection. Rank 3: a measurement-first pass on spatial/patch seeing variation, leading to tile-aware proper coadd and a PSF field (Ideas S1 + P2, with P1/P3 as small prerequisites), which has the largest uncertain upside and the largest cost. Everything else on the list is lower priority or not recommended.

### Cited Findings
- Magain et al.: ringing-free two-channel/target-PSF deconvolution with photometry errors of 1% vs 7% for a two-channel Lucy on the same simulated data — [ar5iv astro-ph/9704059](https://ar5iv.arxiv.org/html/astro-ph/9704059)
- Koho et al.: FRC for denoising, blind deconvolution PSF estimation and monitoring iterative deconvolution — [Koho et al. 2019](https://research.utu.fi/converis/portal/detail/Publication/41357558?lang=en_US)
- Zackay & Ofek: per-image PSF matched filtering is optimal and PSF homogenisation before coaddition loses sensitivity (gain "between a few and 25 percent") — [arXiv:1512.06872](https://arxiv.org/abs/1512.06872)
- Repo: proper coadd gave 0.974-0.987 shared-star FWHM at no noise cost on three sessions, and turning patch weighting off earlier made noise worse (R 1.09 -> 1.15) — [dev-notes/module-notes.md](C:/source/originstack-research/dev-notes/module-notes.md), [dev-notes/siril-comparison.md](C:/source/originstack-research/dev-notes/siril-comparison.md)

### Inferences
1. Relative-kernel (target-PSF) deconvolution + consistency report (D1 + E3). Effort S-M (new function in `psf_deconvolution.py`, wiring as `--deconvolve rl` default behaviour or a new `--deconvolve-target-fwhm`, tests, add to `uncertainty._QUIET_OFF` if it writes a report file, CLAUDE.md mapping row). 2. Split-half FRC/SSNR toolkit (E1, E2): Effort S-M (two accumulator sets in proper coadd, ring binning, `tools/eval_frc.py`). Benefit: ground-truth-free, frequency-resolved comparison of any change in the project; parameter-free Wiener baseline and a principled iteration/cutoff choice. Confidence 0.7 for the metric, 0.5 for automatic strength selection.
3. Measure first, then tile-aware proper coadd + PSF field (S1 + P2, with P1 and P3): Effort S for the two gating measurements (quadrant FWHM scatter; `--aberration-report` cell map), then M-L. Benefit: the only item that can add information to the data entering Phase 4 (lucky-patch weighting that the default path discards); unknown amplitude on Origin optics (confidence 0.3-0.4 that the gate passes). Idea F1 (rotation smear) only after this and only on sessions where the computed smear exceeds 0.5 px.

Not recommended / low priority: further sub-pixel or Bayer super-resolution (Question 4 analysis), lucky imaging or turbulence-mitigation networks (Question 3), joint multi-frame deconvolution (equivalent to proper coadd + one deconvolution under Gaussian noise), diffusion posterior sampling and deep-image-prior methods (covered earlier, CPU-heavy), Gaia-anchored two-channel star model (close to a reverted item), Tikhonet/Deep-Wiener retraining (only for non-Origin support), plug-and-play refinement (experimental, no optical evidence).

### Gaps
- Benefit estimates for ranks 1 and 3 are design properties or gated hypotheses, not published numbers for this camera class; no source reports width-at-equal-noise gains for any of these methods on one-shot-colour CMOS stacks.
- No cost measurements were made (no code was run beyond arithmetic); timings quoted come from the repo notes.
- Primary full texts for Koho et al. 2019, Starck et al. 2002, PIFF and the lucky-imaging papers were not retrieved; their findings above are abstract- or summary-level and flagged accordingly.
