## 1. Colour science for astronomy: Lupton asinh and extensions, perceptual spaces (OKLab, CAM16, ICtCp, JzAzBz), gamut handling, star colour through stretches, noisy sky colour

### Takeaway
The project already implements the core of Lupton et al. 2004 (ratio-preserving asinh-style stretch with largest-channel rescale) and an OKLab lightness/chroma stretch, so the remaining colour-science gain is narrow: replace the "dim the pixel until it fits" gamut step with Ottosson's hue-constant chroma-compression gamut mapping (adaptive L0, alpha 0.05), and optionally use a closed-form hue-preserving path-to-white (Khronos PBR Neutral) or a Mantiuk saturation exponent as tunable alternatives. Star recolouring from Gaia is the most-requested idea but has already been tried in a different form and its Gaia-correlation metric becomes circular.

### Cited Findings
- Lupton et al. 2004 introduced the asinh stretch so faint objects show while bright structure is preserved, with the explicit goal that an object of a given astronomical colour keeps a distinct colour instead of a burnt-out white. — [arXiv:astro-ph/0312483](https://arxiv.org/abs/astro-ph/0312483)
- astropy ships this as `make_lupton_rgb` (default arcsinh; `stretch`, `Q`, black level via `interval`/`minimum`; layers outside 0-1 are clipped) and a newer `make_rgb` taking any `BaseStretch` and a per-filter interval array. — [astropy RGB docs](https://docs.astropy.org/en/stable/visualization/rgb.html)
- OriginStack's `colour_preserving_stretch` already scales each channel by T(n)/n and divides an over-range pixel by its largest channel (Lupton's rescale), with chroma faded in over the first 3 sky sigma; its docstring notes the cost of that rescale: a red star core displays at about half the brightness of a white one. `oklab_stretch` takes lightness from the curve, hue and C/L from the display pixel, compresses chroma toward white over a knee (`PREVIEW_OKLAB_KNEE` 0.75, `MAX_DESAT` 0.5) and "fits by dimming" whatever is still out of gamut. — [src/io_fits.py](file:///C:/source/originstack-research/src/io_fits.py)
- The default preview colour mode is `channel` (per-channel curve); `preserve` measured hue error 0.01-0.07 deg and star-colour correlation 0.80-0.98 against 3-7.5 deg and 0.51-0.91 for per-channel on the bench stacks, but the user prefers the channel look. — [dev-notes/phase4-quality.md](file:///C:/source/originstack-research/dev-notes/phase4-quality.md), [CLAUDE.md](file:///C:/source/originstack-research/CLAUDE.md)
- Ottosson's OKLab gamut-clipping write-up: all methods work in OKLab, keep hue constant and change only lightness and chroma, projecting each out-of-gamut colour along a straight line toward (L0, 0) on the neutral axis. Variants: preserve chroma (L0 = L1), L0 = 0.5, L0 = L_cusp, and "adaptive L0" which blends chroma compression (small alpha) with single-point projection (large alpha). Recommended: adaptive L0 with alpha = 0.05; L0 = 0.5 vs L_cusp "seems to matter very little visually" so 0.5 is preferred (cheaper). Equations given: L_d = L1 - 0.5; e1 = 1/2 + |L_d| + alpha*C1; L0 = (1 + sgn(L_d)*(e1 - sqrt(e1^2 - 2|L_d|)))/2. The line/gamut intersection is closed-form on the lower half and one or more Halley steps on the upper half (error < 1e-6 after one step except some blue hues). — [Ottosson, "sRGB gamut clipping"](https://bottosson.github.io/posts/gamutclipping/)
- Khronos PBR Neutral tone mapper: all changes happen in the plane spanned by the input colour and the white axis [1,1,1], so "there are no hue shifts"; constants F90 = 0.04, K_s = 0.8 - F90 (compression start), K_d = 0.15 (desaturation rate); steps: x = min(R,G,B); f = x - x^2/(4*F90) if x <= 2*F90 else F90; p = max(c - f); p_n = 1 - (1-K_s)^2/(p + 1 - 2*K_s); g = 1/(K_d*(p - p_n) + 1); output (c - f)*(p_n/p)*g + p_n*(1-g) if p > K_s else c - f. Derivatives are continuous and the map is analytically invertible. — [Khronos ToneMapping, PBR_Neutral](https://github.com/KhronosGroup/ToneMapping/blob/main/PBR_Neutral/README.md)
- Mantiuk et al. 2009 ("Color correction for tone mapping", Computer Graphics Forum 28(2)) measured how much saturation correction viewers need after contrast compression: the relation is non-linear and smaller contrast changes need smaller corrections. A secondary source writes their correction as C_out = ((C_in/L_in - 1)*s + 1)*L_out with s the saturation parameter (linear interpolation between the achromatic and chromatic colour; can shift hue in reds/blues). Luminance HDR / pfstmo expose it as "colour saturation" (1 keeps the appearance, >1 more, <1 less). — [Eurographics record](https://diglib.eg.org/handle/10.2312/CGF.v28i2pp193-202), [paper PDF](https://www.cl.cam.ac.uk/~rkm38/pdfs/mantiuk09cctm.pdf), [CEUR secondary source](https://ceur-ws.org/Vol-2485/paper13.pdf)
- Professional presentation images are built by intensity-scaling and colourising each dataset independently and layering them, with colour chosen for science content plus visual grammar. — [Rector et al. 2007, AJ 133, 598 (arXiv:astro-ph/0412138)](https://arxiv.org/abs/astro-ph/0412138)
- Per-star profile-matched recolouring (replace each channel's star excess with luminance profile x channel flux inside 2.5 FWHM) was tried and dropped: it helped Crab (-0.113 to -0.088) and Veil but lowered star-colour correlation with Gaia on Trifid (0.904 to 0.851) and Sunflower (0.877 to 0.859) because of structured nebulosity under large stars. The roadmap still lists "star recolouring / per-star colour correction (Gaia colour is known)" as open. — [dev-notes/phase4-quality.md](file:///C:/source/originstack-research/dev-notes/phase4-quality.md), [dev-notes/ROADMAP.md](file:///C:/source/originstack-research/dev-notes/ROADMAP.md)

### Inferences

**Idea 1.1 - Adaptive-L0 OKLab gamut compression for over-range pixels (replaces "divide by max channel / dim" in `oklab_stretch` and the preserve path)**
- What/why: The current fit keeps hue and saturation but sacrifices lightness (the repo itself documents red/blue cores at half the brightness of white ones), and the MAX_DESAT knee desaturates toward white by a fixed schedule independent of gamut shape. Ottosson's projection keeps the display lightness the curve asked for, keeps OKLab hue exactly, and gives up only the chroma that cannot exist at that lightness - so saturated star cores and bright Ha/OIII regions stay as bright as neutral ones and keep as much colour as sRGB can show.
- Algorithm (vectorised numpy): after computing the target (L, C, h) in `oklab_stretch` (L from the curve, C from sat*L*gate*desat): convert to linear sRGB; mask = any channel outside [0,1]. For masked pixels compute L0 by the hue-independent formula with alpha = 0.05: `Ld = L-0.5; e1 = 0.5 + abs(Ld) + alpha*C; L0 = 0.5*(1 + sign(Ld)*(e1 - sqrt(e1*e1 - 2*abs(Ld))))`; then find t in (0,1] such that (L0*(1-t)+L*t, t*C) is on the gamut boundary: vectorised bisection on t, 20 iterations, test = all three linear-sRGB channels in [0,1] (no cusp polynomial needed; ~20 matrix-vector passes over the masked pixels only). Output (L_t, C_t, h). Keep the existing "negative channel desaturate to grey" step for the sub-black case.
- Source: Ottosson (above). Alternative one-line version if cusp code is unwanted: preserve-chroma-only with L0 = clamp(L).
- Expected gain/evidence: small but directly on the user-visible "white/blown star core" and "dark red core" failure modes; no quantitative evidence beyond Ottosson's visual comparison (he states the comparison is not rigorous). Measure with the existing `bench_phase4` `*_lin` star-colour metrics and a new core-brightness-vs-colour check (core display luminance of red vs white stars of equal linear luminance should now match).
- Plug-in: `io_fits.oklab_stretch` (final gamut fit) and, optionally, the over-range branch of `colour_preserving_stretch`; no change to `channel` mode (its channels are clipped independently, no out-of-gamut notion). Preview-only; FITS stays linear.
- Cost/risk: ~60 lines, <1 s on 6.4 MP for the masked set; risk low, only affects pixels already out of gamut; opt-in stretches (oklab/preserve) only, so benefit is small while `channel` stays default. Confidence: medium (algorithm sound, visible gain modest).

**Idea 1.2 - Khronos PBR-Neutral-style path-to-white as the highlight operator (alternative to 1.1, tuning option)**
- What/why: a closed-form, C1-continuous, hue-preserving highlight compressor that desaturates toward white in proportion to how far the peak channel was compressed; removes the visible knee ring that a piecewise desaturation can leave and needs no OKLab round trip. Constants K_s, K_d are the only knobs.
- Algorithm: as listed in the Cited Findings; apply per pixel to the display-referred RGB produced by the luminance curve (replace the final `out / max(out.max, 1)` rescale). Choose K_s about 0.75-0.85 so the knee sits where the OKLab knee (0.75) is now.
- Source: Khronos README (above). Expected gain: marginal over 1.1; mainly simplicity. Plug-in: `colour_preserving_stretch` end. Cost: ~15 lines, risk low; designed for physically based material colours not astronomical objects (the dark-value offset `f` shifts black by up to F90 = 0.04 - skip that term or set F90 = 0 for sky-centred data). Confidence: low-medium that it beats 1.1.

**Idea 1.3 - Mantiuk saturation exponent for the preserve path**
- What/why: pure ratio preservation (s = 1) is a saturation-increasing choice after strong contrast compression; the channel look the user prefers is effectively s < 1 in the highlights. A per-pixel s tied to the local log-log slope of the tone curve (compression -> less correction) reproduces the channel look's highlight desaturation but keeps hue, as a controlled alternative to `--stretch-color channel`.
- Algorithm: c_out = (c_in/L_in - 1)*s + 1) * L_out on the ratio-preserved pixel, with s = clip(1 - k*(1 - slope_log), s_min, 1) where slope_log = (dT/dn)*(n/T). Constants k, s_min must be fitted on the star-colour bench (the paper's own fitted s(slope) function was not retrievable - see Gaps).
- Expected gain: uncertain; plug-in `colour_preserving_stretch`; cost ~20 lines; risk: hue shift in reds/blues per the secondary source. Confidence: low. Do only if a user wants channel-like highlights with intact hue.

**Do not do:** (a) per-star Gaia recolouring of the display - it was tried in a similar form, and any recolouring that uses Gaia colour as its target makes the bench's Gaia-correlation score circular (score it against held-out stars or against the linear-stack colour index instead); (b) CAM16 / JzAzBz / ICtCp as the stretch space - OKLab is already in place and I found no source showing a benefit of those spaces for astronomical images (not researched in depth; see Gaps), so I do not recommend the extra complexity.

### Gaps
- Lupton 2004 full-text details (the asinh softening rule relative to sky noise, exact saturated-pixel treatment, parameter recommendations) could not be read: the arXiv PDF came back as unparseable binary and the abstract page lacks the method. The project's docstring attributes the largest-channel rescale to the paper; I did not verify that independently.
- Mantiuk 2009's fitted function s(slope) and its constants could not be retrieved (PDF unparseable, project page unreachable); only the correction equation via a secondary source.
- No source found comparing CAM16/JzAzBz/ICtCp against OKLab for astronomical images (I did not search for them specifically); no astro-specific evidence for any gamut-mapping method.
- Ottosson's OKLab primary post itself was not fetched this session (only the gamut-clipping article); the project's OKLab matrices were not re-verified.

## 2. Spectrophotometric colour calibration: Gaia XP with the camera's response, correction matrices, dim-star noise, galaxy references, spatially varying correction

### Takeaway
The project's default `solar` fit and its blackbody x Gaussian `spcc` are weaker than PixInsight/Siril SPCC mainly because the Origin178's passbands are unknown ("no usable published QE curve"). The one thing that would close that gap without any published curve is to learn the camera's effective R/G/B stellar-continuum response once, from Gaia XP spectra and the project's own 28-33 calibrated sessions, and ship it in the camera profile; that unlocks true XP-based SPCC, a star-trained 3x3 colour matrix, and a diagnostic for the unexplained night-to-night G-R slope. It is the largest effort on this list and its measurable gain on the existing white-point metric is likely modest.

### Cited Findings
- PixInsight SPCC (Nov 2022) integrates each star's Gaia DR3 BP/RP spectrum (343 samples, 336-1020 nm at 2 nm) against the user's sensor/filter curves, fits a robust (repeated-median) regression of catalogue vs image flux ratios, and recommends an "ideal QE" option for OSC cameras; PixInsight reports ~400% / 300% lower R / B calibration uncertainty than the older PCC (vendor claim, not independently validated). Siril implemented its own SPCC from the Gaia `xp_sampled` product and ships an offline spectrophotometric catalogue. — [PixInsight SPCC docs](https://pixinsight.com/doc/docs/SPCC/SPCC.html), [Siril SPCC docs](https://siril.readthedocs.io/en/latest/processing/color-calibration/spcc.html), [earlier note](file:///C:/source/originstack-research/research_notes/Astrophotography%20stacking%20techniques%20since%202022/uncertainty_photometry_performance.md)
- Gaia XP spectra are flux-calibrated over 330-1050 nm; synthetic photometry in any passband inside that range reproduces top-quality photometry within a few per cent for wide and medium bands, and "up to millimag" after standardisation against external photometry. — [Montegriffo et al. 2023, arXiv:2206.06215](https://arxiv.org/abs/2206.06215)
- XP access: spectra are retrieved through DataLink after an ADQL query (5,000-source threshold per request); the continuous-coefficient table is `gaiadr3.xp_continuous_mean_spectrum` (queryable on the ARI-Gaia TAP service), and GAVO republishes all sampled spectra as a TAP-queryable table; GaiaXPy has a Generator subpackage for synthetic photometry. — [ESA Gaia DataLink help](https://www.cosmos.esa.int/web/gaia-users/archive/datalink-products), [GAVO blog, "Gaia DR3 XP Spectra: All Sampled"](https://blog.g-vo.org/gaia-dr3-xp-spectra-all-sampled.html), [GaiaXPy docs](https://gaiaxpy.readthedocs.io/en/latest/)
- BP-RP to Teff: Mucciarelli, Bellazzini & Massari 2021 give dwarf and giant colour-Teff relations from ~600 IRFM stars, 60-80 K dispersion for pure Gaia colours (dwarf (BP-RP)_0 fit covers 0.39-1.50). — [arXiv:2106.03882](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2106.03882)
- Stellar Locus Regression (High et al. 2009, AJ 138, 110) adjusts instrumental colours so stars sit on the universal stellar colour-colour locus, with no per-passband zero-points needed first, accounts for atmospheric extinction, reaches 5-14 mmag RMS colour residuals, and can run in real time at the telescope; a later survey paper notes it still needs at least one externally calibrated reference band for absolute zero-points. — [arXiv:0903.5302](https://www.arxiv.org/abs/0903.5302), [ADS](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2009AJ....138..110H/abstract), [J-PLUS, arXiv:1907.12939](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1907.12939)
- Project state: `spcc` = blackbody at Gaia `teff_gspphot` (most stars lack it, colour-index fallback) x Gaussian R/G/B responses (centres 620/540/460 nm, sigma 45 nm), then median(expected/measured) per channel, scales clamped to [0.5, 2]; `solar` (default) fits instrumental B/R and G/R vs BP-RP by Theil-Sen and sets BP-RP 0.82 white. — [src/color_calibrate.py](file:///C:/source/originstack-research/src/color_calibrate.py), [dev-notes/phase4-quality.md](file:///C:/source/originstack-research/dev-notes/phase4-quality.md)
- Camera facts measured by the project: no usable published QE curve; B-R slope median 0.828 (+/-0.06 between nights); G-R slope 0.27-0.77 and night-dependent "unexplained"; B-R scatter about the fit 0.05-0.10 mag on real fields; with only 6-14 stars a slope prior is used; a per-unit vignetting map gains 0-5%. — [dev-notes/camera-profile.md](file:///C:/source/originstack-research/dev-notes/camera-profile.md)
- The Crab fit failed once because 32 isolated stars, all red and spanning <1.5 mag, extrapolated a noisy slope to the solar point (0.35 vs 0.31 from ~1000 stars); the fix was a deeper query when the usable slice is thin, and a scatter > 0.2 mag declines. — [dev-notes/phase4-quality.md](file:///C:/source/originstack-research/dev-notes/phase4-quality.md)
- Gray-locus fallback (used when no catalogue): per-star fractional fluxes, sigma-clip on G/R and G/B, drive the median fractions to 1/3. — [src/photometric_calibration.py](file:///C:/source/originstack-research/src/photometric_calibration.py)
- Colour-calibration steps run on the linear stack before Phase 4 (`pipeline._colour_calibrate_stack`), per-channel scales keep it linear. — [dev-notes/phase4-quality.md](file:///C:/source/originstack-research/dev-notes/phase4-quality.md)

### Inferences

**Idea 2.1 - Learn the Origin178's effective R/G/B stellar-continuum passbands from Gaia XP spectra + the project's own stars; then run true XP-based SPCC (replaces blackbody x Gaussian)**
- What/why: SPCC's advantage is per-star spectral integration against real instrument curves. The project lacks the curves but has what is needed to infer them: ~33 sessions x tens-to-hundreds of aperture-measured Gaia stars. Once the curves ship in `src/data/camera_profiles/Origin178.json`, `fit_channel_scales_spcc` can use each star's XP spectrum (not a blackbody) and fit only the 2 free white gains (no slope), working with 6-14 stars without a prior, handling reddened/Galactic-plane fields (where the BP-RP-linear locus breaks), and giving a principled expected colour for every star.
- Algorithm: unknowns r_c(lambda) = sum_k theta_{c,k} phi_k(lambda) (cubic B-splines, ~14 knots 380-760 nm, theta >= 0, small second-difference penalty), nuisance per-session per-channel gain g_{s,c}. Model F_{s,i,c} = g_{s,c} * sum_k theta_{c,k} A_{i,k}, A_{i,k} = sum_j phi_k(l_j) S_i(l_j) l_j dl (photon weighting; S_i the XP sampled flux of star i). Alternate (a) NNLS for theta per channel (scipy.optimize.nnls on stacked sessions, weights 1/sigma^2 from aperture photometry) and (b) closed-form g_{s,c}; fix the gauge ||theta_G||_1 = 1. Use only the solar fit's star selection (isolated, unsaturated, SNR > 20, G < 17). Validate leave-one-session-out: predicted vs measured (R/G, B/G) rms, targeting < 0.03 mag vs the current 0.05-0.10 scatter. Persist ~3 x 80 numbers.
- Source: PixInsight/Siril SPCC (method), Montegriffo 2023 (XP synthetic photometry and its accuracy), GAVO/ESA (data access). The self-calibration of passbands from stars + spectra is my construction (no source found for exactly this on amateur data).
- Expected gain/evidence: no published number for OSC cameras; vendor-only claim for SPCC vs PCC. On the project's own metric (G2V display colour index within 0.03 after `solar`) the average white point will barely move; gains are expected for reddened fields, for stars far from solar colour (curvature), and as a diagnostic for what the G-R slope "should" be (separating spectral effects from pedestal/dark errors; the repo found ISO-mismatched darks with colour-dependent offsets).
- Plug-in: Phase 2.5 colour calibration, `color_calibrate.fit_channel_scales_spcc` + `net_query` (new XP fetch, cached like `gaia_cache`); a one-off tool `tools/learn_camera_passbands.py` beside `tools/measure_camera_profile.py`. Needs training data (the local sessions + one-time network); runtime needs a TAP/DataLink fetch of XP rows (or a compact cache).
- Cost/risk: L (about a week). Key risk: stellar spectra vary in only ~3-4 independent directions, so the fit is determined only on the span of stellar spectra - the learned curves are valid for stellar continua and NOT for emission lines (so they cannot predict Ha/OIII leakage; see section 4). The fit also absorbs any flat-field or pedestal colour error as if it were passband shape. Confidence: medium that it is technically sound, low-medium that it improves the existing white-point metric.

**Idea 2.2 - Camera-wide 3x3 colour-correction matrix trained on stars (exposure-invariant), applied to denoised chroma**
- What/why: per-channel gains (3 numbers, 2 after normalisation) cannot change the star-locus slopes (B-R 0.83, G-R 0.4 median) - only a channel-mixing matrix can. Bayer filter overlap means camera RGB is not display RGB; a CCM trained on stars (the colour-chart patches of astronomy) moves star hues and extended-object colours toward the standard rendering.
- Algorithm: chromaticity form, c_i = F_i/sum(F_i) (after diagonal calibration), target t_i = expected linear-sRGB chromaticity of star i from its XP spectrum (CIE 1931 2-degree XYZ integration, Bradford adaptation so the BP-RP 0.82 solar star is neutral) or, offline, a blackbody at Teff from the Mucciarelli relation. Solve M = argmin sum w_i ||M c_i - t_i||^2 + lambda ||M - I||^2 subject to M*1 = 1 (white preserved): a 6-parameter linear least-squares with a closed-form KKT solution; choose lambda and accept/reject by leave-one-session-out held-out Delta-E. Ship per camera in the profile (pooled over sessions; the per-night instability of G-R argues against per-session fitting). Reject if noise gain max_row ||m_r||_2 > 1.5.
- Source: standard camera-colorimetry practice (not sourced here), XP data from Gaia, targets as in Montegriffo 2023 synthetic photometry.
- Expected gain: improves colour separation of stars and of continuum-dominated objects; for emission nebulae a stellar-trained matrix is not validated. No quantified evidence found. Noise: negative off-diagonals amplify chroma noise (my guess is x1.2-1.5 for a Bayer sensor; not measured) - apply after the denoiser on chroma only; that means a new resumable Phase 4 step (`_at`, `SNAP_STEPS`, `PHASE4_TOGGLES`, desktop label per CLAUDE.md) or applying before Phase 4 and accepting the noise.
- Cost: M-L, depends on 2.1's XP data path. Confidence: low-medium.

**Idea 2.3 - Stellar-locus regression as the offline / no-WCS colour fallback (replace gray-locus)**
- What/why: when `--offline`, plate solving or the Gaia query fails, the pipeline falls back to a median-star-is-neutral rule, which is biased by the field's stellar population (e.g. reddened Galactic fields). SLR instead uses the whole shape of the stellar locus and needs no catalogue match.
- Algorithm: build a camera template locus T(s) from pooled calibrated Origin178 stars (median of (log R/G, log B/G) in BP-RP bins, stored as a polyline). For a new stack: x_i = (log R/G, log B/G) for isolated, unsaturated, SNR > 20 stars from the existing star detector; fit offsets delta (2 parameters, optionally a colour-term scale) minimising sum rho(dist(x_i - delta, T)) with Huber/Tukey loss, alternating nearest-segment projection and robust mean; scales = exp(-delta), anchored by "BP-RP 0.82 neutral" through the template.
- Source: High et al. 2009 (SLR; 5-14 mmag RMS on survey data). Gain only offline/unsolved sessions; confidence medium; cost S-M; plug-in `photometric_calibration._gray_locus_calibration` replacement.

**Idea 2.4 - Smooth spatial zero-point map per channel from Gaia stars (low priority)**
- What/why: unlike the dropped ubercal flat (self-calibration needs field rotation), Gaia gives the true fluxes, so a low-order 2D polynomial of log(F_c,measured / F_c,expected) vs (x, y) is directly observable and could remove residual coloured vignetting that flats leave.
- Algorithm: after the white-point fit, regress the per-channel log residuals of >= 100 stars on a degree-2 polynomial in normalised (x, y) (robust, penalised), apply as a multiplicative map. Expected gain: the project's own measurements bound the effect at 0-5% (vignette map), so likely below visibility except on odd sessions (Pinwheel R-G 1.5-2.8%). Confidence: low; cost S; plug-in `color_calibrate` after the scale fit. I found no source on angle-of-incidence bandpass shift for fast f/2.2 optics and did not verify it.

**Handling dim-star noise (small, cheap):** the Theil-Sen fit has no x-error model; at G > 17 BP-RP noise reaches 0.1-0.2 mag. Weighting by 1/(sigma_y^2 + slope^2 sigma_x^2) (orthogonal / errors-in-variables) is a one-day change. No source retrieved for gain; confidence low-medium.

**Galaxy/nebula reference white:** PixInsight offers galaxy white references; for a galaxy target using an average-spiral-galaxy spectrum as the neutral reference would shift the white point by a few per cent versus solar. This is a style choice not an accuracy gain; I did not retrieve a source for the reference spectra. Skip.

### Gaps
- No independent validation of SPCC's accuracy (only PixInsight's own 400%/300% claim); no published OSC-specific numbers for XP-based calibration versus linear colour-index fits.
- No source for the Sony IMX178 / Origin178 spectral response (the project says none is published); I did not search the Sony datasheet, so a vendor relative-response curve might exist in a form not considered by the project.
- Whether `bp_coefficients`-type array columns can be pulled in a normal ADQL query was not confirmed (the sources say continuous coefficients are in `xp_continuous_mean_spectrum` on the ARI TAP and sampled spectra in GAVO's table; the exact column names were not retrieved).
- No source for CCM-from-stars in astrophotography, for root-polynomial/exposure-invariant fits, or for angle-of-incidence bandpass shift; these rest on my reasoning.

## 3. Tone mapping and local contrast beyond the existing set: HDR compression of linear data, edge-aware base/detail, histogram specification, noise-aware stretch, output quantisation and HDR output

### Takeaway
The project has global curves, a roll-off for extended highlights, a feather for clipped plateaus, CLAHE, Mertens fusion and a cored multiscale local contrast - but no true dynamic-range compression of the bright base layer (Durand-Dorsey / HDRMT style), which is what M31's core and the bright parts of M42/M8/M17 need to show detail without lifting the white point. A guided-filter base/detail compressor gated by the existing `_extended_highlight_top` detector is the best fit. Two cheap output-side fixes (dithered 8-bit quantisation, HDR-tagged PNG) round it out.

### Cited Findings
- Durand & Dorsey (SIGGRAPH 2002): base = bilateral filter of log10 intensity, detail = log intensity - base; only the base is compressed by a factor = target contrast / (max(log base) - min(log base)) (their FAQ suggests log(5) as target contrast); colour is kept by working on channel/intensity ratios and recomposing with the new intensity; a piecewise-linear approximation plus subsampling made the bilateral filter about two orders of magnitude faster; "no parameter setting" required. — [Durand & Dorsey 2002](https://people.csail.mit.edu/fredo/PUBLI/Siggraph2002/)
- Guided image filtering (He, Sun, Tang, TPAMI 2013, ECCV 2010): an explicit edge-preserving filter from a local linear model, non-approximate linear-time algorithm independent of kernel size and intensity range, no gradient-reversal artifacts (unlike the bilateral filter); applications include detail enhancement and HDR compression. — [paper PDF](https://people.csail.mit.edu/kaiming/publications/pami12guidedfilter.pdf) (via search record, not opened in full)
- PixInsight's HDR wavelet transform (practitioner standard for M101/M42/M31 cores) is an a-trous wavelet method that isolates structures per layer so local contrast of a layer is not disturbed by larger structures; the documented M101 recipe starts from a non-linear image, uses 6 layers (1-32 px), 5x5 B3-spline scaling function, 4 iterations, and a luminance mask to focus on bright areas and limit ringing; a second pass on the first 3 layers (1 iteration, mask limited to nearly saturated features) flattens small-scale contrast but leaves a "donut" artefact around a bright star unless an extra mask is used; a curves step follows. — [PixInsight HDRWT example](https://pixinsight.com/examples/HDRWT/M101/index.html)
- Project state: extended highlights get a global roll-off (`_extended_highlight_top`, `_rolloff_curve`) and the clipped plateau outline is feathered; Orion's Trapezium region clips in every 20 s sub (~15k px plateau), so the plateau itself cannot be recovered; `multiscale_local_contrast` cores each detail layer against its own sky sigma (no boost under 1 sigma, full from 3), caps highlights at the 99th percentile of above-sky pixels, and has a fix for moats round saturated stars. — [src/io_fits.py](file:///C:/source/originstack-research/src/io_fits.py), [dev-notes/phase4-quality.md](file:///C:/source/originstack-research/dev-notes/phase4-quality.md)
- Display sky noise of the default `ghs` preview on the bench stacks is 0.74-2.34 (8-bit units) (Sculptor 0.74, Whirlpool 0.78, Crab 0.76, M24 0.94, Trifid 2.34); smooth, denoised regions inside an object have far less. — [dev-notes/phase4-quality.md](file:///C:/source/originstack-research/dev-notes/phase4-quality.md)
- `to_uint8` rounds (`rint`) with no dither; the JPEG path uses it; `write_png16` is a hand-written PNG writer that emits only IHDR/IDAT/IEND (no colour-space chunk). — [src/io_fits.py](file:///C:/source/originstack-research/src/io_fits.py)
- Dither theory: Lipshitz, Wannamaker, Vanderkooy (JAES 1992, "Quantization and Dither: A Theoretical Survey") assess dither signals for multibit quantisation; triangular-PDF dither avoids the noise modulation that Gaussian dither shows; triangular noise is the sum of two independent rectangular sources. — [Lipshitz 1992 PDF](https://hajim.rochester.edu/ece/sites/zduan/teaching/ece472/reading/Lipshitz_1992.pdf), [Wikipedia: Dither](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dither)
- PNG Third Edition adds the `cICP` chunk for HDR signalling; BT.2100 PQ images are flagged with code points 9, 16, 0, 1 (optional mastering-display and content-light-level metadata); Blink-based browsers implement it, libpng >= 1.6.45 reads/writes it; Safari/Firefox support is unclear in the sources. — [W3C Note: BT.2100 PQ in PNG](https://www.w3.org/TR/png-hdr-pq), [PNG 3e implementation report](https://w3c.github.io/png/Implementation_Report_3e/index.html)

### Inferences

**Idea 3.1 - Edge-aware log-domain base/detail HDR compression of the bright base ("hdr_compress"), gated by the existing extended-highlight detector**
- What/why: GHS + roll-off compresses highlights with one global curve, so a bright but unclipped core (M31 bulge, M42 wings, M8, M17, M27 shell) either sits above the white point and is rolled off flat, or pushes the white point up and buries the faint disk. Compressing only the large-scale base of the luminance, and leaving the detail layer intact, shows internal structure of the core while the stretch keeps the faint disk visible. Complementary to `multiscale_local_contrast` (which boosts mid-scale detail but does not reduce the base range) and to the roll-off (a curve, not local).
- Algorithm (preview domain, before `_sky_stats/_preview_white` so the white point is set after compression):
  1. L = luminance(rgb) (sky-centred); sky, sigma from `_sky_stats`; Ls = max(L - sky, 0) + k*sigma (k ~ 3, a soft floor like the asinh softening, so log never sees noise).
  2. logL = ln(Ls). Star exclusion: dilate the existing star mask (~1.5 FWHM) and fill logL inside it from its surroundings by normalised convolution (masked Gaussian), so stars stay in the detail layer (the HDRWT "donut" problem).
  3. base = guided_filter(guide = logL_filled, src = logL_filled, radius r = ~2% of min(H,W) (about 40 px on 2080 rows), eps = 0.15^2 in log units) via box filters (`scipy.ndimage.uniform_filter` or the native filters module): a = cov(I,p)/(var(I)+eps), b = mean(p) - a*mean(I), q = mean_a*I + mean_b. detail = logL - base.
  4. Knee b0 = base at the 90th percentile of above-sky pixels, b_max = base 99.9th percentile. c = clip(ln(R_target)/(b_max - b0), 0.3, 1) with R_target ~ 8-20. base' = b0 + c*(base - b0) for base > b0, unchanged below; detail gain g_d in [1, 1.3].
  5. Lc = exp(base' + g_d*detail) - k*sigma + sky. Recompose RGB with the repo's rule (fade to an additive change near zero: `f = smoothstep((L - 3 sigma)/...)`; `rgb' = f*rgb*Lc/L + (1-f)*(rgb + Lc - L)`) - never plain `rgb*Lc/L` on sky-centred data (CLAUDE.md, the Orion 1e18 bug).
  6. Run the normal stretch on rgb'. Gate: enable only when `_extended_highlight_top(...)` is not None (an extended region above the p99.5 white exists) and let strength scale with that region's size; off for `black_sigma > 0` presets unless the user asks.
- Sources: Durand & Dorsey 2002 (decomposition, compression factor, colour handling), He et al. 2013 (O(N) edge-preserving base without gradient reversal), PixInsight HDRWT (practitioner precedent: luminance mask, bright regions only, non-linear input, star "donut" artefact).
- Expected gain/evidence: this is the standard classical solution to exactly the M31/M42-core problem, and the practitioner tool for it is widely used; I found no quantitative astro benchmark. Measure on the existing bench: structure per unit sky noise inside the above-white region (should rise), ring/moat metric around stars (must not fall), faint-signal CNR (must not fall), plus an eye check on Andromeda, Orion, Omega, Lagoon. Limit: cannot recover clipped plateaus (Orion's Trapezium).
- Plug-in: preview only, `io_fits.render_preview_float` before the stretch branches (FITS stays linear, no resumable-step plumbing); optional later promotion to a Phase 4 toggle if the NN deconvolver should see the compressed image (it should not: it expects linear input).
- Cost/risk: M (about 4 days with tests); 0.3-0.6 s per 6.4 MP frame in numpy. Risks: "HDR look" at c < 0.5, halos at high-contrast edges if eps too large, interplay with `_feather_clipped_core` and the roll-off thresholds (retune `PREVIEW_ROLLOFF_*`). Confidence: medium.

**Idea 3.2 - Triangular-PDF dither in the 8-bit quantiser (JPEG/preview export only)**
- What/why: after the NN deconvolver/denoiser the interior of galaxies and nebulae can be smooth enough that a stretch with gain in the shadows turns slow gradients into visible 1-LSB contour bands; where display noise is >= ~0.7 LSB (the bench sky) it self-dithers, which is why this is a targeted, not universal, fix.
- Algorithm: `v = x*255; n = rng.random(v.shape) - rng.random(v.shape)` (TPDF, zero-mean, +/-1 LSB; `rng = np.random.default_rng(fixed_seed)` so reruns stay bit-identical, as the repo requires); `out = clip(rint(v + n), 0, 255)`; skip pixels with v < 0.5 to avoid lifting pure black. Add as `to_uint8(x, dither=False)` and enable only in the file writers (`write_preview_jpeg`, `write_final_images`), leaving `bench_phase4` and tests that compare `to_uint8` exactly untouched. Test: synthetic ramp with slope 0.2 LSB/px; measure contour regularity (std of second difference of 16-px block means) with and without.
- Source: Lipshitz et al. 1992 (TPDF avoids noise modulation). Expected gain: small (banding only in smooth regions), effort tiny, risk ~0 (JPEG q95 4:4:4 keeps +/-1 LSB noise mostly intact - not measured). Confidence: medium that a visible effect exists on NN-smoothed outputs; high that it is harmless. Plug-in: `io_fits.to_uint8` + writers.

**Idea 3.3 - HDR-tagged 16-bit PNG export (BT.2100 PQ via `cICP`)**
- What/why: on HDR displays a star core or the Trapezium can be shown above SDR white instead of being rolled off. A tagged PNG carries the extra range in an existing 16-bit export path.
- Algorithm: extend `write_png16(..., hdr_pq=True)`: map display-referred linear light with SDR white at ~203 nits (reference white placement is my assumption, not sourced) and highlights up to ~1000-4000 nits using the pre-roll-off luminance; convert linear-sRGB primaries to BT.2020, apply the PQ inverse EOTF, write a `cICP` chunk (9, 16, 0, 1) before IDAT. The existing writer already builds chunks by hand, so this is ~40 lines. Keep the SDR JPEG/PNG as default; opt-in `--export png16-hdr`.
- Source: W3C PNG HDR PQ Note, PNG 3e implementation report. Expected gain: only for viewers with HDR + Chromium; most web hosts re-encode. Confidence: medium on feasibility, low on user benefit. Cost: S.

**Not recommended / already covered:** noise-aware stretch strength - `noisefloor` solves GHS b from sky noise and the `masked`/`multiscale` weights already blend by signal over sky sigma; Anscombe/variance-stabilising stretch is what asinh with the sky-noise softening already approximates (Lupton). Histogram specification is what CLAHE and the seti median-targeting stretch already do. Mertens fusion exists.

### Gaps
- Local Laplacian filters (Paris et al. 2011; fast version Aubry et al. 2014) and Fattal gradient-domain compression were not fetched; I cannot cite equations or timings for them. They are heavier alternatives to 3.1 (more pyramid levels, per-level remapping) and would be the next step if the guided-filter base shows halos.
- HDRMT's exact compression formula could not be found (the PixInsight page for the tool 404'd; only the HDRWT example and a magazine description were read).
- The guided-filter paper itself and kaiminghe.com pages were not readable; claims rest on the search record of the TPAMI abstract.
- No measurement of 8-bit banding on actual OriginStack outputs; the dither benefit is inferred from the documented display sky noise and the rounding quantiser.
- No source on Ultra HDR / ISO 21496-1 gain-map JPEG; only PNG cICP was researched.

## 4. Dual- and tri-band narrowband from OSC cameras (L-eXtreme / L-eNhance style): Ha/OIII extraction, palettes, star colour, channel mixing

### Takeaway
Siril's CFA-level extraction (Ha from the red photosites only, OIII from green+blue) is the documented way to avoid demosaic cross-contamination, and practitioners add per-band leakage modelling (DBXtract) and HOO/synthetic palettes. OriginStack handles Origin "Nebula"-filter sessions only by switching SCNR and gray-locus off, so it never separates the lines; whether that is worth building depends on what the Origin "Nebula" slot actually passes (the repo's own evidence from one Lagoon session suggests an OIII-dominant passband).

### Cited Findings
- Siril `extract_HaOIII` / GUI extraction: Ha comes from the red photosites only ("only the R pixels are sensitive to Ha"), so the Ha image is half the dimensions; OIII from the remaining G and B photosites with green and blue scaled to equal averages and bilinear interpolation at the R sites; resample options: none (full-res OIII + half-res Ha), upsample Ha x2, or downsample OIII x2; for sequences use `seqextract_HaOiii`, stack OIII normally and Ha with a 2x drizzle. — [Siril extraction docs](https://siril.readthedocs.io/en/latest/processing/extraction.html), [pixls.us thread (user got half-size output, `-resample` workaround)](https://discuss.pixls.us/t/extract-ha-oiii/39700)
- Practitioner workflows: keep R as Ha and take the better of G or B (or max(G,B), or a mix) as OIII; HOO palette = Ha to R, OIII to G and B; a PixInsight script (Dual Band eXtract) "accounts for each band's contribution to the RGB channels" and can make a HOO palette with a synthetic green of 30% Ha / 70% OIII; cross-contamination between channels is unavoidable with OSC dual-band data and extraction equations depend on the specific camera and filter. — [AstroBin: SHO from OSC dual band](https://app2.astrobin.com/forum/topic/10124/sho-hubble-palette-from-osc-dual-band-data), [AstroBin: processing method](https://www.astrobin.com/forum/post/156013/), [Sky at Night: colourise mono narrowband](https://www.skyatnightmagazine.com/astrophotography/colourise-monochrome-narrowband-astrophotos), [AstroBin: DBXtract](https://ssr.app.astrobin.com/forum/topic/120190/pleiades-astrophoto-pixinsight/new-script-dbxtract), [AstroBin: what to do with H-II data](https://ssr.app.astrobin.com/forum/topic/137713/what-should-i-do-with-my-h-ii-data)
- Project state: `--auto` detects FITS `FILTER == 'Nebula'` and turns SCNR and the gray locus off (an OIII-filter Lagoon had raw R/G 0.04 vs 0.63 unfiltered; SCNR removed most of the nebula); Gaia calibration declines on its own; the pipeline otherwise debayers (RCD) these frames as ordinary RGB. — [src/auto_settings.py](file:///C:/source/originstack-research/src/auto_settings.py), [src/frame_discovery.py](file:///C:/source/originstack-research/src/frame_discovery.py)
- `combine` (mono channel files): HOO/SHO palettes, `scnr_green` average-neutral only, `subtract_continuum` with `optimal_continuum_scale`, `fix_narrowband_stars` (desaturate or transplant chrominance from a broadband star image). Roadmap lists "narrowband workflow end to end" as needing data. — [src/channel_combine.py](file:///C:/source/originstack-research/src/channel_combine.py), [dev-notes/ROADMAP.md](file:///C:/source/originstack-research/dev-notes/ROADMAP.md)
- `cfa_drizzle.py` exists (opt-in; "not recommended for Origin data" for broadband because of wider/noisier stars). — [CLAUDE.md](file:///C:/source/originstack-research/CLAUDE.md)

### Inferences

**Idea 4.1 - CFA-level Ha/OIII extraction as a Phase 1 mode for narrowband-filter sessions, with the palette applied in Phase 4**
- What/why: RCD (and any green-guided demosaic) assumes R, G, B are correlated; in a dual-band frame the red photosites carry Ha and the G/B photosites carry OIII, so the debayered "R" plane is polluted by OIII-derived interpolation and vice versa. Extracting before debayer gives clean line images; the palette (HOO, synthetic SHO-like) is then a Phase 4 display mapping instead of whatever the Bayer filters happen to produce.
- Algorithm: after dark/flat calibration (per-CFA-plane, already available), per raw sub: Ha_raw = R sites (H/2 x W/2); OIII = weighted sum of G sites (two per quad, averaged) and B sites with weights w_c proportional to a_c/sigma_c^2 (a_c = the filter's OIII response in channel c, estimated from the data as the median G/B ratio over bright OIII pixels; Siril uses equal-mean scaling) then bilinear-interpolated at R sites. Store as a 3-plane frame [Ha, OIII_from_G, OIII_from_B] so registration/stacking/Phase 4 code paths are untouched; reuse `cfa_drizzle` plane splatting for Ha across the dithered, field-rotating subs (alt-az rotation supplies the sub-pixel diversity a 25%-sampled plane needs - Siril's recommendation to drizzle Ha 2x), unlike broadband where stars suffered. Phase 4 palette step (toggle): R = Ha, G = a*Ha + (1-a)*OIII (a ~ 0.3 as the DBXtract default), B = OIII; star handling via `fix_narrowband_stars`.
- Source: Siril docs; AstroBin/DBXtract practice. Expected gain: cleaner line separation and palette control; no SNR gain (the same photosites feed both paths). No quantitative evidence found.
- Plug-in: `frame_processor` debayer stage (new `--debayer-method dualband`, auto-selected by `is_nebula_filter` only if the session's R/G shows Ha present), `postprocess` palette toggle, `stack_fingerprint` P1 attributes. Cost: M (about 5 days). Risk: the Origin "Nebula" filter may be OIII-only, making Ha extraction moot; two-band frames break the gray-world/Gaia assumptions (already handled by skipping them). Confidence: medium on technique, low on user value until the filter's passbands are confirmed.

**Idea 4.2 - Per-band leakage unmixing (RGB = A * [Ha, OIII])**
- What/why: each line leaks into the "wrong" Bayer channel to some degree (AstroBin sources say contamination is unavoidable; I found no measured fractions), so extracted Ha/OIII are mixed; solving the 3x2 system per pixel removes the leakage. This is the "accounts for each band's contribution" step of DBXtract made explicit.
- Algorithm: per-pixel NNLS of [R,G,B] = A [Ha,OIII] (+ continuum column for broadband fields), A estimated blind from the image: take pixel clusters at the extremes of the (G+B)/R ratio histogram (vertex-component-analysis style), or from the stack itself with the existing NMF code in `source_separation.py`. Do NOT estimate A from the star-learned passbands of Idea 2.1: those only span stellar continua and say nothing about emission-line ratios.
- Expected gain: hypothesis, no source evidence beyond practitioner reports; confidence low; cost M.

**Idea 4.3 - Star colour for narrowband OSC frames from Gaia (low priority)**
- Today `fix_narrowband_stars` desaturates or needs a broadband image. With Gaia matches (already queried for plate solving) star colour could be synthesised from BP-RP via the project's own calibrated locus and applied to the star footprints in the linear domain before the stretch. Same circularity and "tried and dropped" caveats as section 1; effort L; skip unless narrowband becomes a focus.

### Gaps
- What the Origin "Nebula" filter transmits (Ha? OIII only? Hbeta?) is not documented in anything I read; the repo comment calls it "an OIII, dual-band or other narrowband filter".
- No transmission or sensor-response data for L-eXtreme / L-eNhance / Ultra against Bayer channels, and no measured Ha/OIII leakage fractions were found (AstroBin posts say they depend on camera and filter).
- No peer-reviewed or quantitative comparison of CFA-level extraction vs debayer-then-split; Siril's docs and AstroBin practice are the only sources.
- The AstroBin pages are forum posts (practitioner opinion), several read only through search snippets.

## 5. Luminance/chrominance split strategies: synthetic luminance, LRGB chroma transfer, chroma-only denoising, chroma resolution on a Bayer sensor

### Takeaway
Chroma smoothing exists but is an isotropic masked Gaussian that is skipped entirely under the default NN path; the main open options are a luminance-guided, SNR-adaptive chroma filter (matters for non-NN / non-Origin data) and, now that the NN denoiser makes chroma clean, an optional chroma-contrast step. A noise-weighted detection luminance is a plausible but unproven refinement for masks.

### Cited Findings
- `reduce_chroma_noise`: sigma-clipped sky statistics, soft sky mask (or a real star mask), masked Gaussian blur of chroma normalised by the blurred mask, original chroma restored on stars/bright pixels; optional second large sigma; it runs as Phase 4 step 4 (before the denoisers) and is skipped under `--denoiser nn` (the networks "were trained on stacks without it") unless `nn_chroma_nr` is set. — [src/denoising.py](file:///C:/source/originstack-research/src/denoising.py), [src/postprocess.py](file:///C:/source/originstack-research/src/postprocess.py), [CLAUDE.md](file:///C:/source/originstack-research/CLAUDE.md)
- `luminance()` uses fixed 0.299/0.587/0.114 weights everywhere (stretch, masks, noise statistics). — [src/debayer.py](file:///C:/source/originstack-research/src/debayer.py)
- Bench shows display chroma blotch values of 3.7-8.4 (Trifid, Horsehead) and large-scale chroma mottle reductions of 26-66% (Trifid 11%) from the colour-preserving stretch; chroma fades in over the first 3 sky sigma above black. — [dev-notes/phase4-quality.md](file:///C:/source/originstack-research/dev-notes/phase4-quality.md)
- Guided filter (He et al. 2013): output is a local linear transform of a guidance image, so structure in the guide is transferred to the output while the rest is smoothed; linear-time at any radius. — [paper PDF](https://people.csail.mit.edu/kaiming/publications/pami12guidedfilter.pdf)
- Decorrelation stretch (Gillespie, Kahle, Walker 1986, Remote Sensing of Environment 20): principal-component rotation of the bands, independent contrast stretch of each decorrelated band, inverse rotation; designed for highly correlated bands; not every image improves; the amateur-facing derivative is DStretch (rock art). No astro application was found. — [NASA NTRS record (Gillespie 1992 review)](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/citations/19930030923), [CATALYST decorrelation stretch reference](https://catalyst.earth/catalyst-system-files/help/references/pciFunction_r/python/P_decorr.html), [DStretch overview](https://www.diyphotography.net/?p=343130)
- Szalay, Connolly & Szokoly 1999: a chi-squared detection image (sum of squared noise-normalised band values; chi-squared with N degrees of freedom for sky-only pixels) finds faint objects with unusual colours that a single linear co-add misses. — [arXiv:astro-ph/9811086](https://arxiv.org/pdf/astro-ph/9811086)

### Inferences

**Idea 5.1 - Luminance-guided, SNR-adaptive chroma denoising (guided filter in an opponent space)**
- What/why: an isotropic Gaussian at sigma 2 px smooths chroma across luminance edges (colour bleeding at nebula/sky or arm/disk boundaries) and cannot reach large radii without bleeding; a guided filter with luminance as guide lets chroma be smoothed with a large radius where the guide is flat and tracks edges where it is not.
- Algorithm: chroma planes c1 = R - Y, c2 = B - Y (the repo's chroma definition; sky-centred, signed). Guide = Gaussian(Y, 1.5 px). For radii r_small = 4 and r_large = 24, p -> guided_filter(guide, p, r, eps = (lambda*sigma_Y)^2, lambda 1-2) via box filters; blend r_large over r_small by 1/SNR_local where SNR_local = max(Y - sky,0)/sigma_Y smoothed. Apply only where the existing sky/star masks allow (keep the star protection), restore original chroma elsewhere. Add a luminance-independent edge cue (max of |grad c1|, |grad c2| of a heavily blurred chroma) to the guide variance so equal-luminance colour edges (Ha vs OIII) survive.
- Source: He et al. 2013. Expected gain: lower chroma mottle with fewer colour halos; no astro evidence found; measure with the bench's mottle/blotch and hue-error metrics. Important scope limit: Origin data with `--auto` runs the NN path, which skips classical chroma NR, so the benefit lands on the wavelet-denoiser path (Seestar/DSLR/other cameras, NN guard fallbacks).
- Plug-in: `denoising.reduce_chroma_noise` (add `method='guided'`), Phase 4 step 4 (existing `chroma_nr` toggle and snapshot plumbing apply). Cost: S-M (2 days); native optional. Confidence: medium that it helps, low that it is visible on NN output.

**Idea 5.2 - Optional chroma-contrast step after the denoiser: decorrelation stretch restricted to the OKLab (a,b) plane**
- What/why: faint real colour differences (blue arms vs yellow bulge in galaxies; Ha vs OIII regions) are low-contrast and highly correlated across channels; a global saturation boost over-saturates the dominant hue and amplifies noise, while a decorrelation stretch enlarges the minor axis of the colour distribution. With the NN denoiser the chroma is clean enough to afford it.
- Algorithm: from SNR-gated pixels (Y > 5 sigma, star-masked) compute the 2x2 covariance of (a,b) of the denoised display image; eigendecompose; per-axis gains g_i = min(g_max, s_target/s_i) with g_max about 1.5-2 on the minor axis and 1 on the major; (a',b') = V diag(g) V^T (a,b), faded by the existing chroma gate. Opt-in `--chroma-contrast`, default off (it is an enhancement, not an accuracy step).
- Source: Gillespie et al. 1986 (remote sensing). Expected gain: visible colour richness on galaxies and mixed nebulae; no astro evidence; risk: false colour, exaggeration. Plug-in: new resumable Phase 4 step after the denoiser (needs `_at`, `SNAP_STEPS`, `PHASE4_TOGGLES`, `_PP_STEP_LABELS`, translations). Cost: S-M. Confidence: low-medium.

**Idea 5.3 - Noise-weighted (chi-squared / matched-colour) detection luminance for masks and structure detection only**
- What/why: Rec.601 weights ignore that OSC channels have different noise and that emission nebulae live mostly in R; for detecting faint structure (DBE exclusion masks, protect masks, `_halpha_excess_mask`) the optimal combination weights each sky-subtracted channel by its signal/noise^2 (matched filter) or uses a chi-squared image.
- Algorithm: sky-subtract each channel; sigma_c from the robust sky noise; chi2 = sum_c ((I_c - sky_c)/sigma_c)^2 smoothed 2 px, or matched L_m = sum_c w_c (I_c - sky_c) with w_c = s_c/sigma_c^2 and s the target colour estimated from the brightest extended pixels. Use for masks; keep Rec.601 for the display luminance.
- Source: Szalay et al. 1999. Expected gain: uncertain (mask quality on faint emission); no evidence on amateur data; cost S; confidence low.

**LRGB on a Bayer sensor:** an L layer derived from the same photons gives no SNR advantage; the useful LRGB principle on OSC is chroma at lower resolution (what 5.1 does) with the luminance carrying the detail; `lrgb_combine` already exists for real mono L data.

### Gaps
- No astro-specific evidence for guided-filter chroma denoising, decorrelation stretch, or chi-squared luminance for display; these are transfers from other fields.
- The guided-filter primary page (kaiminghe.com) returned 404; only the TPAMI listing via search was used.
- Joint bilateral upsampling (Kopf 2007) and Bayer chroma-resolution literature were not researched.

## 6. Objective ways to measure colour accuracy and tonal quality

### Takeaway
`tools/bench_phase4.py` already scores star colour by rank correlation, Theil-Sen slope, hue error and Gaia G2V display colour, plus CNR, ring, mottle and flatness. Missing are a perceptual colour-difference score on star colours against an independently derived expected colour (CIEDE2000 or OKLab distance at matched luminance), a chroma-noise score in a perceptual space, and a structure-fidelity score of the stretch against the linear stack (TMQI's fidelity term).

### Cited Findings
- CIEDE2000 implementation notes and test data (Sharma, Wu, Dalal 2005, Color Research & Application 30(1):21-30) identify implementation errors not caught by the original worked examples and supply a larger test set (edge cases: zero chroma, hue discontinuity); reference code in Excel/MATLAB, community ports in Python. — [Sharma's CIEDE2000 page](https://www.ece.rochester.edu/~gsharma/ciede2000/), [paper PDF](https://hajim.rochester.edu/ece/sites/gsharma/papers/CIEDE2000CRNAFeb05.pdf)
- TMQI (Yeganeh & Wang 2013, IEEE TIP 22(2):657-667) scores a tone-mapped LDR image against the HDR original by combining multi-scale structural fidelity (modified SSIM) with a statistical naturalness term (intensity statistics of natural images); correlates with subjective rankings on independent databases; MATLAB code and the HDR Toolbox implementation are available. — [TMQI project page](https://ece.uwaterloo.ca/~z70wang/research/tmqi/), [paper PDF](https://ece.uwaterloo.ca/~z70wang/publications/TIP_TMQI.pdf)
- Existing bench metrics: same-star FWHM ratio, star colour index (display vs linear, Spearman + Theil-Sen), hue error on bright pixels, display sky noise, faint-signal CNR, dark rings, large-scale chroma mottle, flatness, and the G2V display colour from Gaia (cached `<stem>_gaia.json`). — [dev-notes/phase4-quality.md](file:///C:/source/originstack-research/dev-notes/phase4-quality.md)
- BP-RP to Teff (for an expected-colour reference without XP): Mucciarelli et al. 2021 relations, 60-80 K dispersion. — [arXiv:2106.03882](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2106.03882)
- Gaia XP spectra allow synthetic photometry at mmag level after standardisation (for an expected colour reference with XP). — [Montegriffo et al. 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2206.06215)

### Inferences

**Idea 6.1 - Non-circular star-colour accuracy score (Delta-E00 / OKLab distance, chroma-only)**
- Algorithm: for Gaia-matched, unsaturated, isolated stars with SNR > 30, take the display colour from an aperture of 1.5 FWHM on the displayed image (not the wings - the Crab case showed wings bias the aperture colour), convert to CIELAB (D65, sRGB), and compare with the expected colour of the star; use Delta-E00 on (a*, b*) with L* set equal (chroma/hue error only, since the stretch changes lightness by design). Expected colour must NOT come from the same Gaia-fitted calibration being scored: use XP-synthesised sRGB (needs the XP fetch of Idea 2.1) or a blackbody at the Mucciarelli Teff mapped through CIE 1931 CMFs to sRGB, with the solar star as white. Report median and 90th percentile per stack plus the Theil-Sen slope of display chroma vs expected chroma (the slope shows compression/expansion of star colours by the stretch).
- Why: a single scalar that is stable across stretches and comparable with the sRGB-neutral white. Cost S; confidence high that it is useful as a guide, with the caveat that the expected colour is only as good as Teff/XP.

**Idea 6.2 - Sky chroma-noise score in OKLab**
- Std of OKLab (a,b) in star-masked sky tiles, in units of the L* noise; replaces the ad-hoc "blotch" scale for comparing denoiser and chroma NR variants (and the proposed 5.1). Cost XS.

**Idea 6.3 - Structure fidelity of the stretch (TMQI's S term only)**
- Compute the multi-scale SSIM-style structural fidelity between the linear-stack luminance (after a fixed monotone reference mapping) and the display luminance within the object mask. Do NOT use TMQI's naturalness term: its prior is fitted to natural daylight photographs and a mostly-black astro frame violates it (my inference). Use as a regression guard for 3.1 (does compression keep local structure?). Cost S-M; confidence medium-low.

**Banding score (for 3.2):** std of the second difference of 16-px block means in smooth masked regions, or the count of distinct 8-bit levels per 64x64 smooth tile relative to the expected count. Cost XS.

### Gaps
- No standard metric set for star-colour fidelity in amateur astrophotography was found; Delta-E for stars is my adaptation.
- The TMQI naturalness prior's suitability for astro images was not tested.
- Delta-E_ITP (ITU-R BT.2124) and OKLab-distance validity for dim, self-luminous point sources were not researched.
