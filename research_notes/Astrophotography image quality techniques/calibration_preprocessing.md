Scope and method. Read first: the research worktree's [CLAUDE.md](file:///C:/source/originstack-research/CLAUDE.md) (module table, four phases, "Tried and reverted"), the headings of the five earlier notes under `research_notes/Astrophotography stacking techniques since 2022/`, and the dev-notes ([camera-profile](file:///C:/source/originstack-research/dev-notes/camera-profile.md), [siril-comparison](file:///C:/source/originstack-research/dev-notes/siril-comparison.md), [module-notes](file:///C:/source/originstack-research/dev-notes/module-notes.md), [native-kernels](file:///C:/source/originstack-research/dev-notes/native-kernels.md)). Nothing already implemented or reverted is re-proposed; where an existing feature could use a published method it is named. Besides literature, I ran **A/B stacks and measurements on the user's own Origin178 sessions** (`C:\source\Astrophotography\*`) using the unmodified worktree code, monkeypatched at run time from scripts in the scratchpad; outputs were written outside the repo and no repo file was edited (this notes file is the only write). Measurement scripts are linked inline. All numbers labelled "own measurement" are single-camera, a handful of sessions, and deterministic runs (a default re-run was bit-identical), but not a benchmark suite.

Framing fact that shapes every recommendation below: **Origin178 lights are sky-shot-noise limited** (own measurement: per-plane sky 199-385 e- per pixel per 20 s sub at the shipped 0.0165 e-/ADU; measured per-frame high-pass sigma is only 3-6% above the pure shot-noise expectation, i.e. non-shot variance is roughly 7-13% of the total). Calibration refinements below the ~100 ADU level therefore only matter for the *stack* (noise at 82-285 frames is 35-100 ADU), and only for sensor-locked patterns that dithering/rotation does not average away.

## 1. Noise modelling: Poisson-Gaussian, variance-stabilising transforms, per-pixel variance maps

### Takeaway
State of the art is the Poisson-Gaussian (P-G) raw model, variance stabilisation with the generalized Anscombe transform (GAT) and its exact unbiased inverse, and (since 2020) physics-based models with heavy-tailed read noise, row noise and quantisation, whose extra fidelity pays off only in extreme low light. OriginStack already has measured gain, PTC, correlated-noise factors and a GAT in Phase 4; on Origin data (200-385 e- sky per pixel per sub, 4.4% total vignetting) none of the not-yet-used pieces is worth building except as a low-priority tidy-up.

### Cited Findings
- Foi et al. (2008) give the standard single-image raw noise model: signal-dependent noise whose standard deviation combines a Poissonian (photon) term and a Gaussian (stationary) term, explicitly accounting for clipping, with parameters tied to QE, pedestal and analog gain, estimated from one image — [Foi, Trimeche, Katkovnik, Egiazarian, IEEE TIP 17(10) 2008 (dblp record)](https://dblp.org/rec/journals/tip/FoiTKE08); summary text at [synapsesocial/typeset mirror](https://typeset.io/papers/practical-poissonian-gaussian-noise-modeling-and-fitting-for-4cn04dppj4).
- Mäkitalo & Foi (IEEE TIP 22(1), 2013) give the exact unbiased inverse of the GAT for Poisson-Gaussian noise (interpretable as a maximum-likelihood inverse) and show it matters for accurate denoising; a closed-form approximation exists for the Anscombe case (TIP 2011) and for the generalized case in the author's thesis (section 4.3.6, per the table of contents) — [IEEE Xplore 6212354](https://ieeexplore.ieee.org/document/6212354); [Foi project page](https://webpages.tuni.fi/foi/invansc/); [thesis record](https://trepo.tuni.fi/handle/10024/114970). The simple algebraic inverse "generally gives a biased estimate" (same thesis record's search summary).
- OriginStack inverts the GAT algebraically and says so in its docstring ("adequate at the moderate-to-high SNR this pipeline's stacked images sit at"); it clips the input with `np.maximum(Y, 0.0)` and fits (gain, read-noise) from tile mean-variance on the stack itself — [denoising.py](file:///C:/source/originstack-research/src/denoising.py) (`_generalized_anscombe`, `_inverse_generalized_anscombe`, `_estimate_noise_level_function`). `photometry.poisson_coefficients` already derives the stack's exact Poisson coefficient (proper-coadd factor or sum w^2/(sum w)^2) — [photometry.py](file:///C:/source/originstack-research/src/photometry.py).
- Wei et al. (CVPR 2020) model raw noise as D = K*I + N with N = K*N_p + N_read + N_r + N_q: Poisson shot noise, zero-location Tukey-lambda read noise, per-row Gaussian offset, uniform quantisation. Ablation on the SID Sony set (PSNR gain over a Gaussian-read + true-Poisson baseline at x100 / x250 / x300 darkening): Tukey read noise +0.79 / +2.07 / +2.32 dB, row noise +0.13 / +0.43 / +0.32 dB, quantisation +0.04 / +0.24 / +0.29 dB, total +0.96 / +2.74 / +2.93 dB. Parameters are calibrated per camera from bias frames (row noise, Tukey-lambda fit) and flats (gain via photon transfer) — [arXiv:2003.12751](https://arxiv.org/abs/2003.12751); numbers read from [ar5iv HTML](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2003.12751).
- Brooks et al. (CVPR 2019, "Unprocessing") invert each camera-pipeline step to synthesise raw training data from ordinary sRGB images, add shot and read noise, and train a raw denoiser; reported 14%-38% lower error than the prior state of the art on the Darmstadt Noise Dataset — [arXiv:1811.11127](https://arxiv.org/abs/1811.11127) (figures via search summary of the abstract).
- EMVA 1288 (via Imatest/Teledyne secondary descriptions) defines DSNU (spatial std of the dark image) and PRNU = sqrt(s_light^2 - s_dark^2)/(mu_light - mu_dark), and notes pattern noise "is rarely significant" for high-quality scientific cameras — [Imatest uniformity statistics](https://imatest.com/docs/uniformity-statistics); [Teledyne, pattern noise DSNU and PRNU](https://www.teledynevisionsolutions.com/learn/learning-center/imaging-fundamentals/pattern-noise-dsnu-and-prnu/). The EMVA PDF itself was not readable in this session.
- Rubin coadds carry an explicit variance plane and inverse-variance weights (recorded in the earlier research note; not re-fetched) — [Rubin DP2 deep coadd](https://dp2.lsst.io/products/images/deep_coadd.html).
- Own measurement, Origin178 sky-limited regime: raw-plane sky above bias 12,086 / 23,175 / 23,307 / 15,175 ADU (R, G1, G2, B planes) = 199-385 e- at 0.0165 e-/ADU; per-frame high-pass sigma 909 / 1234 / 1241 / 990 ADU against shot-noise expectations of 856 / 1185 / 1189 / 959 ADU (ratio 1.03-1.06) — [sensor_locked_pattern.py](file:///C:/Users/hansd/AppData/Local/Temp/claude/c--source-originstack/7fb708f8-e00f-49e8-9d6e-e26e83d3a85f/scratchpad/sensor_locked_pattern.py) (Sunflower 2026-03-12, 40 lights).
- Own measurement, vignetting is small: the library flat, blurred as the pipeline does, spans 0.956-1.013 of its plane median (corner/centre 0.978-0.981); in the sky-limited regime variance goes as 1/F, so sigma differs by only 1.6% corner to centre — [vig_range.py](file:///C:/Users/hansd/AppData/Local/Temp/claude/c--source-originstack/7fb708f8-e00f-49e8-9d6e-e26e83d3a85f/scratchpad/vig_range.py).
- Own measurement, ADC quantisation: raw light values come in steps of 6-7 ADU (0.10 e-), against a per-frame sigma of 900-1240 ADU; quantisation variance step^2/12 = 3.3 ADU^2 is ~4e-6 of the pixel variance — one-liner on `Light0050.fits`, Sunflower session (step histogram 6: 1878, 7: 628).

### Inferences
#### N1. Exact unbiased GAT inverse, and GAT parameters from the analytic stack noise (very low value)
- What/why: replace `_inverse_generalized_anscombe` by the Mäkitalo-Foi exact unbiased inverse (precomputed table or closed form) so the VST round trip is unbiased at low counts; take (gain, sigma) from `photometry.poisson_coefficients` + camera profile instead of a tile regression that nebula-filled fields can corrupt.
- Algorithm: forward f(y) = (2/g) sqrt(g*y + 3/8 g^2 + s^2). For a grid of means x, E[f(y)|x] = sum_k Pois(k; x/g) * integral f(g*k + n) N(n; 0, s^2) dn (Gauss-Hermite with ~16 nodes, k from 0 to x/g + 6 sqrt(x/g)). The exact inverse I(D) is the x with E[f(y)|x] = D: build the monotone table once per (g, s) and `np.interp` it (cache with `lru_cache`).
- Expected gain: effectively zero here. A stack of N frames is again P-G with equivalent counts ~ N x 200 e- (tens of thousands), where the algebraic inverse's bias term (~1/8 count) is invisible; the fitted "read noise" absorbs the sky variance. I did not trace the size of the Phase 4 pedestal at the GAT input (Gaps).
- Plug-in: `denoising.py`. Cost: ~60 lines, no Rust needed. Risk: none. Confidence that it is not worth prioritising: medium-high.

#### N2. Per-pixel variance plane carried through Phase 3 (not worth it on Origin)
- What/why: Rubin-style variance plane V_i(x) = (a_i(x) - ped)/g + sigma_r^2 divided by flat^2, warped with squared-kernel weights, used for per-pixel inverse-variance weights and spatially adaptive Phase 4 thresholds.
- Expected gain: negligible. Flat range is 4.4% (sigma ratio 1.016), so per-pixel weights differ <1% between frames even under 13 deg of rotation; the existing scalar per-frame `s_j` in proper coadd already captures the dominant variation. Star Poisson variance is already handled by the GAT in Phase 4.
- Confidence: high that it is low value on this camera. It would matter for a strongly vignetted camera (other Origin models) or for a mosaic.

#### N3. Physics-based noise model (ELD) for synthetic data (not needed)
- Wei et al.'s gains (up to +2.9 dB) are for extreme low light (x100-x300 darkening); the project's denoisers train on real stack pairs, and Origin pixels are shot-noise dominated. Row noise is already handled by `banding.py`. Skip unless a synthetic-noise training path is ever added, in which case use Tukey-lambda read noise + row noise + quantisation from bias frames as in the paper.

### Gaps
- Did not trace how large the sky pedestal is when `directional_wavelet_denoise(variance_stabilize=True)` runs (CLAUDE.md lists a "sky floor" and "sky pedestal" step before the denoiser), so the low-count bias of the algebraic inverse is unquantified; the closed-form GAT inverse constants were not retrievable (formula pages did not render) and are not quoted.
- No EMVA 1288 primary text was readable; PRNU/DSNU definitions are secondhand.
- Origin read noise is still unmeasured (the library bias master is pre-averaged: high-pass sigma 29.65 ADU identical in all four planes), so the 7-13% non-shot variance share is an upper bound mixing read noise, dark shot noise, PRNU and the shipped-gain uncertainty.

## 2. Dark, bias and flat strategies (dark scaling, flat error, glow, FPN, RTS, ADC)

### Takeaway
Two calibration weaknesses are measurable on the user's data and both come from how a *single pre-averaged library master* is treated. (1) The master flat's real pixel-scale and dust-scale detail is blurred away (sigma 15 per plane) — restoring it lowered sky structure noise by 1-5% at 16-32 px on a 158-frame session. (2) The dark is scaled by exposure ratio only, but its glow component scales very differently with sensor temperature (fitted residual-glow scale from -0.67 at 19.5 C to +0.15 at 30 C against a dark taken at 31.3 C); correcting it lowered structure at 32-64 px by 7-11% on an 82-frame cold session. RTS noise and ADC non-linearity are irrelevant for this sky-limited sensor.

### Cited Findings

### Inferences
#### D1. Restore the master's real detail when the master is a single pre-averaged file (flat first)
- What/why: the blur heuristic assumes n = 1 means "one noisy frame". The Origin library flat is evidently an average of many frames (flat pixel pattern is 65% real by the regression, and the whole library is shared by every session), so sigma 15 discards real PRNU and dust/microlens structure at 3-60 px that the lights carry.
- Algorithm (two options, cheapest first):
  1. When the flat/dark/bias come as one file, use sigma_f = 3-4 (not 15) for the flat. The A/B above shows sigma 4 gives essentially the same gain as 1.5.
  2. Wiener restore: S = G_15(M), H = M - G_1.5(M); estimate beta by regressing the sensor-locked pattern of ~40 lights, P_hat = median_i[(L_i - med5(L_i)) / (med5(L_i) - ped)], on the relative high-pass H_rel = (M - med5(M)) / med5(M): beta = sum(P_hat * H_rel) / sum(H_rel^2) over unflagged pixels. Because the noise in P_hat is independent of M's noise, beta is the optimal linear (Wiener) gain var(PRNU) / (var(PRNU) + var(master noise)); I measured 0.63-0.70 for the flat. Use M' = S + beta * H, cache beta by file hash (the same master serves every session of that unit). The same recipe gives 0.39 (dark-bias) and 0.45 (bias) as starting points for the dark/bias patterns, untested end-to-end.
  3. Extension (untested): pool the sensor-locked pattern of the lights across all sessions of the unit (thousands of frames; per-pixel noise 1000/sqrt(5000) = 14 ADU vs a 35-90 ADU pattern) and combine with the master by inverse variance — the pro-pipeline "star flat / super-flat" idea (DES above) without needing stars. The project's earlier per-unit *vignetting* library gained only 0-5% (camera-profile.md), so expect gains only at pixel-to-60 px scales.
- Expected gain (measured, sigma 1.5; sigma 4 within about one percentage point): -0.3 to -1.5% sky noise at 1-4 px, -1.3 to -4.4% at 16 px and -0.5 to -5.1% at 32 px on the 158-frame Sunflower stack; weaker on the 13-frame session. Mechanistically the pixel-scale pattern (0.3-0.4% of sky = 34-87 ADU raw) is averaged away by dither and drift in well-dithered stacks, so the visible gain is at dust scale and in undithered sessions.
- Plug-in: `cli._build_masters` (the three `_gaussian_blur` calls); beta estimation next to `_measure_session_cfa` in `frame_processor`. Phase 1; new attribute needs `_P1_EXTRA` in `stack_fingerprint`. Cost: option 1 is a one-line heuristic plus a test; option 2 ~120 lines of numpy. Risk: injecting master noise if a user-supplied flat is a genuinely noisy single frame (guard: only when beta > ~0.3 or when the flat file's name/headers mark it as a master; keep the n-based rule for multi-frame user flats). Confidence: medium (one 158-frame session, one unit).

#### D2. Fit the dark's glow scale per session (per CFA plane) instead of assuming the exposure ratio
- What/why: glow is not linear in exposure and depends strongly on sensor temperature (table above); the Origin firmware itself scales darks by exposure, gain and temperature. A data-driven delta removes 10-160 ADU p-p of smooth glow-shaped residual that a quadratic cannot model (whether Phase 4's DBE already absorbs it was not measured).
- Algorithm: calibrate ~12 frames spread through the session exactly as now (bias, exposure-scaled dark, flat). For each, take 64 px block medians y of the calibrated mosaic (or per CFA plane) and of G = blurred (dark - bias) / flat_norm; solve y = P(x,y) a + delta*G with P a full 2nd-order polynomial in normalised coordinates, IRLS with 3-sigma clipping (6 iterations), SE from the residual scatter; session delta = median of per-frame deltas; shrink delta_use = delta * tau^2 / (tau^2 + SE^2) with tau ~0.3; accept only |delta| < 0.8, SE < 0.1 and spread across probes < 0.1, else keep 0; apply as dark_scale *= (1 + delta_use) for all frames (same mechanics as the session CFA constants). Siril's `-opt` (search for one scalar k on a central window) is the nearest published practice but cannot see edge glow and ignores the sky gradient; the polynomial nuisance term is the added safeguard. For ISO-mismatched darks fit per CFA plane (B overshot in the project's ISO test).
- Expected gain (measured): Markarian (cold): -7 to -11% sky structure at 32-64 px, -6 to -11% in quadratic-removed 32 px block scatter; Whirlpool (13 frames): -5% at 64 px, mixed after quadratic removal; pixel scale +1-2% on Markarian (unexplained, possibly quality-weight shifts). The effect after Phase 4 DBE/edge-bands was *not* measured, so the real-image gain is unproven; it is mainly a faint-structure fix for sessions far from the dark's temperature (4 of the 5 probed sessions have |delta| <= 0.15; only the coldest, Markarian, is far off, so the benefit is concentrated in cold sessions).
- Plug-in: new `_measure_session_dark_scale` beside `_measure_session_cfa` in `frame_processor.py`, result carried like `args._session_cfa`; `_P1_EXTRA`. Cost: ~100 lines numpy, ~0.2 s per probe frame, no Rust. Risk: aliasing of real extended nebulosity into the G shape in fields that fill the frame (bounded by clipping and the acceptance rules); the physical cause of the steep temperature dependence is unestablished. Confidence: medium-low.

#### D3. Tiered treatment of warm versus hot pixels (low value)
- What/why: 83% of flagged "hot" pixels contribute <1 frame-sigma of excess in a 20 s light, yet all are replaced by neighbour medians, discarding their photon data; only 7.2% (0.25% of the sensor) exceed 2 sigma.
- Algorithm: amp_i = per-plane 7x7 high-pass of the dark at flagged pixels; HOT if amp_i * s > 2 sigma_frame (replace as now); WARM otherwise: keep the pixel and subtract beta_w * s * amp_i with beta_w ~0.6 (0.39 / 0.667 from the dark-pattern regression), s = exposure ratio times the D2 factor.
- Expected gain: tiny (<=1% of pixel variance; 2.8% of the sensor stops being interpolated). The project's "no dark" tests suggest the dark already adds error, so warm-pixel subtraction could add outliers. Rank low. Confidence: low.

#### D4. RTN detection/correction: not worth it for the Origin178
- Evidence for irrelevance: the literature gains (>5% SNR) are for sources below 3 e-/pix/frame at high frame rates; the Origin has 199-385 e- of sky per pixel per sub, and an RTN pixel with a few-e- jump is ~0.1-0.3 sigma of shot noise. The Layden/Czech methods (mixture-of-Gaussians per pixel from thousands of darks) also need a dark series the Origin never provides (the library dark is pre-averaged). If a future model (Origin678C/IMX678, ISO 500 dark sites) is read-noise limited, reuse the Anderson-Darling + Gaussian-mixture recipe above on the lights' temporal series. Confidence: high.

#### D5. ADC non-linearity / quantisation-aware processing: not worth it
- Measured quantisation step 6-7 ADU vs 900-1240 ADU noise; Wei et al. find quantisation worth only +0.04 to +0.29 dB even at x100-x300 darkening. Confidence: high.

### Gaps
- Origin read noise, dark-current temperature law and the cause of the glow-scale/temperature relation are not established (five sessions, one unit, one dark at 31.3 C). The 13-frame Whirlpool A/B is too short for pixel-scale conclusions.
- The end-to-end benefit of D1/D2 after Phase 4 (DBE, edge bands, denoiser) was not measured; only linear-stack sky-structure statistics were.
- No published method for "restore master detail with a light-derived Wiener gain" was found; it is ordinary PRNU/FPN estimation (EMVA vocabulary) and linear shrinkage, so treat the recipe as engineering rather than citation-backed.
- Did not run Siril or PixInsight on the same data; PixInsight's dark "optimization threshold" documentation was not retrieved.
- HSC/Euclid calibration pipelines and Gaia raw-data handling were not researched (CCD/TDI-specific, low transfer).

## 3. Hot/cold/defective pixels and cosmic rays, including CFA-space handling

### Takeaway
The project's defect handling is already beyond amateur norms (CFA-space statistical fix with a star-support rule, opt-in `--spike-reject`, global map from the dark). The remaining published practice that differs is professional: flag-and-zero-weight (DES) or flag-and-propagate masks (Rubin) rather than interpolate, and PSF-aware interpolation; on Origin data the expected gain is small because sigma-clipping and dithering already hide isolated defects.

### Cited Findings
- DES single-epoch processing: bad-pixel masks from bias and dome-flat analysis (FLAT_MIN <50% of mean, FLAT_MAX >150% in >=3 inputs, BIAS_HOT >500 e-); cosmic rays are pixels >6 sigma above the mean of opposing-pixel pairs that pass an extra PSF-based test; streaks by Hough transform; "pixels flagged BADAMP, EDGEBLEED, EDGE, or CRAY receive zero weight" — [DES Image Processing Pipeline](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1801.03177).
- Rubin `FindDefectsTask` thresholds master darks (hot) and flats (cold) either in sigma or in fixed physical units (a dark-current threshold in e-/s for hot pixels; a fractional threshold relative to the per-amplifier mean for cold pixels) — [lsst.cp.pipe.FindDefectsTask](https://pipelines.lsst.io/v/v24_1_0/py-api/lsst.cp.pipe.FindDefectsTask.html). Rubin carries mask planes through coaddition: CLIPPED marks pixels where an input was excluded as an artifact, INTRP marks interpolated pixels (the DP1 and DP2 pages define SAT/CR differently, so check the release) — [DP2 mask planes](https://dp2.lsst.io/products/images/mask_planes.html), [DP1 mask planes](https://dp1.lsst.io/products/images/mask_planes.html). `interpolateFromMask` takes a FWHM that builds a double-Gaussian PSF but, per its own API note, the interpolation code does not currently use it — [lsst.ip.isr.interpolateFromMask](https://pipelines.lsst.io/py-api/lsst.ip.isr.interpolateFromMask.html).
- Project history: the Bayer star-support rule fixed star-core clipping; spikes smear over 9 px in R/B vs 5 in G after Malvar; forced per-frame lacosmic cut noise 11-16% but widened stars 7-12% — [siril-comparison.md](file:///C:/source/originstack-research/dev-notes/siril-comparison.md); `--spike-reject` (opt-in) is documented in [native-kernels.md](file:///C:/source/originstack-research/dev-notes/native-kernels.md).
- Own measurement: the hot map's global 5-sigma rule and a local-plane high-pass rule agree on 98% of pixels (above), so the 3.4% count reflects real warm pixels, not an over-sensitive threshold.

### Inferences
#### C1. Carry a validity/weight plane for defects instead of baking in interpolated values (low value, high effort)
- What/why: interpolation (hot map, trail inpaint, spike fix) injects a correlated estimate that the Lanczos warp then spreads; DES zero-weights and Rubin clips such pixels. Normalised convolution keeps the warp unbiased: out = warp(I*W)/warp(W).
- Algorithm: build W (0 at hot-map/spike/trail pixels in CFA space, dilated by the debayer footprint), debayer W by the same footprint (any pixel within RCD's support of a bad CFA sample gets W < 1), warp I*W and W with the same Lanczos-3 kernel, give the combine a per-sample weight.
- Cost: high — the native combine/warp kernels take per-frame weights, not per-sample ones, and proper coadd works in Fourier space (no per-sample weights). Gain: small (dither + sigma clip already suppress isolated defects; the dominant 83% of flagged pixels are sub-sigma warm pixels). Confidence: medium that it is low priority.

#### C2. PSF-aware defect interpolation inside star footprints (low value)
- Replace the neighbour median by a linear predictor from the local star model (Rubin intended exactly this with its PSF-FWHM argument but has not wired it). Only 0.25% of the sensor is hot enough to matter and the star-support rule already protects peaks. Skip.

### Gaps
- Did not read the cp_pipe source for how variance is used in defect detection (search returned only API pages). No evidence found that a published method treats RTN-affected pixels inside an amateur stacking tool.

## 4. Demosaicing and CFA handling

### Takeaway
Nothing published clearly beats RCD for stars; the project has already tried and reverted the multi-frame CFA routes (CFA drizzle, kernel regression). The only new, cheap candidate is a noise-aware second demosaicer (LMMSE) for low-contrast sky in a dual scheme, which darktable's documentation supports qualitatively but nobody has measured for stacked astro data. Learned joint demosaic-denoise methods (self-supervised on bursts, or trained on "unprocessed" stacks) are feasible with the project's existing numpy U-Net path but expensive and unlikely to pay off at the Origin's 3.4-5 px star width.

### Cited Findings
- darktable manual: "AMaZE and RCD offer better reconstruction of high-frequency content (finer details, edges, stars) but might struggle with color reconstruction overshoots or added noise in areas of low contrast"; "LMMSE is better suited for use on high ISO and noisy images than AMaZE or RCD, both of which tend to generate overshooting artefacts when applied to such images"; dual demosaic modes run a detail algorithm (RCD) and a smooth one (VNG4) and blend by a local-contrast threshold — [darktable 4.6 demosaic module](https://docs.darktable.org/usermanual/4.6/en/module-reference/processing-modules/demosaic/). RawPedia's guidance (AMaZE best at low ISO, LMMSE/IGV for very noisy high-ISO images, RCD "excellent for round edges, for example stars") came through a search summary; the RawPedia page itself returned 404 — [RawPedia Demosaicing](https://rawpedia.rawtherapee.com/Demosaicing).
- Zhang & Wu (2005) directional LMMSE demosaicking estimates the missing green samples along rows and columns by LMMSE and fuses them; Getreuer's reference implementation is BSD-2-licensed C — [IPOL article](https://www.ipol.im/pub/art/2011/g_zwld/). (The page gave no algorithm details; the method is described here only at that level.)
- Ehret et al. (ICCV 2019): mosaic-to-mosaic training with loss ||M(T(D(I1))) - I2||_p between a demosaicked burst frame, warped by a bicubic transform and masked to where both are defined (only one third of the warped output is compared), and a second registered frame I2; transform estimated on the four Bayer-phase half-resolution planes; fine-tuning a pretrained network on 10 noisy images of the same burst improved the Gharbi et al. network by more than 1 dB (36.2 -> 37.6 dB, sigma = 5) — [arXiv:1905.05092](https://arxiv.org/abs/1905.05092), details read from [ar5iv](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1905.05092). No runtime figures are given.
- Project facts: RCD is the default; Menon is not better; Malvar smears spikes; CFA drizzle (+18 to +75% pixel noise, stars 2.5-8% wider) and a Wronski-style kernel-regression merge (1.3-2.9x noisier at matched width) were tried and kept opt-in/removed; lateral CA is measured once per session and applied per frame; proper coadd uses **one** Moffat PSF per frame for all three channels — [pipeline-notes.md](file:///C:/source/originstack-research/dev-notes/pipeline-notes.md), [siril-comparison.md](file:///C:/source/originstack-research/dev-notes/siril-comparison.md), [proper_coadd.py](file:///C:/source/originstack-research/src/proper_coadd.py).
- Own measurement, per-channel star width in the 158-frame Sunflower stack (300 isolated unsaturated stars, Gaussian fits): FWHM R 3.56, G 3.56, B 3.39 px (B/G 0.963 per star) — [chan_fwhm2.py](file:///C:/Users/hansd/AppData/Local/Temp/claude/c--source-originstack/7fb708f8-e00f-49e8-9d6e-e26e83d3a85f/scratchpad/chan_fwhm2.py).
- An OSC forum discussion states the purpose of Bayer drizzle is to avoid interpolation by using real samples and that it needs many frames — [pixls.us thread](https://discuss.pixls.us/t/osc-extract-haoiii/34804) (anecdotal; no astronomy study of star-profile aliasing under CFA sampling was found).

### Inferences
#### M1. Dual (RCD + LMMSE) demosaic for low-contrast sky
- What/why: darktable and RawPedia say RCD adds noise/overshoot in low-contrast, noisy areas where LMMSE is more stable; per-frame Origin sky SNR is ~14-20 per pixel. Linear low-pass interpolation does not change *low-frequency* noise power (it only shapes high-frequency correlation), so the expected stack gain is mostly less R/B overshoot and a smoother pixel-scale noise texture, not lower faint-structure noise.
- Algorithm: run RCD as now; run a directional-LMMSE (Zhang-Wu) pass; blend per pixel with w = smoothstep(c0, c1, local_contrast), local_contrast from a 5x5 gradient energy normalised by the frame's noise sigma, using RCD where stars/edges are (w ~ 1) and LMMSE elsewhere. Port Getreuer's C to Rust under the project's native-kernel rules (numpy mirror + parity test).
- Test design: A/B at matched star width with `tools/bench_vs_siril.matched_noise`; accept only if noise at matched width falls and star colour (`*_lin`) is unchanged. Expected gain: 0-3% (guess; no data). Cost: moderate-high (new kernel). Risk: softens stars; the project found Menon no better than RCD. Confidence: low.

#### M2. Per-session self-supervised mosaic-to-mosaic fine-tuning (not recommended)
- Idea: fine-tune a small joint demosaic-denoise CNN on the session's own registered frames (Ehret et al.), no ground truth. Cost: training per session on CPU, per-frame inference 10-20x RCD (rough: ~22k MAC per half-resolution pixel for a 5-layer 24-channel net = ~3.5e10 MAC per 6 MP frame, a few seconds single-threaded), and in a stack the per-frame denoising part is redundant. Gain unquantifiable at 3.4-5 px FWHM, where demosaic aliasing of stars is modest. Confidence: low.

#### M3. Origin-specific learned demosaicer trained on "unprocessed" project stacks
- Idea: synthesise training mosaics from the project's deep RGB stacks with Brooks et al.'s unprocessing (CFA-sample the stack, add Poisson-Gaussian noise using the measured 0.0165 e-/ADU gain), train offline with the existing `tools/train_nn_denoise.py` path, ship weights like `nn_denoise.npz`, run on native `nn_conv3x3`. Fits the project's "no big download, numpy inference" rule. Caveat: the ground truth is itself RCD-demosaiced and Lanczos-resampled (band-limited), so the net learns to invert CFA sampling of already smooth images; for 3.4-5 px stars RCD is already near that limit. It could help sharper sessions (Sculptor 2.78 px). Gain unknown; cost high. Confidence: low.

#### M4. Per-channel PSF in proper coadd (low value)
- `proper_coadd` fits one Moffat per frame for all channels; own measurement finds B 3.7% narrower than R/G in the Sunflower stack (Origin PSF is chromatic per [star-n2n-runbook.md](file:///C:/source/originstack-research/dev-notes/star-n2n-runbook.md)). Fitting the Moffat per channel would correct the Fourier-domain weights by a few percent in width; the weights are second-order in that error. Cost: low (3 fits instead of 1, ~3x the fit time on ~30 stars). Gain: <1% expected. Confidence: low.

#### Excluded (already tried here)
- Joint multi-frame demosaic via dither (CFA drizzle, kernel regression), demosaic-free luminance, Menon: reverted per CLAUDE.md. Lateral CA: already measured once per session; colour crosstalk/spectral response is handled downstream by Gaia colour calibration.

### Gaps
- No astronomy study of OSC star-profile aliasing or demosaic choice for stacking was found. LMMSE algorithm details were not retrievable from the IPOL page. The "noise-aware demosaic helps stacked data" claim is an inference from photography documentation.

## 5. Professional pipelines (Rubin, DES, JWST) and what transfers

### Takeaway
Most professional instrument-signature-removal steps are either already present in OriginStack in an equivalent form (JWST flicker-noise ~ `banding.py`; DES star flat ~ the tried-and-reverted ubercal flat) or irrelevant to a sky-limited consumer CMOS (crosstalk, brighter-fatter, linearity). What transfers: zero-weight/propagated masks (C1), per-region rather than global defect statistics (D3), and t_eff-style quality accounting (Section 6).

### Cited Findings
- DES: nightly star flats, bad-pixel masks from bias/flat analysis, zero weights for flagged pixels, t_eff-based exposure acceptance — [DES Image Processing Pipeline](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1801.03177).
- Rubin: variance plane + inverse-variance coadd; mask planes with CLIPPED/INTRP propagated through coadds; "interpolation and warping modify the noise properties" so the noise image is run through the same procedures (from the earlier note) — [DP2 deep coadd](https://dp2.lsst.io/products/images/deep_coadd.html), [DP0.2 processing](https://dp0-2.lsst.io/data-products-dp0-2/data-processing.html), [mask planes](https://dp2.lsst.io/products/images/mask_planes.html).
- JWST: `clean_flicker_noise` after the jump step, scene mask from a draft image, median (or FFT) fit along the slow axis — [docs](https://jwst-pipeline.readthedocs.io/en/stable/jwst/clean_flicker_noise/main.html).
- The project's ubercal flat from stars was unconstrained on the local sessions (<=0.4 deg rotation per session) — [CLAUDE.md](file:///C:/source/originstack-research/CLAUDE.md).

### Inferences
- DES star flats rely on 22 well-dithered exposures; the Origin's drift is tens of pixels over a session, enough for dust-scale structure (D1 extension, light-derived) but not demonstrated for pixel-level flats. The DES statement that star flats are unreliable within 25 px of the edge suggests restricting any light-derived pattern to the interior.
- The Rubin "run the noise image through the same pipeline" practice is the justification for the project's difference-of-aligned-frames correlated-noise measurement; no new action.

### Gaps
- HSC and Euclid pipelines and Gaia raw handling were not researched; no source was gathered on them.

## 6. Per-frame quality scoring and weighting beyond FWHM/SNR

### Takeaway
The project's proper coadd already weights each frame by F_j^2/s_j^2 with a per-frame PSF, which is the optimal point-source/extended-source weighting; what is missing is a single principled per-frame depth scalar (DES's t_eff) for the quality gate, reporting and cross-session merging, and a spatially varying (not scalar) photometric scale. Both are small wins on Origin data.

### Cited Findings
- DES effective exposure time: t_eff = (FWHM_fid/FWHM)^2 (B_fid/B) F_trans, with F_trans = 1 for a median magnitude offset dm <= 0.2 and 10^(-0.8 (dm - 0.2)) beyond; wide-field exposures are accepted only above a minimum t_eff (0.2-0.3 by band) and FWHM_max (1.5-1.8 arcsec); in DES t_eff is a **cut, not a coadd weight** — [DES Image Processing Pipeline, Section IV.7](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1801.03177). The Year 3 deep-field variant is T_eff ~ T * eta^2 / (s * FWHM^2) with eta the transmission and s the sky-noise variance — [DES Y3 deep fields](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2012.12824) (via search summary).
- PixInsight LocalNormalization (PCL `LocalNormalizationData.h`): v' = U(A(c,i)) * (v - C(c)) + U(B(c,i)), with a **scaling matrix A and a zero-offset matrix B** at a dyadic scale of 64/128/256 px (>=32), "suitable for pixel rejection and image integration tasks, including drizzle integration" — [PCL header mirror](https://gitea.nouspiro.space/nou/tenmon/raw/commit/12c6385f776ba446a4f5f40efb90ecc4da723046/3rdparty/include/pcl/LocalNormalizationData.h). OriginStack's `local_normalize.py` is additive only by design (the earlier multiplicative version divided by local sigma and amplified noise and was removed) — [local_normalize.py](file:///C:/source/originstack-research/src/local_normalize.py).
- OriginStack's gate uses hard limits (no stars, SNR <0.5, contrast <2, dynamic range <20), a 2.5-sigma statistical outlier test and a percentile filter, before registration; transparency is a scalar per frame from a fixed star ensemble after registration — [frame_processor.py `quality_gate`](file:///C:/source/originstack-research/src/frame_processor.py), [transparency.py](file:///C:/source/originstack-research/src/transparency.py).
- Earlier project notes: no shipped amateur tool uses learned frame scoring (see the earlier ML note); not repeated here.

### Inferences
#### T1. t_eff per frame and "effective integration time" (small, nearly free)
- What/why: summarise FWHM (use the Moffat noise-equivalent area n_eff = 1/sum(P^2) proper coadd already fits instead of FWHM), sky noise s_j and transparency F_j in one number t_eff_j = F_j^2 / (s_j^2 * n_eff_j), normalised to the best 10% of frames or a fixed fiducial. Report sum(t_eff)/sum(exposure) as "efficiency" and the equivalent integration time in the FITS header (`TEFF`); use it (i) as a post-registration gate replacing ad-hoc relative thresholds (reject t_eff below ~0.2-0.3 of the best, as DES does), and (ii) in `merge.py` to choose the reference session and weights instead of `INTGTIME`.
- Expected gain: small. Optimal weights already down-weight bad frames, so gating adds robustness and a trustworthy summary, not noise reduction. Plug-in: after the transparency step in `run_registration_phase`; header in `populate_fits_header`. Cost: ~50 lines. Confidence: medium that it is useful as a diagnostic, low that it changes image quality.

#### T2. Spatially varying multiplicative transparency (low value)
- Add a smooth gain map A_j(x,y) per frame from the star ensemble's flux ratios to the reference (2nd-order polynomial or a 3x3 grid with thin-plate smoothing), as the scale half of PixInsight's v' = A (v - C) + B, used as F_j(x,y) in proper coadd. It avoids the old LN failure (dividing by local sigma) because the scale comes from star photometry, not noise.
- Why low: differential extinction across a 1.5 x 1 deg field at airmass 2 is ~1-2% (my order-of-magnitude estimate from dX/dtheta, not a cited value), thin cirrus gradients are rare, and sky additive terms are already handled by LN/DBE. Confidence: low.

### Gaps
- No source on noise-/background-/transparency-aware scoring in PixInsight SubframeSelector or APP (PSF Signal Weight, SNR weight) was retrieved; DES t_eff is the only primary formula found. The DES paper's text beyond the first 100k characters was not read.

## 7. Which ideas to build first: ranked by (benefit / effort)

### Takeaway
On sky-limited Origin data most calibration refinements are worth <1% (N1, N2, D3, D4, D5, C1, C2, M4), and the measurable wins are two calibration-master fixes plus one diagnostic: (1) restore the single library flat's detail (D1), (2) fit the dark's glow scale per session (D2), (3) report t_eff / effective integration time (T1). Learned demosaicers (M2, M3) and LMMSE (M1) are speculative and expensive.

### Cited Findings
