## 1. PSF homogenisation across the field and across frames (kernel matching, spatially varying PSF, how professional coadd pipelines do it)

### Takeaway
Professional pipelines (HSC/LSST, DESI Legacy Surveys) do NOT homogenise PSFs before coaddition: HSC coadds are plain weighted means with a *propagated* spatially varying coadd PSF, and Legacy Surveys fit sources to every exposure with that exposure's own PSF. The theory (Zackay-Ofek) agrees: homogenising before combining loses sensitivity, which is exactly why OriginStack's proper coadd is right. Where a uniform, round PSF IS wanted (KiDS lensing, Euclid multi-band colours), it is imposed AFTER coaddition with a spatially varying Wiener/shapelet kernel, at the cost of correlated noise. For OriginStack the transferable pieces are (a) a post-coadd, star-footprint-only, flux-conserving, spatially varying PSF-matching step and (b) a better PSF model (empirical, spatially varying, per channel) feeding the existing proper coadd, deconvolution and star tools.

### Cited Findings
- HSC pipeline: coadds are a direct weighted average of Lanczos-3 resampled CCD images; the pipeline states it does "not match the PSFs of the input images before combining them" and says this is not optimal — [Bosch et al. 2018, Sec. 3.3](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1705.06766)
- HSC coadd PSF: the pipeline follows Jee & Tyson (2011): the input PSF models are resampled and combined with the same coordinate transforms and weights as the image data, evaluated on demand at each object's position and cached; each coadd patch stores PSF models, WCS, weights and CCD boundaries — [Bosch et al. 2018, Sec. 3.3.1](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1705.06766)
- The same paper states that robust estimators (median, sigma-clipped mean) "cannot be used" for PSF-coaddition because they break the linearity the propagated PSF relies on and bias the PSF of bright stars; instead it builds a direct mean and a 1.5-sigma clipped mean, differences them, flags regions at 2 sigma and excludes only flagged inputs from a final linear mean — [Bosch et al. 2018, Sec. 3.3.2](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1705.06766)
- DESI Legacy Surveys: the Tractor fits each source "simultaneously to the pixel-level data of all images containing the source", using each exposure's own PSF (PSFEx per CCD), inputs are "not all convolved to a common PSF"; stacks are built for source detection — [Dey et al. 2019 (Legacy Surveys overview), Sec. VIII](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1804.08657); Tractor code: [github.com/dstndstn/tractor](https://github.com/dstndstn/tractor), [ASCL 1604.008](https://ascl.net/1604.008)
- Proper coaddition (Zackay & Ofek): applying a matched filter per image with that image's own PSF and summing is optimal for detection; the abstract states that methods that "filter after coaddition, or homogenize PSFs before coaddition, lose sensitivity", and claims a few to 25 percent gain in survey speed over weighted coaddition — [Zackay & Ofek, arXiv:1512.06872](https://arxiv.org/abs/1512.06872)
- Piff (DES Y3) replaced PSFEx: models the PSF in sky (not pixel) coordinates, can use the full focal plane, PixelGrid model with 3rd-order polynomial interpolation in DES Y3 — [PSF-modelling review, arXiv:2306.07996 (snippet only)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2306.07996); [Jarvis et al. 2021, MNRAS 501, 1282](https://iac.es/en/science-and-technology/publications/dark-energy-survey-year-3-results-point-spread-function-modelling). An independent HSC comparison found PSFEx and Piff residuals "of comparable order of magnitude", Piff's M40/M04/M60/M06 residuals a few times larger — [arXiv:2205.07892 (snippet only)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2205.07892)
- KiDS (Kuijken et al.): high-S/N stars are modelled with a shapelet expansion whose scale radius is matched to the worst seeing among the sub-exposures; shapelet coefficients are fitted across the field with polynomials (PSF map); a grid of kernels that turns the PSF map into a Gaussian is likewise fitted with polynomials (kernel map) and each coadd is convolved with its kernel map; a residual non-Gaussianity map gives a perturbative correction; the cost is noise correlated on small scales, tracked as a noise covariance matrix — [Kuijken et al. 2015, Sec. 4.2 (Appendix A not read)](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1507.00738). DR4 changed to solving the kernel coefficients directly in pixel space for a Gaussian target instead of modelling the PSF first (snippet only) — [KiDS DR4, arXiv:1902.11265](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1902.11265); DR5 reiterates the noise-correlation trade-off (snippet only) — [KiDS DR5, arXiv:2503.19439](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2503.19439)
- Euclid/Herschel-style homogenisation: kernels are obtained by Wiener filtering with a tunable regularisation parameter, using the full (anisotropic) PSF; gains of "up to two orders of magnitude" over kernels built from Gaussian/circularised PSFs — [Boucaud et al. 2016, arXiv:1609.02006](https://arxiv.org/abs/1609.02006); tool: [pypher](https://pypher.readthedocs.io/). Euclid MER builds kernels per object from PSF stamps and assumes a band's PSF does not depend on the source's colour (a stated limitation) — [Euclid Q1 MER, arXiv:2503.15305 (snippet only)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2503.15305). A Euclid high-z paper homogenised to the broadest-PSF band (VISTA Y, 0.85") rather than the sharpest — [arXiv:2510.00945 (snippet only)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2510.00945)
- Image-differencing kernel matching: Alard-Lupton (1998) solves a linear least-squares kernel whose coefficients vary polynomially with position; SFFT (Hu et al. 2022, ApJ 936, 157) uses a delta-function kernel basis and solves for a spatially varying kernel, photometric scaling and sky in Fourier space in one global solve, about an order of magnitude faster than other published subtraction codes — [Hu et al., arXiv:2109.09334 (snippet only)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2109.09334); Python implementation in [stdpipe.sfft](https://stdpipe.readthedocs.io/en/latest/api/stdpipe.sfft.html) (snippet only)
- Spatially varying convolution in practice: Lauer (2002) uses a Karhunen-Loeve (PCA) basis of "eigen-PSFs" (about four describe the variation well) with their FFTs precomputed, inside Lucy-Richardson — [Lauer 2002, astro-ph/0208247](https://arxiv.org/pdf/astro-ph/0208247); Efficient Filter Flow makes space-variant filtering "almost as cheap" as space-invariant via overlap-add — [Hirsch et al. CVPR 2010](https://optml.mit.edu/papers/cvpr10.pdf); applied to blind correction of optical aberrations with an orthonormal PSF basis — [Schuler et al. 2012 (snippet only)](https://link.springer.com/content/pdf/10.1007/978-3-642-33712-3_14.pdf)
- Photutils ships `GriddedPSFModel` (image-based ePSFs at grid nodes, interpolated to a position) for spatially varying PSFs, and `ImagePSF` for one global PSF — [photutils PSF guide](https://docs.astropy.org/projects/photutils/en/stable/user_guide/psf.html)
- Commercial baseline: BlurXTerminator AI4 was trained on coma, astigmatism, trefoil, defocus, field curvature, lateral/longitudinal CA and guiding blur and has a flux-conserving linear mode (vendor claim; taken from the earlier note, not re-fetched) — [RC Astro](https://www.rc-astro.com/blurxterminator-2-0-ai4-release/)

### Inferences
- OriginStack already follows the optimal recipe for combining (per-frame PSF, per-frequency weights). The gap is the OUTPUT: nothing in the project makes stars round and equally wide over the field. `shrink_stars` is isotropic, RL-SV leaves stars alone, and the nn deconvolver rescales to a trained width globally. A KiDS/Euclid-style step is the missing piece, but must be done after the coadd (Zackay-Ofek) and ideally restricted to star footprints, which sidesteps KiDS's main cost (correlated sky noise).
- Because the Origin field rotates, the stack PSF at a sky position is a rotation-average of the fixed sensor-frame PSF: anisotropic aberrations (coma/astigmatism/tilt) are partly averaged to round, while radial growth of FWHM survives. So the stack's measured PSF map is the right input for homogenisation (it already includes the averaging), and a per-frame spatial PSF model (Idea 2) is a smaller win than a post-coadd one (Idea 1).
- Bosch's linearity warning applies to `--stack-method median/sigma-clip`: PSFs measured from such stacks differ from the proper-coadd/mean ones, so PSF maps should always be measured from the stack actually being processed, never propagated from per-frame models for those methods.

#### Idea 1: Post-coadd, star-footprint-only, flux-conserving PSF homogenisation ("roundening")
- What/why: Make every star equally wide and circular (and optionally equal in R, G, B) without touching sky noise or nebulosity. Removes corner coma/tilt elongation, FWHM growth towards the edge, and R/B-vs-G width mismatch that shows as coloured fringes on stars. Gives the nn deconvolver and `shrink_stars` a uniform input.
- Algorithm:
  1. PSF map. From the stack's detected stars (matched-filter catalogue), build per-channel PSFs on a coarse grid (e.g. 5x4 nodes) with the shape model of Idea 4 (or elliptical Moffat sx, sy, rho, beta as in `proper_coadd._fit_psf`). `aberration.py` already bins stars per cell; extend to return full PSF parameters. Interpolate parameters (bilinear/thin-plate) to any pixel.
  2. Target PSF `P_t`: circular Moffat (beta fixed, e.g. the median beta) with FWHM_t = q80 of node FWHMs x 1.05 (broader than the sharp 80% of the field, as the Euclid z~7 paper chose a broad reference). Optional "mild sharpening" mode: FWHM_t = median, accepting regularised deconvolution at the worst nodes.
  3. Kernel per node (Fourier): K_hat = P_t_hat * conj(P_s_hat) / (|P_s_hat|^2 + mu * |h_hat|^2), h = discrete Laplacian (the regularised-Wiener form used by pypher; the abstract verifies only "Wiener filter with tunable regularisation"). Choose mu so that the kernel's noise gain sqrt(sum K^2) <= 1.5 (stars have S/N >> 1 so mu can be small); crop to <= 15x15; renormalise sum K = 1 (flux conservation); reject nodes whose kernel has negative lobes below -5% of peak (fall back to larger mu).
  4. Apply to the star layer only: S = M_star * (I - B), with B the local background (ring median, as in `shrink_stars`) and M_star the feathered footprint mask (`star_removal.build_star_mask`). Output I' = I + (K_local * S - S) with K_local blended between nodes by raised-cosine tiles (`psf_deconvolution._feather_window` already exists) or by the eigen-PSF trick (Idea 3). Per channel with its own P_s and a common P_t.
  5. Linear data, Phase 4, right after DBE/sky steps and before denoise/deconvolve (same neighbourhood as the halo step in `postprocess.py` ~line 361).
- Source: Kuijken et al. 2015 (https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1507.00738), Boucaud et al. 2016 (https://arxiv.org/abs/1609.02006), Hu et al. 2022 for kernel fitting alternatives (https://arxiv.org/abs/2109.09334), Zackay & Ofek (do it after, not before, the coadd) (https://arxiv.org/abs/1512.06872).
- Expected gain/evidence: Survey-scale evidence that spatially varying kernels give a uniform PSF (KiDS GAaP, Euclid MER); no published amateur OSC validation. Measurable here with existing tools: star FWHM spread and ellipticity before/after (`tools/bench_phase4.py`, `aberration.py`), shared-star widths per channel (`tools/bench_vs_siril.py` rules in CLAUDE.md). Photometry preserved by sum K = 1.
- Plug-in: new Phase 4 step in `postprocess.py` (needs `SNAP_STEPS`, `PHASE4_TOGGLES`, `_PP_STEP_LABELS` entries and a `_at('name', enabled)` guard per CLAUDE.md), helper module e.g. `psf_homogenise.py`; numpy FFT per star stamp is cheap (stamps ~64x64); Rust only if profiling demands.
- Cost/complexity/risk: M (about a week). Risks: PSF-map error produces ringing (mitigated by mu and negative-lobe check); a broader target softens the sharpest stars (mode flag); interaction with `--deconvolve nn` (it rescales to a trained width via `measure_star_fwhm`) must be tested in both orders; saturated stars need the Idea 6/8 profile, not the core PSF.
- Confidence: medium.

#### Idea 2: Spatially varying and per-channel PSF in `proper_coadd.py`
- What/why: The current filter uses one field-averaged elliptical Moffat per frame for all channels, so corner pixels are matched-filtered with the wrong PSF and R/B planes (half/quarter-density CFA, different MTF after RCD) with the G-dominated one. Per-image PSF matching is what the optimality proof assumes; extending "image" to "tile x channel" follows the same logic.
- Algorithm: (a) per channel fit the same joint Moffat on the same star centroids (the fit is already a linear-amplitude least squares; cost x3); (b) position dependence: P_j(x, y) = Moffat(sigma_j * s(x,y)) with a session-constant sensor-frame aberration field s(x,y), e1(x,y), e2(x,y) estimated once from all frames' stars (PSFEx-style low-order polynomial, orders 2-3, in sensor coordinates because optics are fixed in the sensor frame), times a per-frame seeing scale; (c) in `_combine_tiled` evaluate the PSF spectrum per column tile (the code already tiles by `_COL_TILE`) and interpolate spectra between tile centres.
- Source: Zackay & Ofek (https://arxiv.org/abs/1512.06872); PSFEx/Piff practice (https://iac.es/en/science-and-technology/publications/dark-energy-survey-year-3-results-point-spread-function-modelling); Legacy Surveys per-exposure PSF (https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1804.08657); static-optics-plus-varying-atmosphere decomposition is my adaptation (inference, not taken from a source).
- Expected gain/evidence: Unknown and probably modest; no published number for Fourier-domain proper coadd with tile-varying PSF in a ground-based amateur setting was found. First measure the potential: from `aberration.py` output, if FWHM varies by less than ~10-15% over the field and R/G/B widths agree within ~3%, skip this idea.
- Plug-in: `proper_coadd.py` (PSF fit and combine pass), Phase 3.
- Cost/complexity/risk: M-L; changes a bit-identity-tested path (`tests/test_proper_coadd.py`, native `proper_coadd_scatter_tiles` parity); risk of tile seams in the output PSF; do per-channel first (small), spatial second.
- Confidence: low-medium (mechanism sound, size of gain unknown).

#### Idea 3: Eigen-PSF (PCA) spatially varying RL instead of 3x3 raised-cosine tiles
- What/why: `richardson_lucy_svpsf` blends nine tile results; PSF changes are not continuous and tiles with too few stars fall back to the global PSF. A KL/eigen-PSF forward model is continuous and costs about 4 convolutions per iteration.
- Algorithm: forward model I(x) = sum_k (a_k(x) * O(x)) conv psi_k, k = 1..4; a_k(x) smooth coefficient fields (polynomial in x,y fitted to per-star projections onto eigen-PSFs); precompute FFT(psi_k); RL multiplicative update uses the adjoint, same cost structure as Lauer 2002.
- Source: Lauer 2002 (https://arxiv.org/pdf/astro-ph/0208247); Hirsch et al. 2010 EFF (https://optml.mit.edu/papers/cvpr10.pdf).
- Expected gain: low; RL-SV is opt-in and nn deconvolution is the default path. The same eigen-PSF machinery would however give Idea 1 its continuous kernel interpolation for free, so build it once.
- Plug-in: `psf_deconvolution.py`, new shared helper. Cost S-M. Confidence medium (method is old and proven; benefit to this project small).

## 2. Alt-az specific effects: field-rotation blur, intra-sub drift, elongated stars

### Takeaway
Field rotation about the frame centre makes sub-exposure smear grow linearly with radius and point tangentially; no stacking tool or paper found models it in the PSF — it is absorbed as a rotation term in registration (earlier note) and handled by hardware derotators otherwise. The physics is simple (rate = sidereal rate x cos(lat) x cos(az) / sin(zenith distance)), so a known tangential smear can be added to the per-tile PSF model and used for frame gating; the project already weights frames by measured PSF, so the gain is mostly at the corners and near the zenith.

### Cited Findings
- Field-rotation rate on an alt-az mount: dp/dt = Omega * cos(phi) * cos(A) / sin(z), with Omega = 7.29e-5 rad/s, phi latitude, A azimuth, z zenith angle; the IRAIT design paper gives the same dependence as -0.262 cos(phi) cos(A) / sin(Z) rad/hr — [Keck NIRSPEC design note (snippet only)](https://irlab.astro.ucla.edu/instruments/nirspec/docs/Design/Cryomech/NCDN0100.pdf); [IRAIT, astro-ph/0609624 (snippet only)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/astro-ph/0609624). One other web page gave a different expression (sin A, cos(lat) in the denominator) that does not match the derivation, so use the Keck/IRAIT form — [LambdaGeeks (contradicting)](https://lambdageeks.com/?p=257593)
- Hardware derotation requires counter-rotating the camera; amateur alt-az practice without it relies on registration of rotated frames; planetary tools (AutoStakkert, WinJUPOS) derotate using ephemerides — summarised in the earlier note [registration_alignment.md]; [Cloudy Nights thread](https://www.cloudynights.com/topic/764538-field-rotation-alt-az-tracking-software-to-de-rotate/)
- Anisotropic PSF fixed in the optics is what PSF-modelling pipelines fit in focal-plane coordinates; Piff models in sky coordinates and uses the full focal plane — [arXiv:2306.07996 (snippet only)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2306.07996)
- Infante-Sainz et al. rotate their empirical PSF to the survey's drift direction before subtracting it from each star, i.e. a feature fixed in the instrument frame is oriented per field — [Infante-Sainz et al. 2020](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1911.01430)
- Euclid MER found that resampling with a bilinear instead of a Lanczos-3 kernel broadens the PSFs by an extra 15% (relevant to why the native Lanczos-3 warp matters) — [arXiv:2503.15305 (snippet only)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2503.15305)

### Inferences
- Smear length at radius r (px) for a sub of length t: L = r * omega * t, omega from the formula above; direction tangential to the circle about the rotation centre (the sensor centre for an on-axis optical train, not the target). Example (my arithmetic, assumptions flagged: latitude 40 deg, azimuth 180 deg, altitude 60 deg, t = 10 s): omega = 7.29e-5 * 0.766 / 0.5 = 1.1e-4 rad/s, so L = 1.1 px per 1000 px of radius; towards the zenith sin(z) -> 0 and omega blows up. Origin's sub length and sensor half-diagonal were not found in the repo or in sources, so plug in real values from the headers; the project note that the cross-session rotation *model* over-predicted by an order of magnitude on Origin data (`cli._predict_rotation_spread` docstring) means the rate should be MEASURED from consecutive frames' registration rotation / time gap, not predicted.
- The existing per-frame elliptical Moffat fit captures only the field-average of that smear; the corner stars are smeared more and the centre stars less. This is a well-defined, known-shape error that costs no training data to model.

#### Idea 5: Measured rotation-and-drift smear as a PSF term and a frame gate
- What/why: (i) add the known tangential line-kernel (length r*omega*t) and drift line-kernel (frame-to-frame shift velocity x t) to the position-dependent PSF used by Idea 1/2; (ii) predict per frame, from header time stamps and transforms, the corner smear in pixels and log it in `--session-report` / `frame_report`, downweighting or flagging frames above ~0.7 px; (iii) deblur is NOT recommended as a separate step: for lengths of 0.3-2 px, folding the kernel into the PSF used by proper coadd or the Wiener kernel is better conditioned than a stand-alone 1-D deconvolution.
- Algorithm: from `session_report.collect_session_table` (already holds rotation/shift per frame): omega_meas = d(rotation)/dt between consecutive subs; drift v = d(shift)/dt; per tile centre (x, y) relative to the rotation centre c: smear vector s = omega_meas * t_exp * R90 (x - c) + v * t_exp; kernel = line of length |s| along s, convolved into the node PSF (sub-pixel line rasterisation, 1-D box). For Idea 1 the stack's node PSF already contains the average smear, so apply (i) only to per-frame models (Idea 2).
- Source: formula above (Keck, IRAIT); proper-coadd weighting (Zackay & Ofek, https://arxiv.org/abs/1512.06872). The assembly is my own inference; no paper found that does it for deep-sky stacking.
- Expected gain: small except near the zenith or long subs; the diagnostic (ii) is cheap and useful for telling users why corners are soft.
- Plug-in: `session_report.py`, `frame_report.py`, `observing_geometry.py` (rate cross-check), optionally `proper_coadd.py`. Cost S (diagnostic) / M (PSF term). Confidence: medium that the physics is right, low-medium on gain.

### Gaps
- No paper on within-sub field-rotation smear for deep-sky stacking was found (searched alt-az field rotation, corner blur); only exposure-limit calculators and hardware derotators.
- Origin sub-exposure length and sensor geometry were not located in the repo or in sources; the example numbers are illustrative.
- No source on deblurring elongated stars with a known motion kernel in astrophotography specifically.

## 3. Bright-star handling: saturated cores, halos, bloat, spikes, ghosts/reflections, PSF-subtraction, learned star removal

### Takeaway
The directly applicable professional method is the extended-PSF subtraction of Infante-Sainz et al. (core/intermediate/outer empirical PSF spliced at junction radii, scaled per star against Gaia-catalogued bright stars, brightest first, two passes). It replaces the Gaussian `--halo-removal`. Spikes and reflection ghosts are tied to the instrument, not the sky: survey pipelines predict them from the optical model (ray tracing, LSSTCam/DECam) or mask them per exposure; on an alt-az stack they appear as arcs, and the right place to model them is the sensor frame. No peer-reviewed method for *removing* (rather than detecting/masking) diffraction spikes was found.

### Cited Findings
- Extended PSF recipe (SDSS): ~1000 stars per filter; three regimes — outer (stars brighter than ~7 mag normalised in a 1-pixel ring at 60"), intermediate (~8.3-9.0 mag, ring at 7"), core (unsaturated stars fainter than 14 mag, 2D Moffat centring, ring at 2"); 3-sigma-clipped median stacks of recentred, neighbour-masked cutouts; second pass after subtracting the first PSF; junction by scaling and offset, f = (O(+1) - O(-1)) / (I(+1) - I(-1)), c = O(-1) - I(-1) * f, at a radius where S/N matches; PSF normalised to sum 1; extends to 8 arcmin with 20 mag dynamic range, outer slope alpha ~ -2.49 — [Infante-Sainz et al. 2020](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1911.01430)
- Same paper, Coma example: Gaia DR2 stars with G < 13 are modelled brightest first; scale factor = 3-sigma-clipped median of pixel ratios star/PSF over the range from 0.1x saturation level to 3x background; two passes with masks rebuilt after bright stars are removed; leftover residuals are in saturated cores, a bump from internal reflections near 25", and uncorrected diffraction spikes — [Infante-Sainz et al. 2020](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1911.01430)
- HSC: saturated/defect pixels are repaired by linear predictive coding during CCD processing and not masked on the coadd; a BRIGHT_OBJECT plane comes from geometric regions around catalogue stars; "the main Wide-layer failure is missing some satellite trails and optical ghosts that overlap bright objects" — [Bosch et al. 2018](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1705.06766). Bright-star masks are built from Gaia and Tycho-2 with radii set by the cross-correlation of stars and detected sources per magnitude bin — [Coupon et al. 2018 (snippet only)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2018PASJ...70S...7C)
- Legacy Surveys: stellar cores are masked, not interpolated; larger residuals "associated with very extended galaxies and the halos of bright, saturated stars"; spurious sources arise from "scattered light halos or diffraction spikes"; pupil-ghost subtraction is a calibration step (BASS g band only) — [Legacy Surveys overview](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1804.08657)
- Ghost prediction from the optics: LSSTCam uses the Yale Bright Star Catalog, magnitude transforms and the Batoid ray tracer to get each ghost's morphology; flux = R_A * R_B * f_src with ~2% reflectance per surface; 0.57% of the focal plane affected on average over ~3,100 visits (u-band about 8%) — [LSSTCam ghosts, arXiv:2606.31826](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2606.31826). DES used sequential ray tracing with reflectivities calibrated on ~100 images, found it misses many ghosts (snippet only) — [arXiv:2105.10524](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2105.10524). A Subaru poster describes cometary ghosts from planar surfaces near the focal plane, ghost position estimated from the generating star's position by the optics model, and masking on individual frames removing them from coadds; positional error of several tens of pixels, so masks are ~2x the true area (snippet only; poster PDF unreadable) — [Komiyama poster](https://subarutelescope.org/Archive/Science/SubaruUM/SubaruUM2016/files/poster/P06_Komiyama.pdf)
- K-DRIFT study: ghosts replicate as the camera rotates, forming a chain-like pattern; authors suggest subtracting ghosts or deconvolving the PSF (snippet only) — [arXiv:2504.14914](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2504.14914)
- Learned work is detection/masking only: DeepGhostBusters (Mask R-CNN, 2000 annotated DES images, beats a ray-tracing baseline) masks ghosts and scattered light but does not remove them — [arXiv:2109.08246](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2109.08246); MaxiMask builds rotated spike-cutout libraries to train CNN contaminant masks (snippet only) — [arXiv:1907.08298](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1907.08298)
- Amateur diagnosis of artifact origin: rotate the camera; true optical spikes rotate with the field, sensor artifacts stay fixed to the chip; ring/halo threads attribute rings to re-reflection between sensor and camera window/filter, with one RASA 8 owner reporting residual halos from the camera's protective glass; all are forum anecdotes — [AstroBin spike thread](https://app.astrobin.com/forum/topic/206848/what-could-cause-this-bizarre-diffraction-spike-effect), [AstroBin artifact thread](https://app.astrobin.com/forum/topic/10930/help-with-an-image-artifact/), [AstroBin bright-star thread](https://app.astrobin.com/forum/topic/19012/advice-with-problem-on-bright-stars)
- Learned star removal baselines (StarXTerminator, StarNet2, Cosmic Clarity, BlurXTerminator flux claims 2-5% vs 75% in the older version) are covered in the earlier note; no independent photometric validation of any of them was found — [RC Astro](https://www.rc-astro.com/blurxterminator-2-0-ai4-release/), [StarNet release notes](https://starnetastro.com/release-notes/)
- Photutils: PSF fitting yields a model image and a residual image (`make_model_image`, `make_residual_image`); noise-like residuals indicate good fits — [photutils PSF guide](https://docs.astropy.org/projects/photutils/en/stable/user_guide/psf.html)

### Inferences
- The project's open items (symmetric halo only in the removal path, spikes/rings left, saturated core refill from a Moffat wing) all reduce to one missing asset: an EMPIRICAL, extended, per-channel star template with the sensor-fixed asymmetric part separated from the radially symmetric part. Two stages follow naturally: symmetric (cheap, rotation-invariant, Idea 6) and asymmetric (needs the sensor-frame trick, Idea 7).
- "Needs the optics' orientation per star" (runbook) has a clean answer: the orientation is the same sensor-frame orientation for every star in every sub; only the per-frame rotation differs, and the transforms are already stored. Forward-modelling the stack's version of the template is the CoaddPsf idea (Bosch / Jee & Tyson) applied to a bright-star template rather than to a PSF at a point.
- Gaia is already in the project (`gaia_cache`, `color_calibrate`), so predicted flux of every bright star, including those just outside the frame, is available; Infante-Sainz had to use USNO-B because Gaia is incomplete below ~7 mag (an issue only for the very brightest stars).

#### Idea 6: Gaia-anchored empirical extended-PSF halo subtraction (replace Gaussian `--halo-removal`)
- What/why: Removes the symmetric scattered-light halo/bloat of bright stars (and of stars just outside the field) from the linear stack before DBE, so the background model, denoiser and stretch do not see a bowl of light around each bright star; also gives a physically derived mask radius per star. The current Gaussian halo cannot represent the power-law aureole (alpha ~ -2.5 in SDSS) and only touches the brightest 5%.
- Algorithm:
  1. Template per channel T_c(r) (radially symmetric; Origin's channel difference is chromatic, bluer core, redder halo per the runbook). Build from the session stack: core regime from unsaturated stars (S/N > 50) with ePSF centring (Idea 4); intermediate from stars whose peak is 0.5-1.0x ceiling, normalised in a ring just outside the core regime; outer from clearly saturated stars, normalised in a ring where their profile is unsaturated; cutouts radius R about 200 px; 3-sigma-clipped median of recentred cutouts after masking neighbours (existing detections grown by profile radius); pass 2 after subtracting pass-1 template. Splice by f = (O+ - O-)/(I+ - I-), c = O- - I- f at a radius of comparable S/N. Because the optics are fixed, a shipped default per camera in `src/data/camera_profiles/Origin178.json` with a session refit of amplitude is plausible (halo changes with dew/dirt/focus, so refit scale).
  2. Fit per star, brightest first: F = sigma-clipped (3 sigma) median over annulus pixels r_in..r_out of (I - sky) / T, with r_in = just outside pixels above 0.9 x ceiling (or the Moffat core), r_out where F*T falls below 1 sky sigma; per channel; flux-prior from Gaia G and the camera colour slopes (`camera_profile.py`) for stars out of frame or with contaminated annuli (ZP from in-frame stars).
  3. Subtract F*T_c from the linear stack with a fade-in over r_in..1.5 r_in; floor so no pixel is driven below the local sky by more than 1 sigma; skip stars inside extended-structure masks (`--galaxy-mode`, bright-nebula flag already used by `fill_saturated`).
  4. Second pass after rebuilding masks, as in the source.
  5. Use r_k(F) = smallest r with F*T(r) < k*sigma_sky as the star-mask radius for DBE, denoising protection and `remove_stars` (replaces fixed FWHM multiples; HSC does the same with magnitude-dependent radii).
- Source: Infante-Sainz et al. 2020 (https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1911.01430); mask radii from Coupon et al. 2018 (https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2018PASJ...70S...7C).
- Expected gain/evidence: Infante-Sainz demonstrate scattered-light removal on Coma but report no numeric residual amplitude and note remaining reflection bumps and spikes; so the evidence is qualitative. Acceptance test: the project's own halo-ring metric (`ring x sky_noise` in `dev-notes/nn-deconv-runbook.md`) and `tools/bench_phase4.py` on Orion/Pleiades/Sunflower stacks.
- Plug-in: `denoising.remove_star_halos` -> new module (e.g. `star_halo_model.py`), Phase 4 step at the existing halo position in `postprocess.py` (before background extraction), reuses `star_n2n.fill_saturated`'s radial profile code and `color_calibrate`'s Gaia flux fits. Add to `uncertainty._QUIET_OFF` if it writes a template side file.
- Cost/complexity/risk: M. Risks: over-subtracting real nebulosity under halos (clipped median + masks + fade-in), template contamination by crowded fields (two-pass masking), halo varying with focus/dew (refit amplitude and optionally a per-session power-law slope).
- Confidence: medium-high that it removes halos; medium on visible benefit on Origin data.

#### Idea 7: Sensor-frame spike / ring / ghost template, in three stages
- What/why: Handles the two artifacts the runbook says remain: diffraction spikes and reflection rings. They are fixed in the instrument (sensor) frame, so in a stack of field-rotated subs they smear into arcs/fans around bright stars; each sky pixel is contaminated in only some frames, which is exactly what per-frame masking and forward modelling exploit.
- Algorithm:
  - Stage 0 (diagnostic, S, do first): pick the 10-30 brightest stars; for each cut a 201x201 stamp in SENSOR coordinates from every debayered Phase 1 frame; (a) stack aligning on the star centroid only (no rotation) -> S_sensor; (b) stack after applying each frame's rotation -> S_sky. Compare spike/ring contrast: the frame in which they are sharper is the frame they are fixed in (the amateur "rotate the camera" test, automated). Also bin stamps by the star's sensor position (e.g. 3x3) and measure the ghost-ring centre offset against the star position: fit offset = a + B * (x - x0) (planar-reflection ghost; the Subaru poster and LSSTCam work both predict ghost position as a deterministic function of the generating star's position). If rings are circles centred on the star regardless of position, skip the position model.
  - Stage 1 (combine-time masks, M): from the sensor-frame template T_s (per channel, normalised by star flux, splice as in Idea 6), for each frame j and each star with F above a threshold, mark pixels where F*T_s(x - x_star + ghost offset) > kappa * sigma_pix (kappa ~ 0.7) and give them zero weight in that frame's combine (reuse the per-frame mask hook used by `trail_reject`/proper-coadd cleaning). Mask area x2 to allow position error (Subaru practice). Efficiency rises with total session rotation; for small rotation the features stay and Stage 2 is needed.
  - Stage 2 (forward-model subtraction, L): model_stack(x) = sum_j w_j * T_s(R_j(x - x_star)) / sum_j w_j, with R_j the stored per-frame rotation (and ghost offsets per frame), amplitude by clipped-median ratio in the feature annulus as in Idea 6, subtract in the linear stack (including `fill_saturated` use for `--remove-stars-method nn`).
- Source: HSC/Subaru ghost workflow (catalogue search, ghost prediction, pixel masking, coadd of masked exposures) — [Komiyama poster (snippet only)](https://subarutelescope.org/Archive/Science/SubaruUM/SubaruUM2016/files/poster/P06_Komiyama.pdf); optical-model ghost prediction — [LSSTCam ghosts](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2606.31826); coadd PSF propagation rule — [Bosch et al. 2018](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1705.06766); spike/ring leftovers in extended-PSF subtraction — [Infante-Sainz et al. 2020](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1911.01430); feature-rotates-with-camera test — [AstroBin](https://app.astrobin.com/forum/topic/206848/what-could-cause-this-bizarre-diffraction-spike-effect).
- Expected gain/evidence: Good physical argument and professional precedent for masking per exposure; no published numeric gain for spikes on amateur stacks, and no published method that subtracts spikes (only detects/masks them). Acceptance test: radial/azimuthal profile at 20-150 px around the brightest stars in the stack, before/after; the runbook's `diag_star_blobs.py` colour-deviation ranking.
- Plug-in: Stage 0 as `tools/diag_sensor_frame_artifacts.py`; Stage 1 in `frame_processor.py` Phase 1 (masks) + `stacking.py`/`proper_coadd.py` weights; Stage 2 in Phase 3/4 next to `star_n2n.fill_saturated`. Transforms already stored by `registration.py`.
- Cost/complexity/risk: S for Stage 0, M for Stage 1, L for Stage 2. Risk: if the features turn out to be sky-fixed (unlikely for optics) the premise fails and Stage 0 will say so cheaply; ghost position model may not be linear in star position; low-rotation sessions leave residuals.
- Confidence: medium that the premise holds (it follows from optics being attached to the sensor); medium-low on Stage 2 effort/benefit.

#### Idea 8: Empirical-PSF core refill with a Gaia flux anchor (improve `star_repair.py`)
- What/why: The Moffat-wing fit extrapolates into a clipped core; the true profile of an obstructed-pupil fast system is not a Moffat, and the wing fit alone can trade amplitude against width. Using (a) the empirical profile from Idea 6/4 for shape and (b) the Gaia-predicted flux as an amplitude constraint (flux conservation: sum of model = F_Gaia-calibrated) removes the degeneracy. The Moffat refill keeps working as a fallback when no template exists.
- Algorithm: per channel, minimise sum over unsaturated pixels in r_in..r_out of (I - sky - A*T_c)^2 / var, with A bounded within +-15% of A_Gaia = ZP-calibrated flux / sum(T_c) (prior as a soft penalty), then refill pixels above 0.9 x ceiling with A*T_c plus sky noise (the runbook's `fill_saturated` already adds correlated noise).
- Source: Infante-Sainz et al. 2020 for the template-fit procedure (https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1911.01430); Gaia use as in HSC masks (https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2018PASJ...70S...7C). Gaia-flux-as-prior is my adaptation.
- Expected gain: modest; improves core roundness/colour for the few brightest stars. Confidence low-medium.
- Plug-in: `star_repair.py` (`_fit_moffat_wing` call site), Phase 4. Cost S-M (after Idea 6).

### Gaps
- No peer-reviewed algorithm that subtracts (rather than detects or masks) diffraction spikes from ground-based imaging was found.
- No source documents the Origin/RASA-type reflection geometry; amateur threads attribute rings to sensor-window re-reflection, none confirm it for this optical train. The cause must be established with the Stage 0 diagnostic.
- HDR core reconstruction from unsaturated short sub-exposures was not researched in depth; Origin sessions use fixed-length subs, so no short-sub data exist to exploit.
- No independent photometric-fidelity benchmark for learned star removal (StarNet/StarXTerminator) was found; the project's own injection-recovery metrics (`dev-notes/star-n2n-runbook.md`) are the only quantitative standard in sight.
- Komiyama (Subaru), K-DRIFT, Coupon, DES ray-tracing statements are from search summaries only.

## 4. Crowded fields and deblending (DAOPHOT / photutils / Tractor) for detection, masks and star colour

### Takeaway
Grouped simultaneous PSF fitting with iterative residual detection (Stetson-style, as in photutils `IterativePSFPhotometry`) is the standard answer to blended stars and is available as open-source Python; Tractor-style joint fitting across all exposures is the professional ideal but conflicts with the stack-then-process design. For OriginStack the useful, cheap parts are the grouped fit (for a model-based, exactly additive star layer and for deblended per-channel star colours) and the empirical ePSF that makes the model fit well.

### Cited Findings
- `PSFPhotometry` finds, optionally groups (`SourceGrouper`, distance criterion following Stetson), fits the PSF model to each source or group simultaneously, and subtracts the models; `IterativePSFPhotometry` repeats on the residual image detecting new sources each pass; `fit_shape` is the fitted region (small, e.g. 5x5, for the S/N-rich core), `aperture_radius` seeds fluxes; very large groups are costly and error-prone — [photutils PSF guide](https://docs.astropy.org/projects/photutils/en/stable/user_guide/psf.html)
- The ePSF builder follows Anderson & King (2000) and Anderson (2016) and differs from the original in centring (peak-based rather than symmetry-enforcing); Godden & Blundell (RASTI, arXiv Dec 2025) show that oversampling, interpolation, gridpoint estimation, smoothing, star distribution and dithering all change ePSF accuracy and the resulting pixel-phase flux/position errors, and that refined routines recover better ePSFs on real data — [photutils ePSF guide (snippet)](https://photutils.readthedocs.io/en/3.0.0/user_guide/epsf_building.html); [Godden & Blundell, arXiv:2512.16764](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.16764)
- The Tractor: generative forward model producing pixel-space predictions with a chi-squared likelihood against the data; early abstracts say it "never" needs to co-add, degrade or reject images; in the Legacy Surveys each source is a small parametric profile (point source, exponential, de Vaucouleurs, composite) fit across all images — [ASCL 1604.008](https://ascl.net/1604.008), [Legacy Surveys overview](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1804.08657)
- Euclid MER assumes a band's PSF does not change with the source's colour (documented limitation), i.e. colour-dependent PSF is a known unsolved nuisance in homogenised photometry — [arXiv:2503.15305 (snippet only)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2503.15305)

### Inferences
- Project check: detection is matched-filter (`star_detect.py`); star colour is measured by aperture photometry (`photometry_core.aperture_photometry_batch`) in `color_calibrate`; `remove_stars` uses inpainting or the N2N net. None of these separate blended pairs or fit a PSF; in dense fields (Orion, M24, Omega) aperture colours of blended pairs and the footprints of merged stars are biased.
- Origin's PSF is chromatic (runbook); PSF-fitting photometry with per-channel PSFs gives noise-weighted per-channel amplitudes, which is a better "true star colour" than aperture sums for both Gaia-based colour calibration and star recolouring.
- Tractor-style multi-frame forward modelling is not recommended: it needs per-frame PSF models and heavy compute, and the deliverable here is a processed stack, not a catalogue. Legacy Surveys itself keeps stacks for detection.

#### Idea 4: Empirical effective PSF (ePSF) as the project's shared PSF model
- What/why: Moffat misses Airy-ring structure of obstructed pupils and non-power-law wings; an oversampled empirical PSF (4x) built from the stack's stars at random sub-pixel phases is the standard cure and is a prerequisite for good PSF subtraction (Ideas 1, 6, 8, 9).
- Algorithm (Anderson & King): select 100-500 isolated, unsaturated, S/N > 50 stars; iterate: (1) fit star positions/fluxes with the current ePSF (start Gaussian); (2) shift each star stamp onto the oversampled grid by its fitted sub-pixel position, divide by flux, accumulate in the 4x grid with sigma-clipped (3 sigma) averaging; (3) smooth with a quartic kernel; (4) recentre; repeat 5-10 iterations. Spatial variation: node grid of ePSFs (`GriddedPSFModel`-like) or Idea 3's eigen-PSF coefficients. Per channel. Photutils `EPSFBuilder` can be used directly if photutils is allowed (it was deliberately removed from detection; the builder is only needed offline or in an optional guarded import per OS002); a numpy re-implementation is ~150 lines.
- Source: Anderson & King 2000 via photutils docs (https://photutils.readthedocs.io/en/3.0.0/user_guide/epsf_building.html), Godden & Blundell 2025 (https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.16764).
- Expected gain: indirect (enabler). Confidence medium. Cost M; stamps small, numpy suffices, Rust not required. New module `epsf.py`, consumers: `star_repair.py`, `star_halo_model.py`, and `psf_deconvolution` (which already has `estimate_psf_blind`: a median of star cutouts with a Tukey radial apodisation, `psf_deconvolution.py:235-330`; what it lacks, and the ePSF adds, is sub-pixel/oversampled construction and spatial variation).

#### Idea 9: Grouped simultaneous PSF fits for a model-based star layer and deblended star colour
- What/why: (i) A third `--remove-stars-method psf`: fit ePSFs to all stars with S/N > ~10 in groups (Stetson grouping, separation ~ 2.5 x PSF radius), subtract; the star layer is exactly additive and flux-preserving, with residual inspection built in; N2N/inpainting handles stars below the fit threshold and the cores of saturated ones. (ii) Use the fitted per-channel amplitudes as "true" star colours for `color_calibrate` (cleaner than apertures in crowded fields) and for Idea 10.
- Algorithm: for each iteration, detect on the residual (existing matched filter), group by distance, fit per group by Levenberg-Marquardt on (flux_c, x, y) with shared positions across channels, fit_shape ~ (11, 11) at 4x ePSF; subtract; stop when no new detections above 5 sigma or maxiters 3-5; report chi^2/dof per star to flag bad fits (variable background, galaxies).
- Source: photutils `IterativePSFPhotometry`/`SourceGrouper` docs (https://docs.astropy.org/projects/photutils/en/stable/user_guide/psf.html), Stetson's DAOPHOT grouping as described there.
- Expected gain: modest vs the existing N2N (which already passes its checks); the benefit is exactness and crowded-field colour. Cost M-L (needs Idea 4); risk of slow fits in dense fields (use the native optimiser path or limit to the brightest few thousand). Confidence medium-low.
- Plug-in: `star_removal.py` (method switch), `color_calibrate.py`/`photometric_calibration.py` (flux source).

### Gaps
- No evidence quantifies aperture-vs-PSF colour bias on Origin crowded fields; it must be measured (Gaia colour vs measured colour scatter, aperture vs PSF).
- DAOPHOT's original papers were not consulted directly; the grouping description comes from the photutils documentation.
- Tractor's methodology paper is "in preparation" per the Legacy Surveys overview, so only secondary descriptions were available.

## 5. Fast Schmidt-type optics and Bayer sensors: reflections, ghosts, colour fringing, CFA aliasing of tight stars

### Takeaway
Lateral chromatic aberration is a radial magnification difference between channels and is corrected by photographic tools as a per-channel radial polynomial (Hugin/Lensfun poly3), whereas OriginStack applies one global shift; this is a cheap, testable improvement. Ghost/reflection handling in professional pipelines is predict-and-mask per exposure (Idea 7). For Bayer aliasing of tight stars no astro-specific source exists; the best-supported mitigation inside this project's own findings is to constrain a star's chroma to a smooth radial profile (what the N2N colour constraint already does) and to apply it to all stars.

### Cited Findings
- Hugin/Lensfun TCA model per colour channel relative to green: Rd = a*Ru^4 + b*Ru^3 + c*Ru^2 + v*Ru (Hugin, quartic) and Rd = b*Ru^3 + c*Ru^2 + v*Ru (Lensfun poly3, a = 0), with v near 1 (example red 0.99999, blue 1.00065) and the parameters estimated automatically from a single image of a high-contrast scene — [Lensfun TCA tutorial](https://lensfun.github.io/calibration-tutorial/lens-tca.html)
- Lensfun's inverse mapping uses a Newton iteration (third-party port documentation, notes an ambiguity in coefficient naming between source comments) — [lensfun crate docs (snippet only)](https://docs.rs/crate/lensfun/0.7.0/source/src/mod_subpix.rs)
- Ghost causes in imaging systems: reflections between planar surfaces near the focal plane (filters, window, CCD) produce defocused images of the star whose position follows the star position — [Komiyama poster (snippet only)](https://subarutelescope.org/Archive/Science/SubaruUM/SubaruUM2016/files/poster/P06_Komiyama.pdf), [LSSTCam ghosts](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2606.31826); amateur reports link bright-star rings to sensor/cover-glass/filter re-reflection, with ring size implying surfaces about 9 mm apart in one thread and horizontal/vertical preference suggesting the sensor structure itself acting as a grating in another (forum anecdotes) — [AstroBin artifact thread](https://app.astrobin.com/forum/topic/10930/help-with-an-image-artifact/), [AstroBin bright-star thread](https://app.astrobin.com/forum/topic/19012/advice-with-problem-on-bright-stars)
- Bayer artifacts: general demosaicing literature attributes coloured artifacts to under-sampling of fine detail (patent/survey snippets); no astrophotography source on sub-pixel-phase colour of point sources was found — [Demosaicing background (snippet only)](https://webee.technion.ac.il/Sites/People/YoninaEldar/files/508.pdf)

### Inferences
- The project's CA step (`debayer.measure_chromatic_aberration`) measures one translation of R and B against G over the whole frame. Real lateral CA grows with field radius; a pure translation can null the centre or the mean but cannot remove the radial term, leaving red/blue fringes at the corners where it matters most (also fast, strongly curved fields). A direct test uses data the project already has: per-channel star centroids from the matched-filter list, regressed against position.
- Per-frame Bayer aliasing is largely averaged out by dither and field rotation in a stack (random pixel phases), which is consistent with the project's finding that CFA-aware resamplers (cfa_drizzle, kernel regression) were not better than RCD + proper coadd. The residual star-colour error therefore lives in the smooth chroma of the star footprints, which a radial-chroma constraint can fix; that is inference, not sourced.

#### Idea 11: Radial (magnification) lateral-CA model for R and B
- What/why: Replace the global R/B translation by shift + radial polynomial relative to G, estimated session-wide from star centroids.
- Algorithm: on ~10 sample frames (or the stack) take matched-filter stars with S/N > 30 and fit centroids per channel (Gaussian/ePSF fit on each demosaiced channel plane); for channel c in {R, B}: d_c(x) = centroid_c - centroid_G; model d_c(x) = t_c + (x - x0) * [ (v_c - 1) + c_c * rho + b_c * rho^2 ] with rho = |x - x0|/R_max, x0 the optical centre (fit or the frame centre); fit by robust (Huber) least squares with 3 parameters (v, c, b) + translation; apply as a per-channel remap in Phase 1 in place of `apply_chromatic_aberration` (Lanczos/bicubic resample of the channel; note R and B are already interpolated by RCD, so one more resample slightly softens them — measure this; remapping only when the residual displacement exceeds 0.15 px). Keep the result session-constant like the current step (`frame_processor._measure_session_ca`).
- Source: Lensfun/Hugin TCA model (https://lensfun.github.io/calibration-tutorial/lens-tca.html); applying it per channel is standard photographic practice. Fit by star centroids is my adaptation.
- Expected gain/evidence: unknown on Origin; first run only the measurement and print d_c(x) maps with the existing aberration report. If the radial term is below 0.15 px at the corners, drop the idea.
- Plug-in: `debayer.py` CA functions, `frame_processor.py` session CA probe. Cost S (measure) / M (apply). Confidence: low-medium.

#### Idea 10: Per-star radial-chroma regularisation and Gaia recolouring (partly on ROADMAP section 2)
- What/why: Star colour is one number per star at each radius; Bayer phase errors, residual CA and the chromatic PSF (bluer core / redder halo) make the stack's chroma noisy inside footprints, and the white clipped cores read white. `star_n2n._radial_colour` already constrains the star layer's colour per 2 px ring for the removal path; do the same for the visible stars in the main path. Optionally anchor the integrated colour to Gaia BP-RP through the camera's colour slopes (`camera_profile.py`, valid for 6-14 stars in the solar fit).
- Algorithm: for each detected star with S/N > 10: ring medians of (R-G, B-G) in linear units from the ring outside the footprint (background) subtracted, per 1.5 px annulus; smooth with a monotone, low-order (cubic B-spline in r) fit; replace chroma inside the footprint by the fit while preserving luminance (the project's rule: never rebuild RGB as img * new_lum / lum on sky-centred data; use an additive chroma change faded near zero luminance); for Gaia anchoring, scale the whole profile so the integrated (R-G, B-G) equals the Gaia-predicted value when the measured and predicted colours disagree by more than 3 sigma (photometric noise from Idea 9 or aperture errors).
- Source: project's own runbook finding (`dev-notes/star-n2n-runbook.md`, per-ring colour from the star's own profile worked where the integrated colour forced the wrong blue/red), roadmap item in `dev-notes/ROADMAP.md`; Euclid MER's PSF-colour-independence limitation shows the chromatic-PSF issue is real in homogenised photometry (https://arxiv.org/pdf/2503.15305, snippet only). No external published algorithm for amateur star recolouring was found.
- Expected gain: moderate on visible star colour (rank correlation of star colour with the linear stack was 0.51-0.91 in `phase4-quality.md`); confidence medium-low (stretch order interacts: the project notes per-channel stretch whitens cores; judge on `*_lin` metrics).
- Plug-in: Phase 4 step before the stretch (linear), `color_calibrate.py` for Gaia colours. Cost M.

### Not recommended / considered and rejected
- Learned ghost or spike detection (DeepGhostBusters, MaxiMask): needs thousands of annotated frames and gives masks, not removal; the geometric prediction in Idea 7 needs no training data.
- Pre-combination PSF homogenisation or per-frame deconvolution: contradicted by Zackay-Ofek and the project's own proper coadd.
- Diffusion/transformer star restoration: already surveyed in the earlier note; no OSC validation, not laptop-CPU friendly.
- Tractor-style forward modelling of all frames: see Section 4.

### Gaps
- No confirmation of the cause or geometry of Origin's reflection rings and spikes; amateur threads are anecdotal and not specific to the Origin/RASA train.
- No astro source on Bayer-phase colour errors of point sources; the CFA claims are inferences.
- Lensfun coefficient-naming ambiguity (a, b, c, v ordering differs between a third-party port's comments and the tutorial); read upstream `mod-subpix.cpp` before copying formulas.

## 6. Top 3 ideas ranked by benefit / effort

### Takeaway
1) Gaia-anchored empirical extended-PSF halo subtraction (Idea 6, on top of the ePSF helper of Idea 4); 2) the sensor-frame artifact programme starting with the cheap Stage 0 diagnostic and combine-time masks (Idea 7); 3) post-coadd star-footprint PSF homogenisation (Idea 1). Cheap measure-first checks (Idea 2 gate, Idea 11 measurement, Idea 5 diagnostic) should be run alongside because they are small and decide whether further work is worthwhile.

### Cited Findings
- Idea 6 rests on the Infante-Sainz et al. extended-PSF subtraction method (three-regime empirical PSF, Gaia G < 13 stars brightest first, clipped-median scale, two passes) — [Infante-Sainz et al. 2020](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1911.01430)
- Idea 7 rests on professional per-exposure ghost prediction and masking, and on the CoaddPsf propagation rule — [Komiyama poster (snippet only)](https://subarutelescope.org/Archive/Science/SubaruUM/SubaruUM2016/files/poster/P06_Komiyama.pdf), [LSSTCam ghosts](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2606.31826), [Bosch et al. 2018](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1705.06766)
- Idea 1 rests on survey-scale spatially varying kernel homogenisation, applied after coaddition — [Kuijken et al. 2015](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1507.00738), [Boucaud et al. 2016](https://arxiv.org/abs/1609.02006), [Zackay & Ofek](https://arxiv.org/abs/1512.06872)

### Inferences
Ranking (benefit x confidence / effort; my scoring, not sourced):

| Rank | Idea | Benefit | Effort | Confidence | Why it ranks here |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Idea 6 Gaia-anchored empirical extended-PSF halo subtraction (replaces Gaussian `--halo-removal`; needs the ePSF/template helper of Idea 4 in simplified radial form) | Visible: halos and bloat of every bright star, cleaner DBE/denoise around them, physically derived star-mask radii | M | medium-high | Published, proven procedure; reuses Gaia cache, colour-calibration zero point, `fill_saturated` radial code; rotation-invariant so no registration complications; measurable with the existing halo-ring metric |
| 2 | Idea 7 sensor-frame spike/ring/ghost template: Stage 0 diagnostic (S), Stage 1 combine-time masks (M), Stage 2 forward subtraction (L, optional) | Visible: the two artifact types the runbook lists as unsolved | S then M | medium (premise) | Stage 0 is a one-day script that decides whether the whole premise holds; Stage 1 uses existing per-frame mask and weight hooks; field rotation, normally a nuisance, becomes the reason masking works |
| 3 | Idea 1 post-coadd star-footprint PSF homogenisation | Round, uniform, channel-consistent stars; fewer coloured star fringes; uniform input for nn deconvolution | M | medium | Fills a real gap (nothing makes stars uniform); flux-conserving and restricted to footprints so sky noise is untouched; needs the shared PSF map (Idea 4) and the eigen-PSF interpolation (Idea 3) |

Runners-up, deliberately lower: Idea 11 (radial CA; run the measurement first, 1 day, apply only if the corner residual exceeds ~0.15 px), Idea 5 (rotation-smear diagnostic in the session report; cheap and informative), Idea 8 (Gaia-anchored core refill, follows Idea 6), Idea 10 (radial chroma regularisation; overlaps ROADMAP), Idea 9 (grouped PSF fits; useful in crowded fields), Idea 2 (per-channel/spatial PSF in proper coadd; gate on measured field variation), Idea 3 (eigen-PSF RL; mostly as infrastructure).

### Gaps
- All gains are expectations from survey-scale literature or physical argument; none were validated on Origin data. Each idea names an acceptance test using existing tools (`tools/bench_phase4.py`, halo-ring metric, `aberration.py`, `diag_star_blobs.py`, Pan-STARRS injection-recovery in the runbooks).
- Training-data needs: none of the ranked ideas needs training data; Idea 6 and 1 need a per-camera template/PSF library that can be rebuilt from any session's stars.
