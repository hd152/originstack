## Self-supervised and zero-shot denoising usable on a single stack or on sub-stacks (N2V, N2S, Neighbor2Neighbor, Noisier2Noise, R2R, ZS-N2N, Noise2Score, SURE): which are worth having here?

### Takeaway
The project already holds the strongest member of this family for its data: a Noise2Noise U-Net trained on real independent quarter-stacks, plus Noisier2Noise for star removal. The single-image methods (N2V, N2S, Neighbor2Neighbor, ZS-N2N, AP-BSN, Noise2Score) all assume pixel-wise independent noise, which Origin stacks do not have (lag-1 noise correlation 0.41), so they are not recommended; the only genuinely new item is Recorrupted-to-Recorrupted (R2R) as an offline way to make camera-specific training pairs from one deep stack with a measured coloured-noise PSD (I-7, speculative).

### Cited Findings
- Project baseline (read from the repo): `--denoiser nn` is a Noise2Noise U-Net trained on quarter-stacks (`i % 4 == q`) of real sessions, numpy inference, "no torch at run time" — [src/nn_denoise.py](C:\source\originstack-research\src\nn_denoise.py). Star removal uses Noisier2Noise — [dev-notes/star-n2n-runbook.md](C:\source\originstack-research\dev-notes\star-n2n-runbook.md). Noise2Self calibration, NLM, BM3D and MMT were removed — [CLAUDE.md](C:\source\originstack-research\CLAUDE.md).
- The project measured the real stack noise (input minus its denoised self, empty-sky patches) at lag-1 correlation 0.41 and wrote "Uncorrelated noise will not transfer" — [dev-notes/nn-deconv-runbook.md](C:\source\originstack-research\dev-notes\nn-deconv-runbook.md). The NN's docstring states its residual error is "~20x smaller than the wavelet denoiser's" — [src/nn_denoise.py](C:\source\originstack-research\src\nn_denoise.py).
- Zero-Shot Noise2Noise (Mansour and Heckel, CVPR 2023) trains a simple 2-layer network on one image with no data; the authors state it "works well for denoising pixel-wise independent noise" and "often outperforms existing dataset-free methods at a reduced cost" (the abstract gives no numbers or parameter count) — [arXiv:2303.11253](https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.11253).
- Noise2Self (Batson and Royer, 2019): J-invariant denoisers can be calibrated from noisy data alone, under the assumption that noise is statistically independent across dimensions of the measurement — [arXiv:1901.11365](https://arxiv.org/abs/1901.11365).
- Noise2Void (Krull et al., CVPR 2019) trains a blind-spot network on the very data it denoises with a small accuracy loss versus supervised; N2V2 exists to fix its checkerboard artifacts — [CVPR 2019 page](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content_CVPR_2019/html/Krull_Noise2Void_-_Learning_Denoising_From_Single_Noisy_Images_CVPR_2019_paper.html), [juglab/n2v](https://github.com/juglab/n2v).
- Neighbor2Neighbor (Huang et al., CVPR 2021) builds training pairs from one noisy image with a random neighbour sub-sampler plus a regulariser; it was validated on synthetic sRGB noise and a raw-RGB benchmark (abstract gives no numbers) — [arXiv:2101.02824](https://arxiv.org/abs/2101.02824).
- AP-BSN (Lee, Son, Lee, CVPR 2022) uses pixel-shuffle down-sampling (PD) to remove spatial correlation of real noise so a blind-spot network can be used, with different PD strides for training and inference; blind-spot networks "assume pixel-wise independent noise" — [arXiv:2203.11799](https://arxiv.org/abs/2203.11799).
- StructN2V (Broaddus et al., ISBI 2020) extends the blind spot to a larger blind mask shaped to the noise structure so spatially correlated ("structured") noise can be removed without a noise model or ground truth (microscopy) — [EPFL record](https://infoscience.epfl.ch/record/281180). Details of how the mask is chosen could not be retrieved.
- Noise2Score (Kim and Ye, NeurIPS 2021) uses Tweedie's formula with a learned score for any exponential-family noise (Gaussian, Poisson, Gamma) — [arXiv:2106.07009](https://arxiv.org/abs/2106.07009).
- Recorrupted-to-Recorrupted (Pang et al., CVPR 2021) builds noisy/noisy pairs from a single noisy image by re-corrupting it; the authors prove the loss on those pairs is statistically equivalent to the supervised loss, and it also works for signal-dependent noise — [CVPR 2021](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/CVPR2021/html/Pang_Recorrupted-to-Recorrupted_Unsupervised_Deep_Learning_for_Image_Denoising_CVPR_2021_paper.html).
- Siril's own documentation says its denoisers (NL-Bayes) "are designed for white Gaussian noise" and that correlated noise "is not handled well: OSC images after debayering and chrominance noise do not fit the white-noise model" — [Siril docs](https://siril.readthedocs.io/en/latest/processing/denoising.html).
- Resampling correlates noise: Drizzle "frequently divides the power from a given input pixel between several output pixels. As a result, the noise in adjacent pixels will be correlated", and per-pixel noise measured on the output grid "underestimates the noise on larger scales" (correction factor R = 1/(1 - r/3) for r = pixfrac/scale <= 1) — [Fruchter and Hook, ar5iv](https://ar5iv.arxiv.org/html/astro-ph/9808087).
- AstroSURE (Apr 2026) reports gains on HST after "domain-consistent initialization" but more limited transfer to CFHT — already summarised in the earlier note [denoising_psf_deconvolution.md](C:\source\originstack-research\research_notes\Astrophotography stacking techniques since 2022\denoising_psf_deconvolution.md) ([arXiv:2604.16793](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.16793)).

### Inferences
- Because N2N needs two independent noisy copies of the same sky and OriginStack can always split frames (quarter-stacks, odd/even), a method that fabricates a pair from one image (N2V/N2S/Neighbor2Neighbor/ZS-N2N/R2R) can only win where no split is available: a `--from-stack` input produced by other software, a merged/hierarchical output, or a camera for which no trained weights exist. None of the blind-spot variants handles the 0.41 lag-1 correlation without PD (which discards the fine scales that matter for stars) or a structured blind mask (StructN2V), and both would be trained per image, which the "numpy at run time" design cannot do.
- **I-7. R2R with a measured coloured-noise PSD for camera-specific NN weights (offline, speculative).**
  - What/why: for a camera where the Origin-trained U-Net transfers poorly (ROADMAP section 1: DSLR, mono, narrowband), make N2N-equivalent pairs from one deep stack instead of needing many split-half sessions.
  - Algorithm: measure the stack noise sample e = y - nn_denoise(y) on empty sky (the repo already does this in `tools/gen_synth_deconv.py`), estimate its PSD P(f) (Welch), draw n = IFFT(sqrt(P) * W) with W white Gaussian, form y1 = y + D n and y2 = y - n / D with D in about 0.5-1. Cov(y1 noise, y2 noise) = Sigma - Sigma = 0 for Gaussian n with the same covariance as the stack noise, so the pair is independent and both are centred on the clean signal (this is the R2R construction; the paper's inference-time protocol was not verified here). Train the existing U-Net on (y1 -> y2) in noise units.
  - Source: R2R link above; PSD construction is the same idea as the project's own Noisier2Noise star work.
  - Expected gain: unknown; removes the need for independent sub-stacks per camera but inherits non-Gaussian stack noise (hot-pixel remnants, trail residue) which breaks the Gaussian assumption.
  - Plug-in: `tools/train_nn_denoise.py` (torch, training only, exempt from the Rust-for-performance rule); no run-time change.
  - Cost/risk: L (training, validation on held-out sessions); risk of weights that smooth faint structure differently from the trained-on-real-pairs net. Confidence: low.
- **Not recommended (with reason):**
  - ZS-N2N / Neighbor2Neighbor / Noise2Void / Noise2Self as run-time denoisers: independent-noise assumption fails at lag-1 0.41 and stacks have long-range correlation from Lanczos warp; a per-image optimisation in numpy is also slow. ZS-N2N's own paper restricts its claim to pixel-wise independent noise.
  - AP-BSN style pixel-shuffle: needs a stride of at least the correlation length (about 3 px), costing a factor 9 in resolution; stars and nebula filaments are the content that matters.
  - Noise2Score: the Poisson/Gamma strength is irrelevant for sky-limited stacked Gaussian noise; needs a score network.
  - Own measurement, geometric self-ensemble around the N2N U-Net (8 dihedral transforms, synthetic scene with correlated noise): sky residual 1.58 -> 1.51 ADU (-4%), faint nebula 5.18 -> 5.12, star surroundings 23.5 -> 22.4 ADU, at 8x the compute (6.1 s for 8 passes of a 640x768 crop); a 4-shift grid ensemble gave 1.55. Not worth the cost; the network is already close to equivariant.

### Gaps
- Could not retrieve the StructN2V paper body (mask construction, reported gains) or the R2R inference protocol; ZS-N2N's parameter count and run time are not in the abstract. The AP-BSN abstract gives no PSNR figures.
- No paper was found applying any single-image self-supervised denoiser to stacked amateur Bayer data with debayer-correlated noise.
- Cross-colour/CFA-domain denoising of raw Bayer frames before debayering (e.g. BM3D-CFA) was not researched; stack averaging probably dominates, but this is unverified.

## Burst and multi-frame denoising that exploits per-frame structure (VBM4D-style, kernel prediction, per-frame variance maps)

### Takeaway
For static, registered sky frames the optimally weighted stack (proper coaddition) is already the maximum-likelihood combine, so burst networks and VBM4D gain nothing the stacker lacks; the unexploited per-frame information is the per-pixel noise (depth) map: the denoisers assume one global sigma although `--full-field`, `--merge`/hierarchical combine and mosaics produce stacks whose depth, hence noise, varies across the frame (I-3).

### Cited Findings
- Burst Denoising with Kernel Prediction Networks (Mildenhall et al., CVPR 2018) predicts spatially varying kernels that both align and denoise handheld bursts, trained with a synthetic realistic-noise pipeline and an annealed loss; it "matches or outperforms" prior work across noise levels — [ar5iv:1712.02327](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1712.02327). The gains come from jointly handling alignment error and motion.
- FFDNet (Zhang, Zuo, Zhang, IEEE TIP 2018) takes a tunable noise-level map as input to remove spatially variant noise with one network, works on down-sampled sub-images for speed, and is reported faster than BM3D even on CPU — [arXiv:1710.04026](https://arxiv.org/abs/1710.04026).
- Project facts: `--full-field` (on by default, FRAC 0.5) extends the stack beyond the common crop; "an edge pixel covered by half the frames is ~1.4x noisier" and `<output>_coverage.fits` records the frame count per pixel — [src/full_field.py](C:\source\originstack-research\src\full_field.py). The coverage array is not read by Phase 4: no reference to coverage/sigma maps exists in `postprocess.py`, `nn_denoise.py` or `denoising.py` (grep). `--merge` uses an inverse-noise-variance weighted mean per footprint, and Phase 3 can emit a per-pixel sigma sidecar (`ivw_combine(..., return_sigma=True)`) — [CLAUDE.md](C:\source\originstack-research\CLAUDE.md), [dev-notes/module-notes.md](C:\source\originstack-research\dev-notes\module-notes.md).
- `nn_denoise.normalise` returns one sigma per channel (lag-4 MAD of the whole image), `z = (src - bg) / sig`, so the network sees one noise level everywhere — [src/nn_denoise.py](C:\source\originstack-research\src\nn_denoise.py). `directional_wavelet_denoise` likewise takes one scalar sigma per plane — [src/denoising.py](C:\source\originstack-research\src\denoising.py).
- Proper coaddition already weights each frame per spatial frequency by its PSF, transparency and noise — [dev-notes/module-notes.md](C:\source\originstack-research\dev-notes\module-notes.md). Spatio-temporal self-supervised denoising across exposures (ASTERIS, Science 2026) was covered in the earlier note ([arXiv:2602.17205](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.17205)).

### Inferences
- **I-3. Per-pixel noise-map (coverage / inverse-variance) normalisation for the NN and the wavelet denoiser.**
  - What/why: make the denoisers see unit-variance noise everywhere. Today a frame whose outer strips are 1.41x noisier gets a global sigma that is too high for the core and too low for the strips, so the core is under-smoothed and the strips are left with relatively more noise (FFDNet's noise-level-map input is the published precedent for conditioning on a map).
  - Algorithm: rel(x,y) = sqrt(N_core / coverage(x,y)) clipped to [1, 2] for equal-weight frames (for weighted combines use sigma_map / median(sigma_map in core), or 1/sqrt(weight_sum) from `merge_previous_stacks`). In `nn_denoise`: compute `sig` on `src / rel` instead of `src`; `z = (src - bg) / (sig * rel[..., None])`; `den = denoise_z(z, w) * sig * rel[..., None] + bg`; leave the restore step as is. In `directional_wavelet_denoise`: resize rel to each band and use `T_local = T_band * rel_band`. Plumb `coverage` from `full_field` / `pipeline` into `args._noise_rel` and read `<stem>_coverage.fits` on the `--from-stack` path (uniform if absent).
  - Source: FFDNet (above) for the idea; the repo's own full_field notes for the size of the effect.
  - Expected gain (own measurement, synthetic scene with correlated noise, right half 1.41x noisier, two runs): residual error versus truth in empty sky fell from 2.22 to 1.49 ADU in the full-depth half (-33%; first run 1.99 -> 1.49, -25%) and from 2.43 to 2.08 ADU (-14%) in the noisy half; in faint nebula regions of the noisy half 4.16 -> 3.47 ADU (-17%); the uniform-noise control was 1.5 ADU. All residuals are small against the 23-32 ADU input noise (about 7-13%), so the visible gain is modest and concentrated at coverage edges.
  - Plug-in: Phase 4 (`nn_denoise`, `denoising`), `pipeline.py` / `full_field.py` / `merge.py` for the map; `--from-stack` early-cache key must include the map (`_early_args_read`).
  - Cost/risk: S-M. Risk: a wrong map is worse than none; normalisation must preserve the NN's `output_scale` Poisson term; star-removal and the deconvolver share `normalise` and must stay consistent. Confidence: medium (clear mechanism, synthetic evidence, not yet tried on a real full-field stack).
- Burst/video methods deliberately not recommended: VBM4D/KPN/deep burst super-resolution target motion and alignment error, need large training sets or GPU, and the project already tried and reverted a kernel-regression merge of Bayer samples (CLAUDE.md "Tried and reverted"). No published evidence was found that spatio-temporal non-local filtering of registered static astronomical frames beats stacking followed by a good denoiser.

- No source quantifying how much Phase-4 denoisers lose when noise varies spatially by 1.4x; the figures above are my synthetic test only.
- VBM4D itself (Maggioni et al.) and HDR+ merge were not fetched; the claim that their gains come from motion/alignment rests on the KPN abstract.
- The effect on a real `--full-field` stack has not been measured.

## Transform-domain and statistical methods (starlet/MS-VST, curvelet successors, low-rank/patch methods, Bayesian shrinkage, noise whitening of debayer/resampling-correlated noise)

### Takeaway
The most valuable finding is a defect, not a new transform: `directional_wavelet_denoise` takes one noise sigma from the finest diagonal band and applies it to every band, which is only valid for white noise. On the project's real correlated stacks the coarser bands carry 1.7-3.5x that sigma (own measurement), so the wavelet denoiser barely denoises; a per-band (level-dependent) sigma, a 1997 statistics result, fixes most of it in a few lines (I-1), and cycle spinning (I-2) and data-driven shrinkage (I-5) are cheap follow-ups.

### Cited Findings
- Johnstone and Silverman (J. R. Statist. Soc. B 59, 1997) handle stationary correlated noise with level-dependent soft thresholds: for stationary correlated noise the coefficient variance depends on the decomposition level but is constant within a level, so the threshold is estimated per level from the data; the estimator is near-minimax for short- and long-range dependence — [IDEAS/RePEc](https://ideas.repec.org/a/bla/jorssb/v59y1997i2p319-351.html). The level-dependence statement is from a search summary of a later review (possibly [Statistica Sinica](https://www3.stat.sinica.edu.tw/statistica/j9n1/j9n13/j9n13.htm); I did not open it), and a 2024 arXiv paper proposes a level-dependent Bayesian shrinkage rule for correlated noise as an alternative ([arXiv:2404.14623](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2404.14623), abstract not read).
- The Noise Clinic (Lebrun, Colom, Morel, IPOL 2015 / IEEE TIP 2015) first estimates a signal- and frequency-dependent noise model, then denoises with a multiscale Non-local Bayes — the patch-method route to correlated noise — [IPOL](https://www.ipol.im/pub/art/2015/125/), [TIP paper](https://mcolom.perso.math.cnrs.fr/download/articles/Multiscale_Image_Blind_Denoising_TIP2015.pdf).
- Starck and Murtagh (PASP 110, 193, 1998) estimate noise automatically from the multiresolution support of the a trous wavelet transform: build the support by marking significant coefficients per scale, then iteratively re-estimate the noise from the pixels in the noise set; they also treat mixed Poisson-Gaussian noise — [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1086/316124), [bibliography page](https://www.cita.utoronto.ca/~mamd/BIB/starck98.html).
- The starlet / undecimated wavelet transform (Starck, Fadili, Murtagh, IEEE TIP 16(2), 2007) relates the standard and isotropic undecimated transforms, introduces filter banks robust to the ringing common in wavelet denoising, and an iterative reconstruction better than direct synthesis — [ADS](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2007ITIP...16..297S/abstract).
- Translation-invariant denoising (Coifman and Donoho, 1995): "cycle spinning" shifts, denoises, unshifts and averages, giving reconstructions with much weaker Gibbs-type artifacts than orthogonal-wavelet thresholding, equivalent to denoising with the undecimated transform when all shifts are used — [Donoho TIDeNoise.pdf](https://web.stanford.edu/dept/statistics/cgi-bin/donoho/wp-content/uploads/2018/08/TIDeNoise.pdf).
- MS-VST (Zhang, Fadili, Starck, IEEE TIP 17(7), 2008) adds multiscale variance stabilisation for filtered Poisson data with significance tests on wavelet/ridgelet/curvelet coefficients; it targets very low-count images — [authors' draft](https://fadili.users.greyc.fr/Pub/bibtex/manuscript/manuscrit434.pdf).
- SURE-LET (Blu and Luisier, IEEE TIP 16(11), 2007): minimise Stein's unbiased risk estimate over a linear expansion of thresholds, which reduces to solving a small linear system; typically on undecimated wavelets; "very competitive"; per-subband minimisation does worse for tight frames; the stated noise model is additive white Gaussian — [abstract record](https://katalog.dhi-paris.fr/vufind/Record/NLM174902379/Description), [PDF](https://mil.ee.ntu.edu.tw/pdfs/blu0702.pdf), [BIG page](http://bigwww.epfl.ch/algorithms/denoise/). I could not read the paper's PSNR tables.
- BLS-GSM (Portilla et al., IEEE TIP 12(11), 2003) models oriented-pyramid neighbourhoods as Gaussian scale mixtures; the Bayes least squares estimate is a weighted average of local Wiener estimates over the hidden multiplier; claimed to "substantially surpass" earlier methods for Gaussian noise of known covariance — [NYU abstract](https://www.cns.nyu.edu/~eero/ABSTRACTS/portilla03-abstract.html).
- EbayesThresh (Johnstone and Silverman, J. Stat. Softw. 2005): prior = point mass at zero plus a heavy-tailed (Laplace or Cauchy-like) component, mixing weight by marginal maximum likelihood, posterior-median thresholding, applied level by level to wavelet transforms — [R docs](https://rdrr.io/cran/EbayesThresh/man/ebayesthresh.html), [JSS record](https://doaj.org/article/726e76a3cdb24bcab1006663e0a9a130).
- Generalised Anscombe: the exact unbiased inverse (Makitalo and Foi, IEEE TIP 22(1), 2013) is needed for accurate denoising at low counts; the algebraic inverse is biased there — [project page](https://webpages.tuni.fi/foi/invansc/). The repo uses the algebraic inverse and says so in `_inverse_generalized_anscombe` — [src/denoising.py](C:\source\originstack-research\src\denoising.py).
- Low-rank patch methods: WNNM (Gu et al., CVPR 2014) reports beating BM3D — [CVPR PDF](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content_cvpr_2014/papers/Gu_Weighted_Nuclear_Norm_2014_CVPR_paper.pdf). BM3D and NLM were removed from the project for speed, licence and star damage — [CLAUDE.md](C:\source\originstack-research\CLAUDE.md).
- SOS boosting (Romano and Elad, SIAM J. Imaging Sci. 2015): x_{k+1} = f(y + rho x_k) - rho x_k, reported +0.13 to +1.26 dB for K-SVD and +0.30 to +0.56 dB for NLM; the denoiser is told a noise level slightly above sigma. Siril ships it (default 3 iterations, rho 0.2) beside NL-Bayes and DA3D — [arXiv:1502.06220](https://arxiv.org/abs/1502.06220), [ar5iv](https://ar5iv.arxiv.org/html/1502.06220), [Siril docs](https://siril.readthedocs.io/en/latest/processing/denoising.html).
- Maximum Noise Fraction (MNF): a transform that orders multispectral components by image quality by solving a generalised eigenproblem between noise and signal covariances (eigenvalues are component SNRs; it reduces to PCA for equal noise variance); standard for hyperspectral denoising and combined with VBM3D — [MathWorks hypermnf](https://www.mathworks.com/help/images/ref/hypermnf.html), [paper listing](https://typeset.io/papers/a-transformation-for-ordering-multispectral-data-in-terms-of-wbuht4c3tr), [MNF + VBM3D](https://www.spiedigitallibrary.org/journals/journal-of-applied-remote-sensing/volume-15/issue-03/032208/Hyperspectral-imagery-denoising-using-minimum-noise-fraction-and-VBM3D/10.1117/1.JRS.15.032208.full).
- Project baseline: `directional_wavelet_denoise` uses a decimated bior1.3 `wavedec2`, `sigma_noise = median(|finest cD|)/0.6745` for all bands, BayesShrink soft thresholds reduced by a structure-tensor coherence map, YCbCr (Rec.601) luma/chroma split, chroma at 2x sigma; it has no cycle spinning, no per-band sigma, no SURE — [src/denoising.py](C:\source\originstack-research\src\denoising.py). `--variance-stabilize` (generalised Anscombe) already exists.

### Inferences
- **I-1. Per-band (level- and orientation-dependent) noise sigma in `directional_wavelet_denoise` [top idea].**
  - What/why: the code estimates sigma once (finest cD) and uses it for all bands; with correlated noise the finest band holds the lowest-power part of the noise spectrum, so every coarser band is under-thresholded and BayesShrink's "signal variance" (mean(d^2) - sigma^2) is inflated by leftover noise.
  - Algorithm: for each level j and orientation o, `s_jo = median(|d - median(d)|) / 0.6745` (optionally over sky-only coefficients: the existing star/object mask resized to the band, or the Starck-Murtagh iteration: `s <- std(d[|d| < 3 s]) / 0.9866` until stable), then `T_jo = s_jo^2 / sqrt(max(mean(d^2) - s_jo^2, 0))` (inf if no signal variance), keep the coherence modulation `T * (1 - protect * coh)`. Chroma planes: multiply by `chroma_factor` as now. Guard: clamp s_jo to at least the white-noise expectation of the finest band so a signal-free band cannot lower it.
  - Source: Johnstone and Silverman 1997 (level-dependent thresholds for correlated noise); Starck and Murtagh 1998 (noise from the multiresolution support); Fruchter and Hook for why resampled noise is correlated; Siril docs for the same limitation in NL-Bayes.
  - Expected gain, own measurements. (a) Real held-out Origin stacks (`C:\nn_work\heldout_stacks\sunflower|omega|orion.fits`, 1024x1024 luma crops, centre and corner, 4-level bior1.3 via the repo's `wavelet.wavedec2`): band MAD-sigma relative to the sigma the denoiser uses is 1.66-1.98 in the finest H/V bands and 2.2-3.5 at the third level (the two coarsest levels read 2.6-21 but include gradients and nebulosity, since these stacks are not background-flattened). Using the NN output as a near-noise-free reference on sky pixels, the share of pixel noise left after the stock denoiser vs the per-band version was: Sunflower centre 90.7% -> 78.0%, corner 90.5% -> 77.4%; Omega centre 93.5% -> 83.1%, corner 92.0% -> 80.8%; Orion centre 97.9% -> 94.5%, corner 95.3% -> 89.0%. So the stock denoiser removes only 2-10% of the noise on these stacks and the per-band fix removes about 2.3-2.6x as much (6-22%), still far below the NN. (b) Synthetic faint-nebula scene with lag-1 0.32 noise (weaker than real): 3-channel-mean sky rms 0.578 input -> 0.574 stock (-0.7%) -> 0.502 per-band (-13%), identical to an oracle that uses the true noise per band (0.502); faint-structure flux slope 1.02-1.03 (no extra shrinkage). With white noise the stock and per-band results are the same within 1% (0.444 vs 0.439), so there is no regression where the old assumption held.
  - Plug-in: `src/denoising.py::directional_wavelet_denoise._denoise_plane` (about 10 lines); add a test that fails on the stock code with correlated noise (synthetic, as above). Also relevant to `reduce_chroma_noise` / `_estimate_sky_sigma`, which the project already noticed "reads far too low after the wavelet denoiser has correlated neighbours" (phase4-quality.md).
  - Cost/risk: S. Risk: on a band dominated by real structure the MAD overestimates noise and over-smooths; mitigated by the sky mask, by Phase 4's DBE having removed gradients before the denoiser, and by the support iteration. Confidence: high that the defect exists in the function; medium on the size of the benefit in the full pipeline (stock function tested in isolation; the NN is the default on Origin data, so the benefit is chiefly the non-NN fallbacks and other cameras).
- **I-2. Translation-invariant (cycle-spun) wavelet denoising.**
  - What/why: the decimated DWT thresholds differently depending on where features sit relative to the 2^L grid; averaging over shifts removes grid-phase artifacts and lowers error.
  - Algorithm: `out = mean_k roll(denoise(roll(img, s_k)), -s_k)` over 4-8 shifts such as (0,0),(2,1),(1,3),(3,2) (for L=4 the grid period is 16); reflect-pad by 2^L instead of wrapping; shift the star mask with the image. A full starlet (a trous B3-spline, w_j = c_{j-1} - c_j, thresholds k_j * sigma_j with sigma_j from the measured noise as in I-1) is the alternative, at about 5x the cost of one decimated pass.
  - Source: Coifman and Donoho 1995; Starck, Fadili, Murtagh 2007.
  - Expected gain (own measurement, synthetic): sky rms 0.444 -> 0.413 (-7%) for white noise; 0.502 -> 0.483 (-4%) for correlated noise with I-1 applied; on the stock sigma it adds little (0.574 -> 0.556). Roughly 0.3-0.6 dB plus fewer pseudo-Gibbs ripples near stars (not quantified). Cost is K times the wavelet step (2.2 s on Sunflower per dev-notes, so about 9 s at K=4).
  - Plug-in: wrapper around `directional_wavelet_denoise` in `postprocess.py` (flag or default); a native batch kernel only if the 4x matters. Cost/risk: S, low risk. Confidence: medium-high (classical result, small own confirmation).
- **I-5. SURE-LET or EbayesThresh shrinkage in place of BayesShrink soft thresholding (second step after I-1).**
  - What/why: soft thresholding subtracts T from every surviving coefficient, biasing strong coefficients (faint filaments) low; a data-fitted expansion or a heavy-tailed prior shrinks small coefficients hard and large ones little.
  - Algorithm (SURE-LET, pointwise form): per band with noise std s, theta(w) = a1 * w + a2 * w * exp(-w^2 / (12 s^2)); SURE(a) = sum[(theta(w) - w)^2 + 2 s^2 theta'(w) - s^2]; with theta = sum_k a_k phi_k this is quadratic in a, minimised by M a = c where M_kl = sum phi_k phi_l and c_k = sum (w phi_k - s^2 phi_k'). Stein's lemma for a pointwise theta only needs each coefficient's own variance, so correlated noise is fine provided s is per band (I-1). The paper warns that for redundant transforms the minimisation must be done in the image domain, not per subband. EbayesThresh alternative: per band estimate the mixing weight w by maximising the marginal likelihood of a (point mass + Laplace) prior with the band's s, then use the posterior median.
  - Source: Blu and Luisier 2007; Johnstone and Silverman 2005. Gain claimed only as "very competitive"; I could not verify numbers.
  - Plug-in: `denoising.py` (replace `wavelet.soft_threshold` call; the native `soft_threshold` already takes a per-pixel array). Cost/risk: M; bior1.3 is biorthogonal so coefficient noise differs from image-domain noise (SURE is then approximate). Confidence: low-medium.
- **I-6. Noise-adjusted (MNF) colour transform instead of fixed YCbCr for the luma/chroma split.**
  - What/why: Rec.601 luma weights are presumably not SNR-optimal for stacks whose R, G, B noise differ (G has twice the Bayer samples; the green-guided debayer interpolation probably correlates channel noise; neither was measured here) and whose nebulae are strongly coloured (Ha in R). MNF finds the colour direction with the highest signal-to-noise and leaves two low-SNR directions to be smoothed hard.
  - Algorithm: noise covariance Sigma_n (3x3) from sky-masked high-pass residuals or y - nn_denoise(y); total covariance Sigma_y over the frame; solve Sigma_s v = lambda Sigma_n v with Sigma_s = Sigma_y - Sigma_n; p = V^T c; denoise p1 with the structure-protecting wavelet, p2 and p3 with stronger shrinkage (as chroma_factor does now); invert. Fix the sign/orientation of V so p1 stays near the all-positive direction.
  - Source: MNF references above. Gain: unknown for 3 bands (the hyperspectral literature uses many bands); no measurement made. Plug-in: `directional_wavelet_denoise` and `reduce_chroma_noise`. Cost/risk: S-M; the wavelet code assumes Y/Cb/Cr scalings. Confidence: low.
- **I-8 (minor). Exact unbiased inverse of the generalised Anscombe transform** in `_inverse_generalized_anscombe` (closed-form approximation from Makitalo and Foi); relevant only at low counts, which sky-limited stacks rarely reach. Cost: S. Confidence: low value.
- Not recommended: MS-VST (Poisson low-count regime; stacks are Gaussian-dominated and the project already has the GAT option); BLS-GSM (strong but needs a steerable pyramid and a 10-dimensional neighbourhood solve per pixel — a native-kernel project with no verified advantage over I-1 plus I-2 plus I-5); WNNM/NLM/BM3D-family (same family the project already removed for speed and star damage; Siril itself says NL-Bayes does not handle correlated OSC noise).
- Own measurement, SOS boosting around the wavelet denoiser (rho 0.2-1.0, 3 iterations, per-band sigma): no gain at the true noise level (sky rms 0.486 -> 0.487-0.490); the improvement at a 10% higher noise argument (0.449-0.465) equals simply raising the threshold. Not recommended for shrinkage denoisers.

### Gaps
- SURE-LET's and BLS-GSM's PSNR figures and the starlet paper's denoising numbers could not be read (PDFs not parsed); the benefit of I-5 over a correct I-1 is therefore unquantified.
- I-1 was measured on the denoiser in isolation on raw linear stacks; the full Phase 4 chain (DBE first, then sky pedestal) was not run end to end, and the real stack noise is not Gaussian-filtered white noise.
- No evidence found for low-rank tensor or dictionary-learning methods on astronomical stacks specifically.

## Methods that explicitly protect or restore low-surface-brightness signal (multiscale significance testing, detect-then-denoise, structure-aware priors)

### Takeaway
The astronomy-native answer is significance-driven adaptive smoothing: give each pixel the smallest smoothing scale at which the local signal-to-noise clears a threshold (ASMOOTH) or use a multiresolution support to decide what is structure; on a synthetic faint-nebula scene this cut RMS error about 3-3.8x versus the stock wavelet denoiser (2.6-3.6x versus a per-band-sigma wavelet), but the scene flatters smooth truth, so it must be tested on real data before adoption (I-4). The NN path already restores low frequencies, so this is mostly for the wavelet fallback and diffuse targets.

### Cited Findings
- ASMOOTH (Ebeling, White, Rangarajan, MNRAS 368, 65, 2006): user sets a minimum S/N tau_min (tau_max defaults to tau_min + 1); kernel is Gaussian K = exp(-r^2 / 2 sigma^2) / (2 pi sigma^2); each pixel is smoothed at the smallest sigma whose counts satisfy N_min <= I'/K(0, sigma) <= N_max, with N = N_bkg + tau^2/2 + tau sqrt(N_bkg + dN_bkg^2 + tau^2/4); scales start at about one pixel and grow nonlinearly in layers (a pixel enters a layer only if not already assigned), top-down, so low-S/N regions get large kernels; maximum scale 1/8 of the image for a Gaussian; total counts conserved. Caveats stated by the authors: noise "not removed efficiently on all scales" for small tau_min, smoothed peaks can exceed raw values slightly, significant negative features are not treated, residual autocorrelation weakly positive (up to 2%) — [arXiv:astro-ph/0601306](https://arxiv.org/abs/astro-ph/0601306), [ar5iv](https://ar5iv.arxiv.org/html/astro-ph/0601306).
- NoiseChisel (Akhlaghi and Ichikawa, ApJS 220, 1, 2015): non-parametric, noise-based detection of faint nebulous objects with no assumed shape or PSF; sub-sky threshold detection, false-detection removal using the ambient noise, then non-parametric substructure; on extremely faint diffuse mock galaxies purity 0.89 vs 0.29 for SExtractor — [arXiv:1505.01664](https://arxiv.org/abs/1505.01664).
- The multiresolution support (Starck and Murtagh 1998, above) is the wavelet analogue: significant coefficients per scale define what is structure; the rest is noise.
- Project baseline: the NN denoiser returns faint extended light with a fixed 4 px Gaussian "restore" of the removed residual (86-97% of injected 0.5-3 sigma discs kept, versus 48-79% without), and the project injects extended discs when scoring (`tools/eval_nn_faint.py`) — [src/nn_denoise.py](C:\source\originstack-research\src\nn_denoise.py). The wavelet path protects structure with a structure-tensor coherence map (`--wavelet-protect 0.6`) — [src/denoising.py](C:\source\originstack-research\src\denoising.py). `background._build_emission_mask` already detects diffuse emission for DBE — [dev-notes/module-notes.md](C:\source\originstack-research\dev-notes\module-notes.md).

### Inferences
- **I-4. Significance-adaptive (ASMOOTH-style) smoothing as a faint-extended-structure layer.**
  - What/why: a per-pixel scale choice by a statistical test is a principled replacement for the heuristic coherence protection; compact bright structure stays at full resolution, faint extended structure accumulates signal over the smallest sufficient area, pure-noise sky goes to the largest scale.
  - Algorithm: scales `s_k` = 0, 0.7, 1, 1.5, 2.2, 3.3, 5, 7.5, 11, 16, 24 px; `S_k = gaussian_filter_ds(L, s_k)` on the sky-subtracted luma; noise per scale `sig_k` = std of the same smoothing of a noise sample (the project already forms one as `y - nn_denoise(y)` on empty sky) or analytically sig_k^2 = sum_f P_n(f) |G_k(f)|^2 with P_n measured from sky; `out = S_k*` where `k*` is the smallest k with `S_k > tau * sig_k` (tau about 4-5), else the largest scale; stars (existing star mask) keep their pixels; apply to luma and add back the chroma from the existing chroma path. Gate on positive S_k since faint emission is positive; negative excursions fall to the large scale.
  - Source: ASMOOTH; NoiseChisel for the noise-referenced detection philosophy.
  - Expected gain (own measurement, synthetic scene of six Gaussian blobs 18-50 px wide at 0.3-1.0 pixel-sigma, one 4 px-wide ridge at 0.6, 25 stars, lag-1 0.32 noise): sky rms 0.567 (stock wavelet) / 0.486 (per-band wavelet) / 0.187 (tau 4) / 0.206 (tau 5); nebula rms 0.569 / 0.487 / 0.150 / 0.135; faint-structure flux slope 0.99 / 0.98 / 1.02 / 0.97; star core ratio 1.00 to 1.01. For comparison the NN on a similar synthetic scene left 1.5 ADU of 23 ADU input noise in sky (about 7%), i.e. the NN is still better; this idea is not a competitor to it. Caveat: the truth is smooth at 18+ px, which maximally rewards smoothing, and the ridge lost some amplitude at tau 5 (0.97); real filaments, dust lanes and galaxy arms will be harder.
  - Plug-in: new optional step after the denoiser (`postprocess.py`, with `PHASE4_TOGGLES` / `SNAP_STEPS` / `_PP_STEP_LABELS` entries per the CLAUDE.md rules) or a replacement for the coarse approximation band treatment; uses `gaussian_filter_ds`. Could also gate `multiscale_local_contrast` boosts by the same significance map.
  - Cost/risk: M (about 12 Gaussian blurs, roughly 1-2 s at 24 MP with downsampled large scales). Risk: blotchy texture where scale changes abruptly (feather between layers), false significant blobs from residual gradients (run after DBE), and the correlated-noise calibration of sig_k. Confidence: medium that it helps diffuse targets (IFN, faint nebulae); low on real-data size. Validate with the split-half and injected-disc protocols in the evaluation section.
- Detect-then-denoise with a NoiseChisel-style mask (kernel-convolve, quantile threshold on the noise distribution, erosion/dilation) could replace the luminance-threshold masks in `reduce_chroma_noise` and the emission mask; no measurement made, and `_build_emission_mask` already covers part of this, so it is listed as a possible refinement rather than a new feature.
- The NN's fixed 4 px restore could be replaced by a significance-weighted restore, but the expected gain is tiny: residual low-frequency noise after a 4 px restore is about sigma/14 for white noise (about sigma/8 with the real correlation), roughly 1-2% of noise power.

### Gaps
- NoiseChisel's convolution, quantile-threshold and erosion steps were not confirmed from the abstract page; only the noise-based, non-parametric principle and the purity figure were.
- No source was found comparing significance-adaptive smoothing to learned denoisers on optical amateur data.
- Low-surface-brightness literature on photometric injection tests (Trujillo and Fliri-type) and JWST/Euclid LSB pipelines were not researched.

## Honest evaluation without ground truth, and failure modes (hallucination, star texture, colour noise)

### Takeaway
The project's split-half error metric and invented-source tests are the right foundation; what is missing is a scale-resolved view of where a denoiser helps or hurts, and a whiteness check of the removed component ("method noise"), both cheap to add (I-9). Noise2Self-style self-supervised scoring should not be re-introduced for these stacks because its independence assumption fails at lag-1 0.41.

### Cited Findings
- Noise2Self shows a J-invariant loss can score and calibrate denoisers from noisy data alone, assuming noise independent across dimensions — [arXiv:1901.11365](https://arxiv.org/abs/1901.11365).
- "Method noise" (Buades, Coll, Morel, Multiscale Model. Simul. 4(2), 2005): the difference between an image and its denoised version; a good denoiser should leave a residual that looks like white noise, and the authors treat that visual test as the most telling — [HAL record](https://hal.archives-ouvertes.fr/hal-00271141), [catalogue entry](https://scholars.ln.edu.hk/en/publications/a-review-of-image-denoising-algorithms-with-a-new-one/).
- Chatterjee and Milanfar, "Is Denoising Dead?" (IEEE TIP 19(4), 2010): a Bayesian Cramer-Rao-style bound on patch-based denoising shows room for improvement remains for a wide class of images at certain SNR levels — [PDF](https://users.soe.ucsc.edu/%7emilanfar/publications/journal/DenoisingBoundsFinal.pdf).
- Siril's practitioner documentation lists failure modes: impulse noise skews the denoiser (run cosmetic correction first), blocky coloured artefacts in colour images (`-indep` per-channel denoise), deconvolution/wavelet sharpening makes noise less Gaussian so denoise first, denoising works better on unstretched data; modulation below 1 blends back micro-texture — [Siril docs](https://siril.readthedocs.io/en/latest/processing/denoising.html).
- NN failure modes known to the project: faint isolated sources are shrunk toward the sky by a minimum-MSE denoiser (48-79% flux kept without the restore), the per-channel stretch whitens sharper star cores so colour must be scored on linear output (`*_lin`), and invented-source checks exist — [src/nn_denoise.py](C:\source\originstack-research\src\nn_denoise.py), [dev-notes/nn-deconv-runbook.md](C:\source\originstack-research\dev-notes\nn-deconv-runbook.md), `tools/eval_nn_denoise.py` (error versus an independent half-stack), `tools/eval_nn_faint.py`, `tools/eval_ps1_truth.py` (Pan-STARRS truth).
- Earlier note: NoiseXTerminator defaults can create "heterogeneous blobs during stretching" (Cloudy Nights review) — [denoising_psf_deconvolution.md](C:\source\originstack-research\research_notes\Astrophotography stacking techniques since 2022\denoising_psf_deconvolution.md).

### Inferences
- **I-9. Scale-resolved split-half risk curve and method-noise diagnostics.**
  - What/why: a single error number hides which spatial scale a denoiser damages. With two independent half-stacks A and B (the odd/even or quarter-stacks the tools already build) the cross-fit error of a denoiser D at band k is E_k = mean[(band_k(D(A)) - band_k(B))^2] - sigma_B,k^2, where sigma_B,k^2 = mean[band_k(A - B)^2] / 2 is B's own noise power in that band (B's noise is independent of D(A)). Doing nothing has risk sigma_A,k^2; plot E_k / sigma_A,k^2 against band scale (Gaussian differences or the dyadic wavelet bands, 1 px to 64 px). Values below 1 mean the denoiser helps at that scale; above 1 it is removing signal there; a bump at 16-64 px is the over-smoothing of faint extended light that the project cares about, and the same curve evaluated on injected extended discs gives its recovery.
  - Method-noise checks (Buades et al.): M = A - D(A) on sky; (i) PSD of M divided by the measured noise PSD per frequency band — it should be about 1 up to the denoiser's bandwidth and 0 beyond, anything else is leakage or colouring; (ii) normalised cross-correlation of M with D(A) and with the local gradient magnitude — structure in M means signal removed; (iii) per-channel chroma-blotch statistics of D(A) in sky for colour noise.
  - Source: Noise2Self (cross-fit logic), Buades et al. 2005; the formula is standard N2N bookkeeping, not a new published method.
  - Plug-in: `tools/eval_nn_denoise.py` / `tools/bench_denoise_quality.py` (offline only). Cost/risk: S, no run-time impact. Confidence: high that it is informative.
- Guard against reusing Noise2Self-type scores on correlated stacks: the J-invariant mask must cover the noise correlation length (the StructN2V idea), otherwise the self-supervised loss rewards denoisers that reproduce correlated noise.
- A deviation bound as a safety clamp (TDR-style restore, already in the earlier note) is largely redundant for the NN path because the low-frequency restore makes the output's content above 4 px agree with the input by construction; it would only matter in sky/faint regions if the network is ever run without the restore.
- Own measurement worth repeating in the project's own harness: "noise remaining versus the NN output" on sky pixels, used above to compare wavelet variants on real stacks, is a cheap proxy when no half-stack exists, but it ranks wavelet variants only (the reference is itself a denoiser).

### Gaps
- No peer-reviewed source on scale-resolved N2N-style risk curves for astronomical images; I-9 is my construction from standard cross-validation arithmetic.
- The project's existing tools (`bench_denoise_quality.py`, `eval_nn_faint.py`) were not run; their exact metrics were taken from docstrings, so overlap with I-9 should be checked.
- Hallucination tests for the NN beyond injected sources (for example Gaia/PS1-based truth checks exist for the deconvolver) were not re-assessed.
