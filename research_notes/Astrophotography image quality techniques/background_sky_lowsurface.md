# Background / Gradient Removal, Sky Modelling, Scattered Light and Low-Surface-Brightness (LSB) Techniques for an OSC Smart-Telescope Stacker

Scope: ideas NOT already in OriginStack (checked against CLAUDE.md, `dev-notes/phase4-quality.md`, `dev-notes/ROADMAP.md`, the earlier notes in `research_notes/Astrophotography stacking techniques since 2022/` and the code of `src/background.py`, `src/local_normalize.py`, `src/quality.py`, `src/frame_processor.py`). Primary sources were read as full text (PDFs converted with pdftotext) unless a bullet says "search-result summary". Sources marked "(secondary)" were not read in full text.

Project baseline used for the comparisons below (local files, read in this session):
- DBE/mesh is per-channel, patch-median sampled, locally-linear Gaussian-weighted regression with Tukey IRLS; patch rejection by an "emission mask" ([src/background.py](C:/source/originstack-research/src/background.py) `_build_emission_mask`, `_sample_background_patches`, `_admit_gradient_patches`).
- The star mask fed to DBE is a Gaussian blob of fixed FWHM 4 px at every detected centroid, independent of brightness ([src/quality.py](C:/source/originstack-research/src/quality.py) `generate_star_mask`, called with `fwhm=4.0` at [src/postprocess.py](C:/source/originstack-research/src/postprocess.py) line 331). The only brightness-aware masking is `_build_emission_mask`, which dilates at most the 3 brightest smoothed peaks by a fixed disk of radius max(15 px, 2% of the short side), plus a "diffuse" threshold measured against the frame border.
- `local_normalize.py` is additive only: 24x24 grid of 30th-percentile cell values per frame, per-cell median over frames as reference, smooth bilinear upsample ([src/local_normalize.py](C:/source/originstack-research/src/local_normalize.py)).
- `_pre_gradient_removal` (Phase 1, off by default) fits a degree-2 polynomial to single luminance pixels sampled every 32 px with plain least squares (no clipping, no star mask) and subtracts the same surface from all channels ([src/frame_processor.py](C:/source/originstack-research/src/frame_processor.py) line 97).
- Project's own measurements: DBE removes ~70% of cross-night gradient mismatch (median 1.44 -> 0.34 sky-sigma) but stays at 0.6-2.3 on frame-filling/reflection nebulae (Horsehead, Lagoon, Pleiades, Flaming Star); DBE settings sweeps move results by under ~5%; a learned U-Net background removed faint nebula because its "clean" training images were DBE outputs ([dev-notes/phase4-quality.md](C:/source/originstack-research/dev-notes/phase4-quality.md)). Saturated-star spikes/reflection rings are an open roadmap item ([dev-notes/ROADMAP.md](C:/source/originstack-research/dev-notes/ROADMAP.md), [dev-notes/star-n2n-runbook.md](C:/source/originstack-research/dev-notes/star-n2n-runbook.md)).

## Q1. Background modelling methods (GP/kriging, RBF/splines, robust fits with object masking, learned models, survey practice, PCA, avoiding subtraction of real extended signal) versus what DBE/mesh does, and where it fails

### Takeaway
The survey literature converges on one diagnosis: sky models fail by absorbing extended real light (halos, outskirts, cirrus) into the "sky", and the fixes are (a) masks that follow the real extent of bright sources, including their PSF wings, (b) sky-model scales larger than the biggest extended object plus a separately fitted static pattern, and (c) external templates (far-IR dust maps, survey references) for structure that no within-image statistic can tell from a gradient. OriginStack's interpolator is already strong (its own sweeps show it is at the method's limit), so the actionable gaps are the fixed-size star mask and the lack of any external prior for frame-filling cirrus/nebulosity, not the choice of RBF vs spline vs GP.

### Cited Findings
- HSC-SSP PDR1 fitted a 6th-order 2-D Chebyshev polynomial to 256x256-pixel "superpixels" per CCD; this "tends to subtract extended halos of bright objects" (dark halo around a nearby galaxy) and had discontinuities between CCDs. PDR2 replaced it by (1) an empirical background over the whole focal plane with 1024x1024-pixel (~2.8') superpixels ("scales significantly larger leave sky subtraction residuals that vary from exposure to exposure") and (2) a static "sky frame" (clipped mean of masked superpixels over several tens of widely dithered exposures) of which a single scale factor is fitted per exposure; a residual of ~29 mag/arcsec^2 is documented for large-scale subtraction — [Aihara et al. 2019, HSC-SSP DR2, Sect. 4.1 and 6.6.8](https://arxiv.org/abs/1905.12221)
- The HSC pipeline's own algorithm: 128x128 bins, iteratively 3-sigma-clipped mean ignoring detected pixels, weighted by inverse variance, then a 6th-order Chebyshev fit; "Chebyshev fitting is better at avoiding over-subtraction than large-scale bins with spline interpolation, largely because it is less sensitive to the placement of bin boundaries relative to the positions of bright objects" (512x512 bins with interpolation behave like the 6th-order fit on 4k x 2k CCDs) — [Bosch et al. 2018, HSC software pipeline](https://arxiv.org/abs/1705.06766)
- SDSS fits a polynomial of "several arcmin" length to the sky; under diffuse emission of similar or larger scale it "interprets the diffuse emission as the sky emission itself ... a systematic over-subtraction" — [Roman, Trujillo & Montes 2020, Galactic cirri in deep optical imaging](https://arxiv.org/abs/1907.00978)
- Kelvin, Hasan & Tyson 2023 (HSC-like simulations; SExtractor, Gnuastro, LSST pipelines): detection+sky estimation "systematically over-subtract the sky"; "modelled masking" (fit parametric models to bright sources, subtract them, estimate the sky on the residual with the original segmentation mask applied, masked pixels = NaN) and "magnitude-dependent dilated masking" (mask grows with source brightness so wing flux is brought under the mask) give sky estimates fainter by up to an order of magnitude than conventional masking; the model must match the wings or it degrades the estimate — [Kelvin et al. 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2301.05793). A follow-up (Watkins et al. 2024, MNRAS 528, 4289) reports undetected faint flux imposes a pedestal offset on every derived sky model — (secondary, search-result summary of the Kelvin work) [search summary](https://arxiv.org/abs/2301.05793)
- Liu et al. 2023 (Dragonfly): box-estimator + low-order-polynomial sky models are biased by cirrus because "some sources (such as Galactic cirrus) may be so widely distributed that they are effectively blended into the sky over most of the image, and there may not be enough uncontaminated pixels"; no choice of box size (128 or 256 px) yielded an appropriate model; their fix fits patch-level background as `I_hat(x,y) = B_sky,n=2(x,y) + alpha * B_dust,n=3(x,y) + eps`, with the dust template from Planck dust radiance/optical depth resampled to the image, a dust polynomial of higher order (3) than the sky polynomial (2), and `alpha` free — [Liu et al. 2023, A Recipe for Unbiased Background Modeling](https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.07922)
- The physical premise: the optical cirrus is starlight scattered by the same dust that radiates in the far-IR; Roman et al. found "an equivalent counterpart" in IRAS 100 um for every optical diffuse feature they examined and "no obvious optical diffuse emission ... without a far IR counterpart" (Stripe 82); Mihos et al. subtracted a scaled Herschel 250 um map from a V-band mosaic with `I(V)/I(250um) = 3.5 (+-0.5) x 10^-3` and it "does a reasonable job of removing the cirrus without significantly oversubtracting" — [Roman et al. 2020](https://arxiv.org/abs/1907.00978); [Mihos et al. 2017, Burrell Schmidt Deep Virgo Survey](https://arxiv.org/abs/1611.04435)
- Caveats of the dust prior stated by the authors: the Planck beam (~5') is ~50x coarser than Dragonfly so only large angular scales can be corrected; optical/IR correlation is "likely to be poorer on small scales"; validation in Liu et al. is visual comparison with Herschel/IRAS/Planck maps, not a numeric error metric — [Liu et al. 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.07922)
- NoiseChisel / Gnuastro sky: sky = mean of *undetected* pixels per mesh; a mesh is used only if the pixel distribution shows no significant signal, judged by the mode-median (2015: minimum mode quantile 0.49) or, in later versions, mean-median quantile difference (more sensitive to skewness than the mode); outlier meshes are rejected because "large galaxies or bright stars ... gradient may be on a smaller scale than the selected tile size ... over subtracting the Sky on the wings of bright sources, and creating artificial dark halos"; `--skyfracnoblank` requires a minimum undetected fraction per tile — [Akhlaghi & Ichikawa 2015](https://arxiv.org/abs/1505.01664); [Akhlaghi 2019, NoiseChisel IAU proceedings](https://arxiv.org/abs/1909.11230)
- Euclid VIS preparation paper: "Such artifacts result from the sky model inclusion of emission of extended sources like galaxies or cirrus deeply buried in the background noise ... at intensity levels significantly below the 1-sigma limit"; proposes sky flat-fields from hundreds of science images plus lamp/self-calibration flats for large-scale gradients, with self-calibration constraining large-scale flat errors to within 0.6% — [Borlaff et al. 2022, Euclid preparation XVI](https://arxiv.org/abs/2108.10321)
- Euclid ERO Dorado: galaxies and faint features masked with MTObjects (max-tree) on the image re-binned 6x for S/N, then a `sep` background with 1000x1000 px cells; the authors warn the flattened image "might have removed real, very extended LSB stellar structures" and keep it only for small-scale photometry — [Urbano et al., Euclid ERO Dorado](https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.17672)
- Gaussian-process / kriging in practice: GraXpert offers RBF, Splines and Kriging (all needing user sample points) plus an AI method with no user input and a "smoothing" slider; its README does not document the AI model's training data or architecture — [GraXpert README](https://github.com/Steffenhir/GraXpert). A GP with Matern kernel returning an uncertainty map exists in the `stdpipe` toolkit — (secondary, search snippet) [stdpipe docs](https://stdpipe.readthedocs.io/en/latest/api/stdpipe.photometry_background.html). A GP-like method, "local pixel-wise infilling", estimates a local covariance matrix from the data and predicts the background behind each masked star with an uncertainty, improving flux uncertainties 2-3x on nebulous fields — [Saydjari & Finkbeiner 2022](https://par.nsf.gov/servlets/purl/10376786)
- PixInsight MGC/MARS (the "learned/reference" route in the amateur world): a gradient-free wide-field reference built from user-contributed mono/RGB masters (Bortle 1-4 skies, FOV 3-50 deg) and a team station; the method compares large-scale structure of the target with the reference and relies on the premise that large-scale gradients "become insignificant at a local level"; no quantitative accuracy is published and the claims come from the vendor — [PixInsight MARS document](https://pixinsight.com/doc/docs/MARS/MARS.html)
- A geometric (steepest-descent minima) background estimator exists but targets low-count UV images where 3-sigma-clipped median/mode fail — [Pandey & Saha 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.17566)

### Inferences
Items marked NEW are not in OriginStack. "Confidence" refers to confidence that the idea yields a measurable improvement on Origin data, not to confidence in the cited facts.

**Idea 1 (NEW): Brightness-scaled, wing-aware star masking ("modelled / magnitude-dependent masking") for DBE and the other background steps**
- What/why: DBE's patch sampler and the 3-peak emission mask never mask a bright star's wings (the star mask is a 4 px blob). The surveys' dark-halo failure (HSC PDR1, SDSS, Kelvin's simulations) is exactly sky estimated on pixels containing unmasked wing light. In OriginStack this should show as a faint positive "veil" sampled as background near bright stars (then subtracted, giving a dark moat) or as patch-level bias inside the surface fit. (Inference: not yet measured on Origin data; the project's `bench_phase4` "ring" metric is the right detector.)
- Algorithm (step 1, cheap): for each detected source with flux F (already in the source table used by `measure_fwhm`), mask radius `r_m = (A*F / (k*sigma_sky))^(1/alpha)` with `alpha ~ 2.5` (SDSS wings decline as r^-2.49 over 20 mag) and `k ~ 0.25-0.5`, capped (e.g. 150-300 px) and floored at the current 4 px blob; `A` and `alpha` are fitted per stack from 10-30 isolated, unsaturated stars (radial profiles of the stack in flux bins). Union with the existing emission mask before `_sample_background_patches`. Step 2 (modelled masking proper): subtract the summed wing model `sum_i F_i * A * r_i^-alpha` (all detected stars, plus Gaia/Tycho stars outside the frame up to ~1-2 deg) from the luminance used to *sample* patches only; apply the fitted background to the original image, so star appearance is unchanged (safer than removing the veil). Step 3 (optional veil reduction): also subtract the model from the output; this is the Roman/Infante-Sainz use.
- Source: [Kelvin et al. 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2301.05793); [Infante-Sainz et al. 2020](https://arxiv.org/abs/1911.01430) (hierarchical brightest-first fitting; scale F = 3-sigma-clipped median of star/PSF profile ratio over radii between 0.1x saturation level and 3x background; Gaia incomplete for G<7 so use Tycho/Hipparcos for the brightest); [Aihara et al. 2019](https://arxiv.org/abs/1905.12221); [Akhlaghi & Ichikawa 2015](https://arxiv.org/abs/1505.01664).
- Expected gain: reduction of dark halos/moats and of bright-star-induced low-order bias on star-rich frames (Orion, Pleiades, Horsehead next to Alnitak). Evidence is from survey data and simulations, not Origin; magnitude unknown. Kelvin's "up to an order of magnitude fainter sky" is for crowded HSC-like simulations and should not be transplanted.
- Plug-in: `quality.generate_star_mask` (add per-star radius) and `postprocess.py` pp_star_mask construction; `background._build_emission_mask`; wing-model fit in a new small module next to `star_detect.py`/`quality.py`; Gaia/Tycho fetch via `gaia_cache.py`/`net_query.py` (off-frame stars; `--offline` must fall back to in-frame stars). Must join `_early_args_read`/early-cache key logic if it reads new args.
- Cost/risk: step 1 small (days); step 2 medium. Risk: wrong wing slope over-masks (fewer patches; dense-field fallback `DBE_DENSE_FIELD_THRESH` already exists) — cap the masked fraction. Saturated-core stars: use flux from the profile outside the clipped core as Infante-Sainz does.
- Confidence: medium (strong survey evidence, no Origin measurement).

**Idea 2 (NEW): Dust-template-regularised sky model for frames with cirrus/IFN/reflection nebulosity (Liu et al. 2023 adapted to DBE)**
- What/why: the project's own sweeps show DBE is at its limit on ~30% of targets (frame-filling nebulosity) and the learned model failed for lack of a "known-real faint nebula" ground truth. A far-IR/sub-mm dust map supplies exactly that independent information: a smooth but non-planar template of where scattered dust light lives.
- Algorithm: (1) fetch a dust map cut-out in the stack WCS (the project already has `args._stack_wcs`, plate solving and `net_query`); CDS `hips2fits` returns FITS cut-outs for any HiPS given a WCS dictionary, width/height, and a HiPS id [hips2fits](https://alasky.cds.unistra.fr/hips-image-services/hips2fits), [astroquery docs](https://astroquery.readthedocs.io/en/stable/hips2fits/hips2fits.html); the exact Planck/IRIS HiPS ids were not verified (see Gaps). Liu et al. used the Planck thermal-dust radiance map (HFI CompMap ThermalDustModel) resampled with `reproject`. (2) Block-average the patch medians used by DBE per channel to the template grid (Planck 5' beam ~16x11 beams across an Origin frame). (3) Robust-fit (IRLS, existing `dbe_fit_surface` machinery or plain weighted lstsq) `V_c(x,y) = P_sky,2(x,y) + alpha_c * P_dust,3(x,y)`, one `alpha_c` per channel. (4) Pass DBE the patch values with the dust term removed (`V - alpha_c * D_lowpass`), fit the flexible surface to that, and subtract only that fitted surface: the dust-explained part `alpha_c * D` is never part of the subtracted model, so it stays in the image. (5) Gate: apply only if the dust template has non-planar structure above noise (variance left after removing a plane/quadratic), `alpha_c > 0` with t > 3, and the design-matrix condition number is acceptable; otherwise do nothing (a dust gradient that is planar across 1 deg is degenerate with a light-pollution gradient and cannot be separated).
- Source: [Liu et al. 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.07922) (equations 1-3, Sect. 4.2); [Mihos et al. 2017](https://arxiv.org/abs/1611.04435) (single scale factor); [Roman et al. 2020](https://arxiv.org/abs/1907.00978) (optical-FIR correspondence).
- Expected gain: preserves large-scale cirrus/IFN and dust-lane structure that DBE and the sky floor currently take; targets the Pleiades / Flaming Star / Horsehead / Lagoon group. Evidence is qualitative (visual match to Herschel/Planck in Dragonfly fields); the sign of the effect on Origin metrics is plausible but unproven. Measure with the project's `tools/eval_background_nights.py` ("kept" slope and two-night mismatch) plus Idea 8's injection test.
- Plug-in: `postprocess._postprocess_early` / `background._patch_background_extraction` (an optional `template` argument to the fit), a new `dust_template.py` fetcher; the fetch is a network call, so it must join `uncertainty._QUIET_OFF`, the README "Network use" table, the `--offline` guard and `_early_cache_usable`, and the template (or its hash) must be stored with the run for `--from-stack`.
- Cost/risk: medium. Risks: emission nebulae dominated by Hα/[OIII] rather than dust-scattered light correlate with dust only loosely (inference; Liu's premise is scattering by the same dust); reflection nebulae (Pleiades, Flaming Star) fit the premise best; Origin FOV is ~3x smaller than Dragonfly's so the template carries few independent resolution elements; the IR/optical ratio varies between fields (Mihos' 3.5e-3 is one field).
- Confidence: medium-low for gain, medium for feasibility.

**Idea 3 (NEW, low priority): Uncertainty-gated subtraction using a GP / local-covariance posterior**
- What/why: a Gaussian-process (kriging) fit to the DBE patch values with an anisotropic Matern kernel and per-patch noise gives a posterior variance map; where the posterior is wide (few clean patches, e.g. under a nebula) shrink the subtraction toward a low-order polynomial. Saydjari & Finkbeiner's local pixel-wise infilling is the same idea with a covariance learned from the image itself.
- Honest assessment: the project's DBE settings sweeps (patch size, bandwidth, outlier sigma, entropy weights) moved results by under ~5%, so a better interpolator will not fix the identifiability problem; only the variance-gated shrinkage and a better infill of masked star holes (`background._harmonic_fill` smooth fill versus covariance-conditioned fill) are plausible gains.
- Source: [Saydjari & Finkbeiner 2022](https://par.nsf.gov/servlets/purl/10376786); [GraXpert README](https://github.com/Steffenhir/GraXpert) (kriging exists in a shipping tool).
- Plug-in: `background._fit_background_surface`, `_harmonic_fill`; Rust not needed at ~1-3k patches. Confidence: low.

**Idea 4 (NEW, retry of a failed approach with a specific fix): synthetic fractal nebula in the learned-background training set**
- The project documents why the learned background failed (training "clean" images were DBE outputs that had already lost every nebula's smooth component) and that no public data give known-real faint large-scale nebula at Origin's field size. Alternative: build the "clean + real faint nebula" composites synthetically — add a random field with power spectrum `P(k) ~ k^-2.9` (the measured cirrus slope; Planck dust emission -2.7) scaled to 0.3-3 sky-sigma at 100-1000 px scales to galaxy/cluster sessions, with backgrounds taken from the DBE surfaces of galaxy sessions; train the network to remove the surface but keep the fractal field.
- Source for the slope: [Miville-Deschenes et al. 2016](https://arxiv.org/abs/1605.08360) (MegaCam g-band cirrus -2.9 +- 0.1; Planck -2.7, WISE -2.9).
- Cost: training run (the infrastructure `tools/gen_background_bank.py` exists); risk: sim-to-real gap, and the network may simply learn "keep anything with k^-2.9". Evaluate only with Idea 8. Confidence: low.

**Considered and not recommended (to save the report-writer time)**
- Sensor-fixed vs sky-fixed separation by field rotation: a static feature can be separated from the sky only if frame rotation moves it by more than its own size, i.e. features of size L need `r * dtheta > L`. With the project's measured <= 0.4 deg (0.007 rad) per session that is ~7 px at r = 1000 px. This is the same identifiability limit that sank `--flat-from-lights` and the ubercal flat (CLAUDE.md "Tried and reverted"); HSC and Burrell Schmidt overcome it with large dithers (up to half a degree at the Burrell Schmidt; "large dithers" for the HSC sky frame) ([Mihos et al. 2017](https://arxiv.org/abs/1611.04435); [Aihara et al. 2019](https://arxiv.org/abs/1905.12221)), which Origin sessions are not known to have. (Inference from the cited designs.)
- A "static sky-frame with one fitted scale per frame" (HSC) is the shipped per-instrument vignetting map idea ([src/vignette_calib.py](C:/source/originstack-research/src/vignette_calib.py)); the project measured 0-5% gain.
- PS1/DSS cut-outs as a gradient-free MGC-style reference: MARS exists precisely because existing public surveys were not good enough for this and the project notes that deep surveys subtract their own large-scale background ([dev-notes/phase4-quality.md](C:/source/originstack-research/dev-notes/phase4-quality.md)); no published evidence was found that DSS2/PS1 preserve scale >0.2 deg nebulosity (see Gaps).
- GraXpert-style AI background: undocumented training data; no quantitative benchmark exists (already noted in the earlier ML note); the project's own U-Net result is the relevant evidence.

### Gaps
- No published quantitative error metric for dust-template sky modelling (Liu et al. is visual); the gain on a 1.3 x 0.9 deg OSC field is unmeasured.
- The exact `hips2fits` HiPS identifiers for Planck/IRIS/WISE dust maps were not confirmed (the fetch of the hips2fits page failed from the research sandbox; only a search-result summary and the astroquery docs were available).
- No evidence found on whether DSS2/PanSTARRS preserve >0.2 deg faint nebulosity well enough to serve as an MGC-like reference.
- GraXpert's AI model training set and architecture are undocumented in the sources reached.
- No Origin-specific wing profile (power-law slope, radius at which wings reach sky noise) is documented; it must be measured from stacks before Idea 1's parameters are fixed.

## Q2. Scattered light, stray light, ghosts, reflections and halos around bright stars (extended-PSF models, halo subtraction, flat/illumination errors, reflections in fast Schmidt-type optics)

### Takeaway
Every LSB survey subtracts the summed scattered light of *all* bright stars (including those just outside the field) using an empirically measured extended PSF scaled per star, and in Schmidt-type systems additionally models the internal reflections as offset, scaled, out-of-focus pupil images whose offset is linear in detector position. OriginStack models only symmetric halos of saturated stars inside `--remove-stars` and lists reflection rings as open.

### Cited Findings
- Extended PSFs: SDSS PSFs to 8' radius were built by stacking ~1000 stars by brightness band (bright stars for the outer part, faint non-saturated stars for the core); profiles fall as a power law with exponent ~-2.49 over a 20-magnitude dynamic range; diffraction spikes with random orientation average out in stacked PSFs and were masked; internal reflections sit at ~25" in g and r — [Infante-Sainz, Trujillo & Roman 2020](https://arxiv.org/abs/1911.01430)
- Their subtraction recipe: stars from Gaia DR2 (G < 13), fitted brightest first because very bright stars affect the fits of fainter neighbours; star centre refined with a Moffat fit; flux factor F = 3-sigma-clipped median of the star/PSF radial-profile ratio over the radial range between 0.1x the saturation level and 3x the background; two iterations, the second with masks regenerated after the brightest stars are removed; Gaia is incomplete for G < 7 and has no stars brighter than ~1.7 mag, so USNO-B1 was used for the PSF stars — [Infante-Sainz et al. 2020](https://arxiv.org/abs/1911.01430)
- Roman et al. 2020 modelled the scattered light of every star to r = 15 (~400 per deg^2) by scaling PSF radial profiles (not 2-D fits) to the star profile, averaging the flux ratio over radii with 2-sigma clipping, and placing PSFs on a blank field with SWarp; the resulting field averages ~29 mag/arcsec^2 (r) and is ~30 (g) to 28 (z); off-field stars whose scattered light enters the frame were masked manually; PSFs extend to 720" — [Roman et al. 2020](https://arxiv.org/abs/1907.00978)
- Trujillo & Fliri 2016: scattered-light field from all R < 17 stars within 7' of the target, ~29.2 mag/arcsec^2 over the target; PSF error below 0.07%, 0.11%, 0.28% at 1', 3', 5'; night flats made as the median of normalised science images of the night, using a dithering plus rotation pattern; sky determined per image from 10^5 random apertures on object-free pixels — [Trujillo & Fliri 2016](https://arxiv.org/abs/1510.04696)
- Slater, Harding & Mihos 2009 (Burrell Schmidt, f/3.5): reflections between the CCD (~10% reflective) and the filter / dewar window are out-of-focus images of the telescope pupil (with the obstruction's shadow), not concentric with the star; each reflection is modelled by (i) an offset from the star that is a linear function of row and column on the detector, (ii) a constant circular size, (iii) a brightness as a fraction of the star's flux; parameters were measured from sixteen 450 s exposures of Arcturus placed evenly over the field; reflections reach radii up to 17'; the model is generative: Tycho-2/SDSS stars within 72' are turned into PSF + reflection images and subtracted; the "Schmidt ghost" (light reflected off the CCD, up the tube, off the corrector, back to the CCD on the opposite side of the optical axis) was masked, not modelled — [Slater et al. 2009](https://arxiv.org/abs/0909.3320)
- Mihos et al. 2017 add that Schmidt ghosts shift with the star's position on the chip so "a combination of images taken with large dithers minimizes any small feature that it might otherwise imprint", reflections hold <0.5% of a star's light, and corrector rings at ~1' and 2' remain after the model; their night-sky "super flat" (30-70 sky images) is accurate to better than 0.5% — [Mihos et al. 2017](https://arxiv.org/abs/1611.04435)
- Dragonfly: flat-fielding errors must be <0.1% to reach the LSB regime; the design averages ghosts out by offsetting individual cameras; the brightest ghost holds ~0.025% of the planet's light; sky-limited systematics, not photon noise, set the limit — [Abraham & van Dokkum 2014](https://arxiv.org/abs/1401.5473)
- Roman 2020 and Liu 2023 both identify the wide-angle PSF as time-variable (dust on optics, aerosols); Liu et al. measure threshold-radii profiles of 8-10.5 mag stars frame by frame, normalise by each star's median over frames, cluster the per-frame profiles and drop frames with anomalous wings (slope and dispersion are absolute alternatives) — [Liu et al. 2023, Sect. 5](https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.07922)
- HSC reports a physical-source fix for an internal-reflection arc (LED light off the lens barrel in y-band) done by hardware screens, and says masks around bright stars were not revised in PDR2 — [Aihara et al. 2019](https://arxiv.org/abs/1905.12221)
- OriginStack today: `shrink_stars` (star reduction) cuts a radial profile at the first radius where it falls below 3x the ring-median error; `fill_saturated` subtracts a per-channel symmetric halo of saturated stars; spikes and reflection rings are explicitly left ([dev-notes/phase4-quality.md](C:/source/originstack-research/dev-notes/phase4-quality.md), [dev-notes/star-n2n-runbook.md](C:/source/originstack-research/dev-notes/star-n2n-runbook.md)).

### Inferences
**Idea 1 (continued above): halo-aware masking and a catalogue-driven scattered-light field.** The summed-PSF field of Gaia/Tycho stars (inside and up to ~1-2 deg outside the frame) is the published way to model *low-order* gradients produced by bright stars outside the frame (e.g. glare from Alnitak/Rigel-class stars), which a patch-based DBE can only mistake for a sky gradient. FFT-convolve a delta-image of star fluxes with a 2-D wing kernel; kernel from the stack's own bright stars as in Infante-Sainz; subtract in the *sampling* image first (safe), in the output only as an option.

**Idea 5 (NEW): Calibrated reflection-ring / ghost model for the Origin optics, shipped in the camera profile (Slater 2009)**
- What/why: the roadmap lists a spike/ring model as open. Per the project brief Origin is RASA-style (Schmidt-type, ~f/2.2); assuming a sensor cover glass and a central obstruction (inference, not verified for Origin), the same physics applies: each reflection is a scaled out-of-focus pupil disc (with a central obstruction shadow) whose offset from the star is linear in position on the sensor. With the project's measured <= 0.4 deg field rotation per session, and if the dither amplitude is small compared with the reflection size (check `--dither-report`), these features would *not* average out (unlike Burrell Schmidt data with half-degree dithers); they would stack coherently into rings around every bright star.
- Algorithm: (i) calibration (offline, from many sessions): for stars with G < ~7 in different sensor regions, subtract a radially symmetric profile, then fit for each reflection j: centre offset `d_j(x,y) = a_j + b_j*x + c_j*y` (2 components), disc radius `R_j` and inner-shadow radius, and flux fraction `f_j` (nonlinear least squares on stacked residual cut-outs binned by sensor region); store in `src/data/camera_profiles/<model>.json`. (ii) application: for each star above a flux threshold, render the reflection disc(s) at `star + d_j(star)` with amplitude `f_j * F`, smooth by the PSF, subtract in the linear stack before DBE (or only from the sampling image as in Idea 1); (iii) test with a held-out session. A reflection "across the optical axis" (Schmidt ghost) needs a separate irregular template, measured by stacking the cut-outs at `-(star - centre) * k`.
- Source: [Slater et al. 2009](https://arxiv.org/abs/0909.3320); [Mihos et al. 2017](https://arxiv.org/abs/1611.04435).
- Expected gain: removal of the coherent ring/halo artefacts around the brightest stars (Orion, Pleiades, Alnitak region). Gain unquantified; the Burrell Schmidt paper itself notes the improvement is "perhaps not as dramatic" there because its optics are exceptionally low-reflectivity (<0.8%), implying larger gain for ordinary-coated optics.
- Plug-in: new module (e.g. `reflections.py`) driven by `camera_profile.py`; apply before the DBE sampling; interacts with `shrink_stars` and the star-removal NN (ring removal first would also simplify `fill_saturated`). Rust not needed.
- Cost/risk: large (data mining of calibration stars, per-unit variation of the cover-glass tilt is unknown — the profile may need per-unit constants, which CLAUDE.md's camera-profile notes say were of limited value for flats). Confidence: medium-low.

**Idea 6 (NEW): Per-frame wide-angle-PSF ("halo index") frame screening (Liu et al. 2023 Sect. 5)**
- What/why: dew, haze, thin cloud or a dirty corrector raise the PSF wings of a whole frame without necessarily changing FWHM; the current Phase 1 gates (FWHM, outliers, percentile gates) do not look at wings (`grep` for halo/wing in `quality.py`/`frame_processor.py` finds nothing).
- Algorithm: in Phase 1, for 10-30 bright unsaturated-to-mildly-saturated stars per frame, compute the radius at which the star's background-subtracted profile falls to fractions p = 0.1, 0.01, 0.001 of its peak (the "threshold radii"); normalise by the per-star median across frames; the frame's halo index is the median over stars of the log ratio at p = 0.01 and 0.001; robust-z across frames; drop (or down-weight in `proper_coadd`) frames above ~3-4 sigma.
- Source: [Liu et al. 2023, Sect. 5](https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.07922).
- Expected gain: modest and session-dependent; cheap insurance for LSB depth. Plug-in: `quality.py` metrics, `frame_processor.quality_gate`; per-star profile code can reuse `measure_fwhm` cut-outs. Cost small-medium. Risk: star-field-dependent normalisation noise; screen only extreme outliers. Confidence: medium-low.

### Gaps
- No Origin/RASA-specific measurement of reflection geometry exists in the sources; Slater et al. describe a Schmidt with a Newtonian secondary and a dewar window, so the number and offsets of the reflections for Origin's sensor stack are unknown until measured.
- The Slater 2009 paper's step-by-step "cookbook" section was not read in detail (only the model description and application); the reflection brightness fitting procedure is summarised from the visible text.
- No source gives the accuracy of subtracting an extended-PSF model at the ~1.5"/px, ~6" aperture OSC regime; all cited work is on professional telescopes with narrower PSFs relative to the pixel.

## Q3. Frame-to-frame sky variation (airglow, moon, twilight, clouds): per-frame background matching before rejection and its interaction with outlier rejection

### Takeaway
Per-frame additive matching to a reference (what `local_normalize.py` does) is standard; the literature adds two things OriginStack lacks: a scale (gain) term estimated by regression against a reference on a coarse grid, and a *spatially-correlated* consistency test, because per-pixel sigma-clipping cannot see background systematics that are small per pixel but coherent over many pixels.

### Cited Findings
- PixInsight's LocalNormalization stores, per channel, a scaling and a zero-offset coefficient sampled on a grid at a large dyadic scale (typically 64, 128 or 256 px) and interpolated to the frame — (secondary, PCL header summary from search) [PCL LocalNormalizationData.h](https://gitea.nouspiro.space/nou/tenmon/src/commit/0368c1f1dc14444ed0835509dff39289b2a1618d/3rdparty/include/pcl/LocalNormalizationData.h). Community guidance reports that using local normalisation as the *rejection* normalisation is "not a recommended method" because it can cause non-uniform rejection — (secondary, forum summary) [Adam Block forum](https://forum.adamblockstudios.com/forums/discussion/comment/369).
- Liu et al. 2023, Sect. 6: "Discrepancies are not obvious at the level of individual pixels, but they become clear at the level of ensembles of nearby pixels. In other words, the individual pixels in erroneous areas are not significantly deviant -- only the mean in an area is." They measure the background (mode) and rms in a box (20') around ~1000 sky-fixed "landmarks" in every frame, train a Gaussian process on the ensemble to predict the mean sky+signal and its local sigma, and form a normalised deviation map `delta_norm = (frame/model - 1)/sigma_GPR` used to mask bad areas or weight frames before sigma-clipped stacking; a radially symmetric deviation in one frame was attributed to a flat-fielding error — [Liu et al. 2023, Sect. 6](https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.07922)
- Trujillo & Fliri determine and subtract the sky for each science image separately before coaddition, from random apertures on object-free pixels — [Trujillo & Fliri 2016](https://arxiv.org/abs/1510.04696)
- Mihos et al. restricted imaging to moonless photometric conditions with frame-to-frame zero-point scatter ~0.01 mag, and flat-fielded each image with the season's night-sky flat — [Mihos et al. 2017](https://arxiv.org/abs/1611.04435)
- HSC: global per-exposure sky from 1024 px superpixels plus a single scaled static sky frame per exposure — [Aihara et al. 2019](https://arxiv.org/abs/1905.12221)
- OriginStack: additive-only (the older multiplicative/sigma-divided version was removed because it amplified background noise); cell estimate is a 30th percentile of raw values, not of a difference to a reference ([src/local_normalize.py](C:/source/originstack-research/src/local_normalize.py)).

### Inferences
**Idea 7 (NEW): Block-level consistency rejection ("sigma clipping on larger scales"), a cheap non-GP version of Liu et al. Sect. 6**
- What/why: `local_normalize` subtracts *any* smooth per-frame deviation up to arbitrary size, so a frame with cloud structure, a satellite-trail glow, a light flash or a dew patch is silently "repaired" rather than down-weighted, and per-pixel clipping cannot see the remaining coherent offsets. This is exactly the failure Liu et al. describe.
- Algorithm: after additive matching, on star-masked pixels, per frame and channel compute block means `m_i(b)` at two scales (e.g. 32 and 128 px, using the existing downsampling `gaussian_filter_ds`/block means); per block the across-frame median `M(b)` and robust spread `s(b)` (1.4826*MAD across frames — the noise of a block mean includes sky noise and residual structure, so it is measured, not assumed); `z_i(b) = (m_i(b) - M(b))/s(b)`; where `|z| > 4` set a feathered per-frame weight map to zero for that block before the combine (the combine already supports weights: proper coadd / weighted sigma combine). Log frames with a large flagged area.
- Source: [Liu et al. 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.07922) (GP version); this simplification is an inference.
- Expected gain: improved robustness against occasional bad frames; negligible on clean sessions. Unmeasured on Origin. Plug-in: `local_normalize.py` (second pass, same grids) feeding the combine as per-frame-per-block weights (verify that `stacking._combine_tiles` / `proper_coadd` can take spatially varying weights; proper coadd already uses per-frame weights); keep the combine per-pixel so RAM/disk results stay bit-identical. Cost small-medium; risk: an adaptive threshold that also flags real transients (comets, novae) — exempt via `--comet-mode`. Confidence: low-medium.

**Idea 8 (NEW, low priority): reference-difference estimate and optional global gain term in local normalisation**
- Estimate each cell's offset from the *median of (frame - reference frame)* with the reference being the median/mean aligned stack, instead of a 30th percentile of raw values; stars and nebulosity cancel to first order so no low-percentile bias is needed and the cell value no longer depends on structure moving across cell boundaries as the field rotates. Cost trivial; gain small (inference: estimation noise of a 30th-percentile cell with ~10^4 px is ~0.02 sigma, so noise is not the issue — bias from structure is).
- A gain (scale) term fitted by robust regression of frame vs reference on star-masked pixels, smoothed at >= 128 px, would handle thin cloud that scales nebulosity multiplicatively; the project removed its earlier multiplicative version for noise amplification, and the PCL design (regression-estimated scale, coarse grid) is different from dividing by local sigma. Only attempt with a global (1-parameter) gain per frame; `transparency.py` already measures per-frame transparency.
- Source: [PCL header summary](https://gitea.nouspiro.space/nou/tenmon/src/commit/0368c1f1dc14444ed0835509dff39289b2a1618d/3rdparty/include/pcl/LocalNormalizationData.h) (secondary). Confidence: low.

**Improvement to an existing feature (NEW, small): `frame_processor._pre_gradient_removal`.** It fits one degree-2 surface to single unmasked luminance pixels and subtracts it from all channels; replace with the HSC recipe (block means of 3-sigma-clipped unmasked pixels, inverse-variance weights, iterative clipping of the fit) per channel — [Bosch et al. 2018](https://arxiv.org/abs/1705.06766). Colour-dependent gradients then no longer survive to DBE. The option is off by default and cannot be set by `--auto` (CLAUDE.md), so effect size is limited. Confidence: low.

### Gaps
- No source quantifies how often per-pixel clipping misses coherent frame defects in short-exposure OSC data; the benefit for Origin's 10-20 s subs is unmeasured.
- The PixInsight LocalNormalization behaviour was reached only through a header summary and forum text, not its reference documentation.

## Q4. Revealing faint extended structure: masked multiscale smoothing, large-scale structure enhancement that does not amplify gradients, star-masked binning, significance-based thresholding

### Takeaway
LSB papers reveal faint structure with the same recipe: mask sources generously (PSF-wing-aware), bin or adaptively smooth the *masked* image so each element reaches a target S/N, and judge features by significance against a measured box noise rather than by a fixed stretch. NoiseChisel-style detect-then-grow masks and MTObjects on a binned image are the published ways to obtain masks that follow faint real extent instead of fixed discs.

### Cited Findings
- NoiseChisel grows detections into the noise with a modified watershed (`--detgrowquant`), which follows signal "with any shape" where simple dilation gives boxy/diamond masks; `--cleangrowndet` discards grown regions whose S/N falls below the pseudo-detection S/N; grown too deep, it follows correlated noise — [Akhlaghi 2019](https://arxiv.org/abs/1909.11230)
- The tile test: mean-median quantile difference identifies tiles with no significant signal; a wide convolution kernel increases skewness and so improves the test, while a sharper kernel is used for the final thresholds — [Akhlaghi 2019](https://arxiv.org/abs/1909.11230); mode-based version with minimum mode quantile 0.49 in [Akhlaghi & Ichikawa 2015](https://arxiv.org/abs/1505.01664)
- Euclid ERO Dorado: MTObjects (max-tree) on the image re-binned 6x produces masks for galaxies and faint features before a 1000 px local background — [Urbano et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.17672)
- Roman et al.: six layers of SExtractor masking with different detection areas/thresholds and enlargement (0-30 px), a final mask at 5-sigma above neighbours in the 6"-rebinned image to remove masking residuals ("hot" pixels), and a large-kernel (30") smoothed sum image to mask diffuse light before calibrating the reference sky level; MMM (mean-median-mode) sky — [Roman et al. 2020](https://arxiv.org/abs/1907.00978)
- Voronoi binning to a target S/N (with the explicit caveat not to bin blindly when pixels contain noise but virtually no signal) and ASMOOTH (adaptive kernel with a per-pixel significance; "all pixel values share the same S/N above the background") are the standard adaptive smoothers; both are usable on ordinary images — (secondary, search-result summaries; full texts not read) [Cappellari & Copin 2003](https://arxiv.org/abs/astro-ph/0302262); [Ebeling, White & Rangarajan 2006](https://arxiv.org/abs/astro-ph/0601306)
- Surface-brightness limit definition: `mu_lim(n sigma; Omega x Omega) = -2.5 log10( n * sigma_pix * Omega / pix^2 ) + ZP` assuming uncorrelated noise (sigma on a box scales as sigma_pix * Omega/pix); Roman et al. quote 3-sigma 10"x10" limits of 28.0, 29.1, 28.6, 28.2, 26.6 (u, g, r, i, z) and note the value depends strongly on the box size; the Euclid ERO nearby-galaxy paper notes the conversion assumes uncorrelated noise (search-result summary) — [Roman et al. 2020, Appendix A](https://arxiv.org/abs/1907.00978); [Euclid ERO deep anatomy](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.13499) (secondary)
- OriginStack today: wavelet denoiser (BayesShrink + structure protection), nn denoiser/deconvolver, multiscale local contrast (mask in noise units), star reduction; the "diffuse" rule in `_build_emission_mask` protects regions >1 smoothed-border-sigma above the border value, which fails where the border itself is nebula (Horsehead) ([dev-notes/phase4-quality.md](C:/source/originstack-research/dev-notes/phase4-quality.md)).

### Inferences
**Idea 9 (NEW): NoiseChisel-style "detect-and-grow" structure mask with a skewness-based tile gate to replace the fixed-disc dilation and border-referenced diffuse rule**
- What/why: `_build_emission_mask` protects at most 3 peaks with a fixed disc and uses a border-referenced diffuse threshold; neither follows the real extent of faint wings of galaxies, planetary nebulae, small nebulae or cluster halos. Detect-and-grow follows the signal into the noise in arbitrary shape and is independent of the border.
- Algorithm: (i) bin 2-4x (or smooth sigma 2-4 px), star-masked, normalised convolution so masked pixels do not contribute (`G*(M*I)/(G*M)`); (ii) per tile (e.g. 64-128 px) compute sky and sigma from the lower-tail-reflected distribution; keep only tiles whose mean-median quantile difference is small (|q_mean - 0.5| < ~0.01, to tune) and reject outlier tiles (the Gnuastro sorted-distance rule) before interpolating sky and sigma; (iii) seeds = smoothed S/N > ~3 over >= N px (S/N of connected regions compared with a threshold calibrated on the flipped-negative image, as NoiseChisel does with pseudo-detections); (iv) grow seeds to S/N > ~0.5-1 by priority-queue flood fill (hysteresis; `scipy.ndimage.label` on the low-threshold map keeping components that contain a seed is an adequate first version); (v) mask = grown regions OR magnitude-dependent star masks (Idea 1); feather.
- Source: [Akhlaghi & Ichikawa 2015](https://arxiv.org/abs/1505.01664); [Akhlaghi 2019](https://arxiv.org/abs/1909.11230); [Kelvin et al. 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2301.05793) (masks must cover wings); [Urbano et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.17672).
- Expected gain: better protection of compact-to-medium extended targets (outskirts of galaxies, small nebulae) from DBE; does nothing for frame-filling nebulosity (a smooth frame-filling feature has no within-tile skew, which is the identifiability limit; Idea 2 is the answer there). Plug-in: `background._build_emission_mask` / `_dbe_prepare_emission_mask`, and `remove_sky_residual`'s independent detector; reuse `gaussian_filter_ds` and the native median kernels. Cost small-medium. Confidence: medium-low.

**Idea 10 (NEW): Optional "faint-structure significance view" (companion product and diagnostic)**
- Algorithm: star + wing mask (Idea 1) -> fill masked pixels with the local median (or the covariance-conditioned fill of Saydjari & Finkbeiner) -> compute adaptive smoothing scale per pixel as the smallest sigma in a geometric ladder (2, 4, 8, 16, 32 px) such that normalised-convolution S/N >= 3 (ASMOOTH-like; noise per scale from the project's correlated-noise model `noise_model.py`) -> map the smoothed *luminance* through an arcsinh stretch and tint with the full-resolution chroma; output as a separate JPEG/FITS, never replace the main image ("stretch one image; never recombine separately stretched layers" in CLAUDE.md applies to the main chain). Also write the S/N map and the box-noise table.
- Source: [Ebeling et al. 2006](https://arxiv.org/abs/astro-ph/0601306), [Cappellari & Copin 2003](https://arxiv.org/abs/astro-ph/0302262) (binning caveat), [Roman et al. 2020](https://arxiv.org/abs/1907.00978) (6" rebinned masked images to show cirrus).
- Expected gain: helps users see whether faint cirrus/IFN or a gradient residual is present (diagnostic), not an improvement of the main image. Plug-in: `postprocess.py` extra output or `tools/`. Cost small-medium; Rust unnecessary (separable Gaussians via `gaussian_filter_ds`). Confidence: medium that it is useful as a diagnostic, low as an improvement.

### Gaps
- No quantitative comparison between adaptive smoothing and a wavelet/NN denoiser on LSB visibility was found; ASMOOTH/Voronoi were designed for X-ray and IFU data.
- The ASMOOTH paper's equations were only available as an abstract-level summary in this session.
- Specific default values for NoiseChisel's mean-median quantile threshold in the current release were not read (2015 paper: minimum mode quantile 0.49); values quoted above are starting points, not published defaults.

## Q5. Quantifying residual gradient / flatness error so a method's gain can be measured

### Takeaway
Published practice measures (a) the noise and bias of blank-sky apertures as a function of scale, (b) the 3-sigma surface-brightness limit at a stated box size with the correlated-noise caveat, and (c) the fidelity of injected faint extended features. OriginStack already scores two-night mismatch and a "kept" slope; it lacks ground-truth injection, a bias-vs-scale curve, and a correlated-noise-correct depth number.

### Cited Findings
- HSC places 100 random points per patch outside object footprints and measures them "just like real objects" ("sky objects"), and documents a residual large-scale sky error of order 29 mag/arcsec^2 — [Aihara et al. 2019](https://arxiv.org/abs/1905.12221)
- Trujillo & Fliri place 10^5 random apertures on object-free pixels to determine the sky and quote depth as 3-sigma in 10"x10" boxes — [Trujillo & Fliri 2016](https://arxiv.org/abs/1510.04696)
- Roman et al. 2020 give the depth formula above and state that rebinning images directly to measure noise is problematic with masking residuals and few remaining elements, so they extrapolate assuming Gaussian white noise — [Roman et al. 2020, Appendix A](https://arxiv.org/abs/1907.00978)
- Optical cirrus has a power-law power spectrum with slope -2.9 +- 0.1 (g-band MegaCam), so a faithful synthetic "real faint nebulosity" can be generated from a k^-2.9 Gaussian random field — [Miville-Deschenes et al. 2016](https://arxiv.org/abs/1605.08360)
- Simulation-based validation of sky estimators with known flat pedestal and injected sources is the standard test in Kelvin et al. (GalSim images mimicking HSC) — [Kelvin et al. 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2301.05793)
- OriginStack's own tools: `tools/eval_background_nights.py` (two-night gain-matched 64 px block medians in sky-sigma; "kept" = slope of extracted vs raw block medians), `tools/bench_phase4.py` metrics (flatness, blotch, faint CNR, ring) ([dev-notes/phase4-quality.md](C:/source/originstack-research/dev-notes/phase4-quality.md)).

### Inferences
**Idea 11 (NEW): Injection-recovery transfer function plus blank-aperture bias/variance curves (the evaluation harness for every idea above)**
- Algorithm: (a) Synthetic faint structure `S(x,y)`: Gaussian random field with `P(k) ~ k^-2.9`, band-limited to 16-1000 px, filament-like if desired (threshold/anisotropy), scaled to amplitudes of 0.25, 0.5, 1, 2 sky-sigma at 128 px; plus a few low-contrast exponential-profile discs (LSB galaxy outskirts) and a planar + quadratic gradient of known amplitude. (b) Injection point: `--from-stack` linear FITS (tests Phase 4 only; the fast path), and, for Phase 1-3 effects (local normalisation, pre-gradient, rejection), into each aligned frame through the inverse transform (`transparency.to_aligned_yx` is the documented inverse of `apply_transform`) scaled by that frame's transparency. (c) Metrics on the output minus the control run (same pipeline without injection, so noise and residual structure cancel): transmitted fraction `T(k) = <S_out S_in>/<S_in S_in>` in radial Fourier annuli; recovered amplitude at 16/64/256/1024 px by block means; gradient recovery error; (d) blank-sky apertures: N random apertures of radius r in {4, 8, 16, 32, 64, 128} px on star/object-masked sky of the *uninjected* output; report mean (bias vs scale) and std; compare std with `sigma_pix * sqrt(correlation-corrected area)` from the lag-1 correlation already modelled in `noise_model.py` ("excess variance ratio" = measured/expected, which rises above 1 at the scale where large-scale residuals dominate); (e) depth: `-2.5 log10(3 * sigma_box / (pix^2 * ...)) + ZP` with `sigma_box` *measured* in boxes of the stated angular size, not extrapolated from `sigma_pix`.
- Source: [Miville-Deschenes et al. 2016](https://arxiv.org/abs/1605.08360) (spectrum), [Roman et al. 2020 Appendix A](https://arxiv.org/abs/1907.00978) (depth), [Aihara et al. 2019](https://arxiv.org/abs/1905.12221) (sky objects), [Kelvin et al. 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2301.05793) (injection/simulation practice).
- Expected gain: not an image-quality change by itself, but it provides the missing ground truth. It would have flagged the learned background's nebula removal without needing two-night data and quantifies exactly the "eats real faint nebulosity" risk of every other idea. It needs no network and no training. Plug-in: a new `tools/bench_lsb_injection.py` built on `tools/bench_phase4.py` / `pipeline.postprocess_from_stack`; metric helpers can reuse `background.gaussian_filter_ds` and `noise_model`. Delete `<stem>_phase4cache.pkl` between runs (CLAUDE.md). Cost small-medium; Rust unnecessary. Confidence: high that it is useful; the numbers it produces are the point.

### Gaps
- No standard published metric combines gradient residual, nebula transmission and noise into one score; any composite is a project decision.
- The exact random-aperture procedure in Euclid and DES SB-limit papers was not read in full text.

## Which ideas rank highest by benefit / effort?

### Takeaway
Rank 1: brightness-scaled, wing-aware star masks for background sampling (small effort, strong survey evidence, targets a verified gap in the code). Rank 2: the injection-recovery + blank-aperture harness (small-medium effort, high leverage, de-risks everything else). Rank 3: dust-template-regularised background (medium effort, the only identified route for the frame-filling-nebula failure group, but unproven on Origin and gated).

### Cited Findings
- Ranking inputs: surveys attribute dark halos / over-subtraction to unmasked wings and fix it by magnitude-dependent masking or model subtraction ([Aihara et al. 2019](https://arxiv.org/abs/1905.12221); [Kelvin et al. 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2301.05793); [Akhlaghi 2019](https://arxiv.org/abs/1909.11230)); the project's DBE star mask is a fixed 4 px blob ([src/quality.py](C:/source/originstack-research/src/quality.py)); dust priors preserve cirrus in Dragonfly data ([Liu et al. 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.07922)); the project's DBE sweeps are within ~5% and its learned model failed for lack of known-real faint nebula ([dev-notes/phase4-quality.md](C:/source/originstack-research/dev-notes/phase4-quality.md)).

### Inferences
1. **Brightness-scaled, wing-aware star masks (Idea 1, step 1; step 2 optional).** Effort: a few days (per-star radius from a fitted wing law, union into the emission mask, a test on the `bench_phase4` ring/flatness metrics). Benefit: medium, concentrated on bright-star fields; evidence strong in surveys, unmeasured on Origin. Risk: over-masking (cap the masked fraction; existing dense-field fallback). Confidence: medium.
2. **Injection-recovery transfer function + blank-aperture curves (Idea 11).** Effort: small-medium, no new dependencies. Benefit: high leverage because it gives ground truth for nebula loss and gradient removal, would have caught the U-Net failure directly, and is the acceptance test for ideas 1, 2, 5, 9. Confidence: high (for usefulness).
3. **Dust-template-regularised sky model (Idea 2).** Effort: medium (fetch, WCS resample, template fit with gating, network-policy plumbing, early-cache key, `--from-stack` storage). Benefit: potentially the largest on the ~30% of targets where DBE is at its limit (frame-filling cirrus/reflection nebulosity); only if the dust prior is informative at Origin's field size. Confidence: medium-low; do it after item 2 so its effect can be measured.

Next tier: halo-index frame screening (Idea 6, small), detect-and-grow structure mask (Idea 9, small-medium), block-level consistency rejection (Idea 7, small-medium), reflection/ghost model in the camera profile (Idea 5, large but the only fix for coherent rings), faint-structure companion view (Idea 10, small-medium, diagnostic).

### Gaps
- Benefit estimates are judgement calls from survey literature; none of the ideas has been measured on Origin data. The injection harness (rank 2) is the way to turn each into a measured number.
- Effort estimates are for a developer already familiar with the codebase and exclude test-writing and the CHANGELOG/translation obligations noted in CLAUDE.md.
