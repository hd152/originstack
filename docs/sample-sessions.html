Sample sessions

Twelve real sessions from a Celestron Origin, one of each kind of target: galaxies, nebulae, a comet, clusters and a narrowband session. Each is 42 to 45 of the night’s best frames with the flats, darks and bias that went with them, free to download, stack and share.

The twelve sessions

Each download is one zip of about half a gigabyte (6.0 GB for all twelve). Each is stored on Zenodo, which gives it a permanent address (a DOI) and keeps it available, separately from this website. The pictures are what OriginStack 2.21 produced from that session with the default settings.

Using one

1. Download and unzip

Download a zip, unzip it, and you get one folder with the light frames (Light0001.fits and so on), the session’s own dark, flat and bias frames and info.json. Keep everything in that folder.

2. Choose the folder in OriginStack

In the desktop app, choose the folder as the light-frames folder and press Start. The app reads the target from the session and picks sensible settings. From a terminal it is python originstack.py -d FOLDER -o stack.fits. The first time you do this, the sample session page walks through every step.

3. Compare with the picture on this page

The result should look very like the one above, though newer versions may differ a little. Each card lists how many frames were accepted and the typical star width, so you can check your numbers against ours.

The comet and the narrowband session

The comet needs comet mode (the Comet card in the app, or --comet-mode) to stack on the comet as well as on the stars. The Lagoon Nebula session was shot through an OIII filter (the FITS headers call it “Nebula”), so its colours and star sizes differ from the broadband sessions.

Why these twelve

Different targets stress different parts of the stacker: a galaxy that fills the frame leaves little sky to fit the background to, a cluster leaves no nebulosity to guide it, and a comet moves. Each session was picked from a large archive for sharp, steady frames, with nothing rejected by the Origin and the target clearly visible in the Origin’s own stack. From each, the best frame of every stretch of the night was kept, so the sets cover the whole session instead of its best five minutes.

What was removed

The observer’s location (latitude, longitude, altitude, GPS and airmass), the capture app’s session id and the camera’s serial number were removed from the FITS headers and the json files. The pixel data is untouched. The capture date and time and the target’s coordinates stay, since they say when and where in the sky the telescope pointed.

Not a full night

42 to 45 frames is a short session, so a stack is noisier than one of a few hundred frames. It is enough to show every step working. The gallery shows what longer sessions give.
