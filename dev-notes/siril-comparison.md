## Sharpness/noise vs Siril, investigated 2026-09 (module-table row as it stood)

**Measure star width on the SAME stars in both stacks, each on its own grid** -- a per-stack FWHM over each stack's own star list moved the number by more than the effect claimed (a published 'tighter stars' claim was wrong and withdrawn). Stable result on shared stars: OriginStack 10-13% wider on Omega, +1.5% Sunflower, -4% Sculptor. **Do not compare single-frame FWHM with stack FWHM**: fits to single low-signal frames are biased narrow by noise (Siril's own stack reads 13% wider than its own registered frames), which produced a false 'registration softens the stack' conclusion. Ruled out: registration accuracy (aligned-crop star scatter: ~0.1 px per-frame translation error, 0.003 deg rotation, scale 4e-5), warp kernel (Lanczos-3 vs cubic spline: identical stack FWHM and noise), rejection method/sigma, patch/IVW weighting, local normalisation, debayer method (Menon is not better). Noise: single frame after calibrate+debayer, regression-matched on the same frame, OriginStack/Siril = R 0.89 G 1.09 B 1.10 (Malvar vs RCD), stack-level 1.01/1.15/1.30 (Omega), worse on longer sessions (532 frames: 1.49/1.24/1.59), **CAUSE FOUND (same month): the statistical Bayer hot-pixel step (`_fix_hot_bayer`) clipped star cores.** Found by switching Phase 1 steps off one at a time with a `sitecustomize.py` hook (spawned pool workers do not inherit a launcher's monkeypatches, so an experiment hook must load in every process): only `remove_hot_pixels_bayer` mattered (stars 5.12 -> 4.41 px vs Siril 4.56; noise 1.01/1.15/1.30 -> 0.99/1.09/1.19); the hot-pixel map, RGB step, white balance and CFA equalisation did nothing. A star ~4 px wide is ~2 px in a half-resolution colour plane, so its peak sits far above the 3x3 plane median and was replaced by it in every frame. Fix: keep a flagged pixel when an adjacent mosaic pixel (another plane, 1 px away) is itself > `Config.HOT_PIXEL_STAR_SUPPORT` (3.0) sigma over its own plane median -- a hot pixel leaves neighbours normal, a star lifts them; native `hot_pixel_bayer(star_support=)` (crate 0.31.0) and the numpy mirror match bit for bit (tests/test_hot_pixel_star_protection.py). After the fix, shared-star widths OS/Siril: Omega 4.40/4.56, Sunflower 3.56/3.60, Sculptor 2.78/2.95; noise vs Siril 0.99/1.10/1.19, 1.15/1.09/1.20, 1.22/1.05/1.33; 532/532 frames used; a ~15% faster Sculptor run. Pitfall while investigating: OriginStack's Phase 1 white balance multiplies R and B, so noise measured on its aligned frames is not comparable to a WB-free single frame without dividing out the gains -- checked and ruled out as an alternative explanation for the remainder below, since this project's regression-based noise-ratio metric (linear fit of Gaussian-smoothed pixel values) is provably invariant to a uniform per-channel gain (it cancels in both the noise numerator and the regression slope). Peak memory is flat at ~17 GB from 114 to 532 frames (workers set it), Siril's grows 6.6 -> 11.2 GB. **Follow-up (2026-09): the remaining gap (R/B up to 1.3x) is explained, not fixed.** Cause: Malvar debayering smears a single-pixel spike (cosmic ray/hot pixel) over more pixels in R/B than in G -- a synthetic single-spike test measured a >5-sigma contamination footprint of 9 px in R/B vs 5 px in G, and R<->B cross-channel leakage (~76%) exceeding G's cross-leakage into R/B (~50-63%), consistent with Bayer sampling density (G 50%, R/B 25% each). `pipeline.py`'s auto-skip of per-frame `lacosmic_reject` for >=20-frame sessions using a rejection stack method (all three benchmark sessions qualify) leaves nothing to catch those spikes before debayer smears them; stack-level sigma-clip runs afterward, on the already-smeared pixels, and doesn't fully compensate. Forcing `--cosmic-ray-rejection` on confirms it on real data -- noise R/G/B: Omega 0.99/1.10/1.19 -> 0.87/0.92/1.03, Sunflower 1.15/1.09/1.20 -> 1.01/0.93/1.05, Sculptor 1.22/1.05/1.33 -> 1.08/0.91/1.15 (11-16% down across the board, all three sessions). Real tradeoff, not a free win: shared-star width vs Siril moved 0.966->0.970 (Omega, 30s subs, ~unchanged), 0.995->1.063 (Sunflower, 20s subs, +6.8%), 0.941->1.052 (Sculptor, 10s subs, +11.8%) -- softening grows as sub length (and per-sub SNR) drops. Also slower: Omega 110.4s->153.9s (+43.5s), Sunflower 128.4s->188.5s (+60.1s), Sculptor 231.6s->405.4s (+173.8s) -- 39-75%, worst on the session Siril already wins on time. **Default left unchanged**: `pipeline.py`'s >=20-frame auto-skip still turns per-frame lacosmic off by default; `--cosmic-ray-rejection` is available today for the cleaner/softer/slower tradeoff. If picked back up, the next thing to try is making the threshold sub-length-aware (e.g. scaled by expected per-sub SNR) rather than a flat frame count, since the softening cost and the noise benefit both move with sub length in the same three sessions. **Correction (2026-09, later): the Malvar-smearing explanation above is not supported, and the lacosmic 'noise win' is smoothing, not cosmic-ray removal.** `lacosmic_reject`'s noise model is `sqrt(signal/gain + (RN/gain)^2)` with gain 1 e-/ADU and RN 6.5 ADU: on a real 20 s Sunflower sub it predicts ~139 ADU where the measured pixel noise is ~963, so `sigclip` 4.5 is really ~0.65 sigma and it median-replaces **~22% of all pixels** per channel (changes of ~0.85 sigma each) -- a 5x5 median smoothing of a fifth of the frame, which is exactly why it lowers noise *and* softens stars more on shorter (lower-signal) subs. The real spikes in that sub are ~200 px (found by `--spike-reject`, a correctly noise-scaled mosaic detector); removing only those before the debayer changed the 158-frame stack's noise by 0.00-0.01% and the shared-star FWHM ratio to 0.99998 -- sigma-clip already rejects them. **Fixed (later the same month):** `lacosmic_reject` now measures its noise model per frame (`stacking.lacosmic_noise_model`: sky sigma from lag-4 differences, Poisson growth above a 0.1th-percentile floor; native kernel takes per-channel `noise_a`/`noise_b`; the old fixed model is still `gain=1.0, readnoise=6.5`). Real 20 s Sunflower sub: 550 px replaced instead of 4.1M. Was the old smoothing worth keeping as an honest step? No: on the 158-frame stack it gave noise x0.83 at **+6.7%** shared-star width, while a plain Gaussian blur of the default stack reaches the same x0.83 at **+4%** (and Phase 4's wavelet denoiser beats a blur). With the fix, forcing lacosmic changes noise and star width by <0.3% but costs ~2.5 min on that session. So the 25 s 'keep it on' rule (`Config.LACOSMIC_LONG_SUB_EXPTIME_S`) is gone, and auto now turns on `--spike-reject` (not lacosmic) where it wants per-frame CR removal (< 20 frames, mean, drizzle); `--cosmic-ray-rejection` still means lacosmic. **The R/B gap, found (Sunflower, `tools/bench_vs_siril.py`, all vs the same Siril stack):** baseline 1.15/1.09/1.20 at shared-star width 0.995. (a) **Debayer -- the main cause.** On a pure-noise mosaic Malvar keeps 0.99/0.86/0.99 of the per-pixel sigma (its gradient-correction kernels put the interpolation's averaging back), RCD 0.89/0.80/0.89 -- the same ~10% as the single-frame OS/Siril gap above. Real stack with `--debayer-method rcd` (new, `debayer.debayer_rcd`): **1.09/0.93/1.05 at width 1.011**. (b) ~~`--auto`'s patch-quality weighting~~ **misread, corrected**: the 'equal weights' run (`--stack-method sigma_clip`, 1.11/1.02/1.15 at width 0.993) was *still* patch-weighted (--auto turns `patch_registration` on regardless); what differed is that an explicit `--stack-method` keeps rejection at sigma 3.0 / 3 iterations instead of --auto's 2.8 / 4. Turning patch weighting off for real (RCD, MAD sigma-clip) made Sunflower *noisier* (R 1.09 -> 1.15), so `--auto`'s weighting stays. **Open lead:** --auto's tighter rejection (2.8/4) costs ~4-6% noise for no measured sharpness gain on this session -- not changed, one session. **Resolved (2026-10):** with proper coadd on (the default since), 2.8/4 vs 3.0/3 changed shared-star width and matched noise by <= 0.1% on 6 of 7 sessions (Markarian's Chain: 0.7% wider, 0.9% noisier); with `--no-proper-coadd` (also the fallback when coadd cannot measure a PSF) 2.8/4 was 4-7% noisier at equal width on Sunflower. `--auto` now uses 3.0/3 from 15 frames up. Ruled out on this data: white balance (`--white-balance none` leaves R/B at 1.16/1.22; G moved because --auto chose differently). Bilinear debayer (forced via a sitecustomize hook) drops noise to 0.98/0.62/0.90 but widens stars 16.6% -- a resolution trade, not a fix. **RCD is the default since 2026-10** (`--debayer-method malvar` restores the old one). Final validation: Sunflower vs Siril **1.09/0.93/1.05 at width 1.011**; Crab vs the old default (Malvar + same --auto) noise **x0.93/0.86/0.85 at width x1.026**. Cost: Debayer step 1.9 s/frame under 16 workers vs Malvar's 0.46 (Crab 1m35 -> 1m43, Sunflower 1m48 -> 2m03). RCD's greens carry a 2x2 position bias too (~+-2.5 ADU vs Malvar's ~+-6), so `debayer_rcd` applies the same grid correction as `debayer_malvar` and the session-constant CFA equalisation now covers rcd (`cfa_frame_stats(method=)` measures on `_rcd_raw`); without it RCD output left a checkerboard (`test_grid_correction_applied_like_malvar`). `--cfa-drizzle` accepts rcd (it writes raw samples back exactly, outside the Malvar-filled 4-px border, like Malvar). Synthetic accuracy (MAE vs ground truth): colour detail 134 vs Malvar 193, dense starfield 13.2 vs 18.1, noiseless stars FWHM 2.5/3.5/4.5 3.8/4.0/5.2 vs 9.4/9.3/9.6 (`tests/test_debayer_rcd.py`).

### Closing the gap with Siril (2026-10)
Measured against Siril on Omega/Sunflower/Sculptor (`tools/bench_vs_siril.py --reuse-siril`), after this round OriginStack is faster to the stack on Omega (53.6 vs 65.8 s) and Sunflower (73.0 vs 95.9 s), 15 s behind on Sculptor (190 vs 175 s, was 240), sharper on all three (shared-star width 0.964 / 0.984 / 0.894) and, blurred per channel to Siril's star width (`bench_vs_siril.matched_noise`, interpolated to a width ratio of exactly 1), quieter or level in every channel (Omega 0.81/0.88/0.89, Sunflower 1.00/0.75/0.93 -- its R already as wide as Siril's -- Sculptor 0.97/0.92/0.89). Back-to-back times after the first pass: Omega 57.0 vs 55.2 s, Sunflower 79.7 vs 76.8 s, Sculptor 194.8 vs 176.8 s; **after the second pass (crate 0.50.0, every step bit-identical, so sharpness/noise unchanged): Omega 68.2 vs 85.8 s, Sunflower 83.6 vs 106.3 s, Sculptor 172.9 vs 194.8 s -- faster on all three** (Siril alone ranged 55-86 s on Omega over the day). This machine's timings move by up to ~40% between identical runs (both tools), so speed decisions were taken on step-level timings and repeated runs.
- **The RGB hot-pixel pass was flattening star peaks.** `remove_hot_pixels_rgb_with_lum` flags any luminance pixel far above its 3x3 median -- the peak of every undersampled star. On a real Sculptor frame 82% of the pixels it replaced were within 3 px of a star centre and 24 of the 30 brightest stars lost their peak (median to 77%). It is now skipped whenever the mosaic pass ran (`_bayer_hot_fixed`: map + `remove_hot_pixels_bayer` with its star-support rule); pre-debayered input and the GPU calibration path keep it (`tests/test_rgb_hot_pixel_skip.py`). This, not noise, was most of the 'R/B gap'.
- **Consensus reference = the middle of the session in time** (`run_registration_phase`, `--auto`'s `consensus_ref`): best quality within the middle 20% of the session by DATE-OBS. The old rule took the frame nearest the median *pyramid* shift -- translation-only shifts that are meaningless for frames rotated against the tentative reference; once the hot-pixel fix changed the quality scores, the tentative reference became an end-of-session frame, the 'consensus' stayed there, and the common crop fell 18% on full Sculptor (2234x1034 -> 2152x876). Now 2244x1028 (subset 2280x916 -> 2244x1028, +10%).
- **`validate_image_data`'s full-frame passes are one native call** (`validate_frame_stats`, crate 0.44.0: all-finite, max, zero count, saturated count against the same float32 threshold numpy used) -- identical verdicts and messages, 34 -> 11 ms per frame.
- **I/O on big sessions**: proper coadd's combine workers read each aligned frame into RAM once (its strided sky/noise samples touched nearly every page of a disk-backed frame once per channel: 34 s of a 531-frame session's 37 s); the frame store is unmapped and deleted in a background thread (~9 s off the critical path); `RESERVE_FRAC` 0.30 -> 0.20 keeps big sessions' aligned stack in RAM (see Streaming memory model). The two Phase 1 session probes (CFA, CA) run concurrently, the CFA values installed only after both finish; the calibration report uses every 3rd pixel (an odd stride visits all four Bayer positions).
- **Fewer full-frame passes in Phase 1** (crate 0.45.0, all bit-identical): `io_fits.read_fits_be16` + `calibrate_frame_from_be16` read a plain BITPIX=16 frame's data block directly and byte-swap, add BZERO, convert and calibrate in one pass (astropy's read + conversion was ~30 of 34 ms); `debayer_rcd_native_into` writes RCD's output straight into the frame's frame-store slot (no fresh 75 MB output, no copy; memmap write 82 -> 19 ms/frame under load, and `debayer_rcd_native` no longer copies its input); `patch_brenner_scores` reads the float32 luminance without a float64 copy (24 -> 2.4 ms isolated, 69 -> 11 under load); the luminance after the (skipped) RGB hot-pixel pass is no longer computed when white balance emits it.
- **Tried and reverted**: a native rustfft `rfft2_batch` (41 ms per frame on 16 threads vs 47 ms for scipy on 4 -- slower than proper coadd's six concurrent single-threaded scipy FFTs), the elliptical Moffat's closed-form spectrum in place of the PSF FFT (no gain once threaded; changed results by aliasing), `FILE_ATTRIBUTE_TEMPORARY` on frame-store files (no effect).
- **Phase 1 layout** (`frame_processor.phase1_layout`): one process per physical core, the Rust kernels in each using the remaining logical cores (8 x 2 here) instead of 16 single-threaded processes. Peak memory 16.8 -> 9.1 GB on Sculptor (Siril 11.2), Phase 1 at least as fast (full Sculptor: 102/98 s vs 117/105 s for 16 x 1). `-j N` keeps N single-threaded workers.
- **Pool before probes**: the Phase 1 pool starts (one no-op per worker) before the session CFA probe and CA measurement, which then travel with each task (`_parallel_frame_worker`'s 16th tuple element); target inference (SIMBAD) runs in a background thread during Phase 1 (`pipeline._prefetch_session_lookups`).
- **Proper coadd speed**: the shape fit uses the 15 brightest stars' 17x17 cores (28 -> 8 ms/frame; fluxes still from all stars' 29x29 stamps); sky and noise moved from the measuring pass (strided reads of a disk-backed aligned stack) into the combine pass; the combine runs in threads that each take every k-th frame and accumulate into their own float32 arrays (`proper_coadd_accum32`, crate 0.43.0), summed in a fixed order -- deterministic (`test_combine_is_deterministic`), 8e-5 ADU from the float64 path. **Tried and reverted**: measuring inside the alignment loop (Python fits contend for the GIL with 16 warp threads; a single sub's percentile saturation guard left 6 PSF stars), a vectorised `compute_patch_scores` (62 vs 22 ms: the full-frame float64 diff does not stay in cache), file writes instead of memmap slot assignment (66 vs 26 ms), Phase 1 arrays in RAM (no gain).

- **Second pass (all bit-identical; whole Sculptor stack compared after each step).** Phase 1 per worker-frame under 8 x 2: calibrate 256 -> 136 ms (`hot_pixel_bayer_inplace`: two streaming passes -- rolling-row 3x3 medians, a |diff| histogram, exact MAD selection from the median's bucket, candidates against a conservative threshold, replacements last; `calibrate_frame_be16(out=)` into a per-thread scratch), quality metrics 235 -> 136 ms (matched filter in 32-row bands, median+MAD grid in one selection per cell, AVX2 tap twins without FMA; column-pass DWT adding whole rows; no float32->64->32 detector copy; median/MAD from the percentile call's partitioned array except at exactly 0), debayer 554 -> ~320-380 ms (session green grid fused into RCD's output write, `debayer_rcd_native_into(grid=)`, no per-tile staging, 128x256 tiles), white balance + validate + memmap write 191 -> ~160 ms (`white_balance_grayworld_lum_into` writes the luminance into its frame-store slot and returns `validate_frame_stats`' tuple plus the `[::3, ::3]` sample `validate_image_data` reads). Phase 2: reference selection 13.8 -> 9.7 s (`downsample_half_f32`, numpy's f32 add order; the luminance is read as a view -- only its 2x downsample is used). Phase 3: no synchronous `flush()` of scratch memmaps (`frame_store._ScratchMemmap`: the aligned stack's flush was ~29 s of a 58 s alignment, writing 14.7 GB that was read back from cache and deleted), proper coadd sky/noise native (`frame_sky_noise`) and its PSF measurement on 2 threads (GIL-bound fit: 8 threads 9.8 ms/frame, 2 threads 5.4), a tiled, cache-banded coadd combine (prep and row FFTs in 8-row bands into scipy's own pocketfft binding with `out=`, row spectra stored column-tiled for contiguous column FFTs, tiled float32 accumulators; memory traffic, not compute, was the limit: 46.9 -> 25.0 ms/frame at 6 threads, 22 -> 15 s on Sculptor), and a `patch_weighted_sigma_combine_fast` (median in place, fused survivor pass, patch-grid corners tabulated per row: ~10%; this combine is compute-bound -- 7.4x on 16 threads, ~0.8 GB/s of 25). **Tried, not kept:** a sort-once (u32 keys) sigma-clip, 20-45% slower than two quickselects; windowed selection predicted from the neighbour pixel, 2x slower (the counting pass costs more than the quickselect); batching frames per coadd accumulator pass and AVX2 twins of the coadd prep/accumulate rows (memory-bound, no gain); a batched Zernike `lstsq` (identical on 8 real frames, ~3 ms, but LAPACK does not promise it); a batched coadd/AVX2 attempt aside, the residual check's warp is now native and bit-identical to scipy (`ndimage_affine_order1` / `ndimage_shift_order1`, ported from scipy 1.17's `NI_GeometricTransform`/`NI_ZoomShift`: plain `mode='constant'` makes the whole pixel cval once either coordinate leaves [0, len-1] -- interpolating against cval is `grid-constant` only; a tap exactly at len mirrors with weight 0; NaN sign follows the x86 rule that `t + coeff` keeps t's NaN, recomputed explicitly only when the fast sum is NaN, since LLVM may commute the add): 169 -> 40 ms single-thread, residual check 12.3 -> 6.1 s and registration 25 -> 17 s on Sculptor (crate 0.51.0). The residual check's scipy warp does release the GIL (44 ms/frame at 16 threads).
